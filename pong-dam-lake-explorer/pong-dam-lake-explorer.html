Pong Dam Lake

Internationally Important Ramsar Wetland

Kangra District, Himachal Pradesh, India

Explore Sanctuary View Map
Ecosystem

A Thriving Reservoir Ecosystem

Reservoir Wetland

Formed by the construction of the Pong Dam on the Beas River in 1975. Its hydrology fluctuates seasonally, creating expansive mudflats that form a rich, biologically diverse freshwater wetland ecosystem.

Ramsar Site Designation

Designated as a Ramsar Site of International Importance in 2002 for its immense value in wetland conservation, biodiversity protection, and sustainable management initiatives.

Fisheries & Livelihood

The reservoir boasts high freshwater fish diversity, supporting local fishing communities through sustainable practices and providing immense economic importance to the region.

Avian Paradise

Maharana Pratap Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary

Wintering grounds for over 100,000 waterfowl every year.

Bar-headed Goose

Least Concern

Arriving via the Central Asian Flyway, Pong Dam Lake hosts the largest wintering congregation of these high-altitude flyers in the world.

Northern Pintail

Least Concern

An elegant dabbling duck that migrates thousands of miles to spend the winter foraging in the lake's shallow waters.

Eurasian Wigeon

Least Concern

Flocks of these striking ducks are drawn to the submerged vegetation exposed as water levels recede in winter.

Ruddy Shelduck

Least Concern

Known locally as 'Surkhab', this unmistakable orange-brown bird is a common sight along the shoreline.

Other Notable Species: Common Teal, Great Cormorant, Grey Heron, and hundreds of other migratory birds navigating the Central Asian Flyway.

Activities

Water Sports

Beyond birding, the expansive lake is a hub for recreational tourism.

  • Boating & Sailing: The wide expanse of the reservoir is ideal for organized sailing events and recreational boating.
  • Kayaking: Navigate the peaceful shoreline and access remote birdwatching spots.
  • Eco-Friendly Practices: Strict safety guidelines and eco-friendly operations are enforced to protect the delicate wetland ecosystem.
Facts

Did You Know?

  • One of India's most important wintering grounds for migratory birds.
  • Recognized globally as a Ramsar Site of International Importance.
  • Created in 1975 by damming the Beas River, producing an immense freshwater reservoir.
  • Supports over 100,000 migratory birds each winter, primarily the Bar-headed Goose.
  • A thriving center for local fisheries and sustainable eco-tourism.
