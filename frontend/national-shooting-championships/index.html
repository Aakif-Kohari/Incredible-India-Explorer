National Shooting Championships

From the inaugural 1957 nationals to the Paris 2024 triple-medal haul — journey through seven decades of Indian shooting glory. Discover champions, disciplines, Olympic heroes, and the range legends who carried the tricolour to global podiums.

Championship History

Seven Decades of the Range

The Indian National Shooting Championships began in 1957, organised by the newly formed National Rifle Association of India (NRAI). What started as a modest gathering of marksmen from a handful of states has grown into the country's premier domestic shooting tournament, crowning champions across rifle, pistol, shotgun, and para disciplines every year.

Early editions were dominated by shooters from the Services, Railways, and Maharashtra — institutions that invested in range infrastructure. The 1980s and 90s saw the rise of Punjab and Haryana as shooting powerhouses, a trend that continues today. The tournament has weathered administrative changes, the reorganisation of NRAI, and the sport's professionalisation — yet it remains the crucible where India's Olympic hopefuls are forged.

🏆 At a Glance

  • 1957 — Inaugural edition held in New Delhi
  • 4 Disciplines — Rifle, Pistol, Shotgun, Para
  • 8 Olympic Medals — 1 Gold, 2 Silver, 5 Bronze (as of Paris 2024)
  • 3 Paralympic Games — Tokyo 2020 breakthrough with 5 medals
  • NRAI Governance — Affiliated to ISSF since 1961, recognised by IOA
Disciplines

The Divisions of Precision

Modern nationals follow ISSF disciplines. Shooters compete across four main categories, each demanding distinct skills and equipment.

🎯

Rifle

Precision long-range marksmanship with air rifle and small-bore rifle events. Requires exceptional stability, breath control, and trigger technique.

  • 10m Air Rifle — 60 shots, indoor
  • 50m Rifle 3 Positions — Kneeling, prone, standing
  • 50m Rifle Prone — 60 shots, precision

Olympic events: 10m Air Rifle, 50m Rifle 3 Positions

🔫

Pistol

Rapid-fire and precision handgun events. Demands split-second timing, steady hand, and mental composure under pressure.

  • 10m Air Pistol — 60 shots, indoor
  • 25m Rapid Fire Pistol — 60 shots, timed series
  • 25m Sport Pistol — Precision + rapid (women)
  • 50m Pistol — 60 shots, free pistol

Olympic events: 10m Air Pistol, 25m Rapid Fire (men), 25m Sport (women)

💥

Shotgun

Moving-target clay pigeon shooting. Tests reaction speed, hand-eye coordination, and smooth swing-through technique.

  • Trap — Single target, 5 stations
  • Double Trap — Simultaneous pair (discontinued 2018)
  • Skeet — High/low house, 8 stations

Olympic events: Trap (mixed team), Skeet

♿

Para Shooting

Adaptive shooting for differently-abled athletes. Governed by World Shooting Para Sport (WSPS) with classification by impairment type.

  • SH1 — Pistol/Rifle, no support needed
  • SH2 — Rifle with spring stand support
  • SH3 — Rifle for visual impairment (future)

Paralympic since 1976 | India's breakthrough: Tokyo 2020 (5 medals)

Services & Railways Dominance: The Indian Army, Navy, Air Force (competing as "Services"), and Indian Railways have won the most team championships. Most Olympians emerged from these institutional systems with dedicated range facilities.

Olympic Connections

From Range to the Five Rings

The National Championships serve as the primary selection pathway for the Indian Olympic shooting team. Nearly every Indian shooter who has competed at the Olympics since 1984 first proved themselves at the nationals.

1984

Los Angeles Debut

India sent its first modern-era shooters to the Olympics. Gagan Narang and others began their Olympic journey through the nationals.

2004

First Medal — Athens

Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore won silver in Double Trap — India's first individual Olympic medal in any sport since 1952.

2008

Historic Gold — Beijing

Abhinav Bindra won Gold in 10m Air Rifle — India's first-ever individual Olympic gold medal. A 9-time national champion.

2012

Bronze at London

Gagan Narang won bronze in 10m Air Rifle. Vijay Kumar added silver in 25m Rapid Fire Pistol — India's first double shooting medal Olympics.

2020

Paralympic Breakthrough — Tokyo

Avani Lekhara (Gold + Bronze), Manish Narwal (Gold), Singhraj Adhana (Bronze) — India's first Paralympic shooting medals.

2024

Triple Medal — Paris

Manu Bhaker (Double Bronze), Sarabjot Singh (Bronze Mixed Team), Swapnil Kusale (Bronze) — India's best-ever Olympic shooting haul (3 medals).

Milestones

Defining Moments in Indian Shooting

Key events that shaped the sport's trajectory in India.

1957

Inaugural National Championships

First Senior National Shooting Championships held in New Delhi. Organised by the National Rifle Association of India (NRAI).

1961

NRAI Joins ISSF

National Rifle Association of India affiliates with International Shooting Sport Federation, connecting Indian shooters to the global stage.

1974

World Championships in India

New Delhi hosts the ISSF World Shooting Championships — first time in Asia. India showcases organisational capability.

1984

Olympic Debut

Indian shooters compete at Los Angeles Olympics. Marks the beginning of India's continuous Olympic shooting presence.

2004

First Olympic Medal — Athens

Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore wins silver in Double Trap — India's first individual Olympic medal since 1952.

2008

Historic Olympic Gold — Beijing

Abhinav Bindra wins Gold in 10m Air Rifle — India's first-ever individual Olympic gold medal. Sparks nationwide shooting boom.

2010

Delhi Commonwealth Games

India hosts CWG at Karni Singh Range. Shooters win 14 medals (4 gold), announcing India as Commonwealth shooting powerhouse.

2012

Double Medal at London

Gagan Narang (bronze, 10m Air Rifle) and Vijay Kumar (silver, 25m Rapid Fire Pistol) — India's first double shooting medal Olympics.

2016

NRAI Reorganisation & BFI Parallel

Administrative restructuring strengthens governance. NRAI recognised by IOA, maintaining Olympic pathway.

2020

Paralympic Breakthrough — Tokyo

Avani Lekhara (Gold + Bronze), Manish Narwal (Gold), Singhraj Adhana (Bronze) — 5 medals, India's Paralympic shooting debut.

2024

Triple Medal Haul — Paris

Manu Bhaker (2× Bronze), Sarabjot Singh (Bronze Mixed Team), Swapnil Kusale (Bronze) — India's best-ever Olympic shooting performance.
