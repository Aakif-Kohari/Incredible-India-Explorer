Alibaug Beach: Black Sand and Sea Forts

Mumbai's Closest Konkan Escape, Guarded by Kolaba Fort

Across the harbour from Mumbai, on the Raigad coast of Maharashtra, Alibaug Beach runs in a long dark-sand curve facing the Arabian Sea. At low tide, a sand path opens to the offshore Kolaba Fort, and the coastline behind it is dotted with coconut groves, weekend homes, and a chain of quieter beaches — Nagaon, Akshi, Kashid — that stretch south along the Konkan.

📍 Location & Beach Overview

Alibaug is a coastal town in Raigad district on Maharashtra's Konkan coast, directly across the bay from Mumbai. By road it sits roughly 95 km south of the city via the Mumbai–Goa highway, though most weekend visitors cross by ferry from the Gateway of India to Mandwa jetty and complete the last stretch by road, cutting the journey to under two hours.

The main beach fronts the town itself and is known for its firm, dark volcanic sand rather than the pale sand typical of Goa's coast further south. Unlike more built-up beach towns, Alibaug has stayed a mix of fishing-village character and low-rise weekend resorts, with the offshore Kolaba Fort forming its most recognisable landmark.

🌴 Coastal Landscape & Surroundings

Alibaug's shoreline is flat and wide at low tide, its dark sand a legacy of the volcanic Deccan basalt that lines much of this stretch of the Konkan coast. Casuarina and coconut groves run behind the beach, screening the town's guesthouses and weekend villas from direct view of the water.

South of the main beach, a string of quieter coves continues the coastline — Nagaon and Akshi beaches a few kilometres down, and further on, Kashid Beach, known for its pale sand and clearer water, a contrast to Alibaug's own dark shore.

🏰 Sea Forts & Nearby Attractions

Alibaug's coastline is part of a chain of forts built by the Maratha navy under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and his admiral Kanhoji Angre to guard the Konkan coast. The nearest and most visited is Kolaba Fort, an island fortress connected to Alibaug beach by a sand causeway that surfaces only at low tide, giving visitors a narrow window each day to walk out and back.

Further south, Murud-Janjira Fort sits on a rocky island and is one of the few coastal forts in India never captured by either the Portuguese, British, or Marathas, thanks to its natural sea defences and thick masonry walls, still largely intact today.

Landmarks Along the Coast

  • Kolaba Fort: Tide-linked 17th-century Maratha sea fort directly off Alibaug's main beach.
  • Murud-Janjira Fort (~55 km South): Island fortress of the Siddis, famed for never having fallen to invasion.
  • Kashid Beach (~35 km South): A quieter, pale-sand beach popular for its calmer waters.
  • Nagaon & Akshi Beaches (~5–8 km South): Smaller, family-friendly stretches with fewer crowds than the main beach.

Key Visitor Activities at Alibaug

  • Kolaba Fort Walk: Time your visit to low tide to walk the sand causeway out to the fort and explore its ramparts.
  • Horse & Camel Rides: A long-standing beachfront tradition along Alibaug's main stretch of sand.
  • Water Sports: Jet-skiing, banana boat rides, and parasailing are offered by local operators near the main beach.
  • Fort-Hopping Day Trip: Combine Kolaba Fort with a drive south to Murud-Janjira for a full day of Konkan sea-fort history.
  • Sunset on the Beach: The wide, flat sand makes Alibaug a popular evening spot for walks and beachside snacks.

Notable Nearby Points of Interest

  • Kolaba Fort (On the Beach): Tide-accessible island fort with a small Ganesh shrine and freshwater well inside.
  • Nagaon Beach (~5 km South): A calmer, family-oriented beach with water sports facilities.
  • Akshi Beach (~8 km South): A long, quiet stretch popular with local day-trippers.
  • Kashid Beach (~35 km South): Pale-sand beach set against the Phansad Wildlife Sanctuary.
  • Murud-Janjira Fort (~55 km South): Historic island fortress reachable by a short boat ride from Rajapuri jetty.
  • Kihim Beach (~12 km North): A forested, quieter beach known for its casuarina groves.

Practical Travel & Safety Guidelines

  • Best Season: October to February offers cool, dry weather; monsoon months (June–Sept) bring rough seas and are best avoided for swimming.
  • Kolaba Fort Timing: Check local tide tables before setting out; the causeway floods quickly once the tide turns.
  • Ocean Safety: Currents can be strong near the fort causeway; avoid swimming close to the rocks at high tide.
  • How to Reach: Ferry from Gateway of India (Mumbai) to Mandwa, then road transfer; alternatively drive via the Mumbai–Goa highway and JNPT route.
