Trek Overview

Garhwal's Cradle Valley Walk

The Har Ki Dun valley lies in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, nestled within the Govind Pashu Vihar Wildlife Sanctuary.

📍 Trek Location

The Har Ki Dun valley lies in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, nestled within the Govind Pashu Vihar Wildlife Sanctuary. This cradle-shaped hanging valley at 3,566 m is surrounded by snow-capped peaks and serves as the base for expeditions to Swargarohini and other peaks in the region.

🚩 Starting Point

The trailhead is Sankri (2,650 m), a traditional Garhwali village reachable via a drive from Dehradun. The walk proper begins at Taluka, the confluence area beyond Sankri where forest permits are verified.

🏔️ Mountain Landscape

The trail descends from alpine meadows through dense pine and rhododendron forests, crossing moraine ridges and glacial streams. The valley opens into a wide basin framed by towering peaks, offering panoramic views of the Himalayan range.

📜 Cultural Heritage

Har Ki Dun is traditionally believed to be the route the Pandavas took to ascend to heaven (Swarga), making it significant in Hindu mythology. The valley villages of Sankri, Osla, and Seema retain ancient Garhwali culture and traditions.

Permits & Responsible Travel

Plan With Safety and Respect

🛂 Forest Permits

Forest department permits are required for Govind Pashu Vihar Wildlife Sanctuary. Indian trekkers need a permit plus registration with local guides; foreign nationals require additional clearances arranged in advance.

🥾 Fitness & Acclimatisation

This is a moderate camping trek with altitudes reaching 3,566 m. Build stamina for 4–6 hour walking days over varied terrain, schedule acclimatisation at Karna Tal, and descend immediately if altitude sickness symptoms appear.

🌿 Leave No Trace

Govind Pashu Vihar is a protected wildlife sanctuary. Carry all waste back out, avoid damaging fragile alpine meadows, use established campsites, and respect the local Garhwali communities and their traditions.

🧭 Go With Local Experts

The trail involves river crossings and variable path conditions. Travel with certified guides, registered porters, and current weather advisories — independent trekking is not recommended on this route.

⚠️ High-Altitude Safety Notice

The Har Ki Dun Trek is a moderate remote-area expedition, not a weekend hike. This page is for educational exploration only — always rely on official permit guidance, trained trek leaders, and up-to-date local conditions before planning any real-world trekking decisions.
