THE NEOLITHIC AGE

A transition toward settled life

The Neolithic period in the Indian subcontinent saw major changes in food production, settlement, technology and daily life. Communities increasingly practiced agriculture, domesticated animals, made pottery and used polished stone tools.

KEY DEVELOPMENTS

What changed?

🌾

Agriculture

Communities cultivated crops such as wheat, barley, rice and millets in different regions.

🐄

Domestication

Animals including cattle, sheep, goats and dogs became increasingly important to settled communities.

🏺

Pottery

Pottery provided durable containers for storing, preparing and transporting food and other materials.

🪨

Tools

Ground and polished stone tools improved farming, woodworking and other everyday activities.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE

Major Neolithic Sites

Burzahom

Kashmir

Known for pit dwellings, stone tools, pottery and evidence of early farming and animal use.

Gufkral

Kashmir

An important prehistoric settlement showing evidence of early food production and changing settlement patterns.

Koldihwa

Uttar Pradesh

Associated with early rice cultivation and Neolithic settlement remains in the Belan Valley.

Mahagara

Uttar Pradesh

Provides evidence of early farming, animal keeping and settlement in the Belan Valley.

Chirand

Bihar

A major Neolithic site known for pottery, bone tools, stone tools and evidence of food production.

Daojali Hading

Assam

An important northeastern site associated with polished stone tools, pottery and early settled communities.

Piklihal

Karnataka

Shows evidence of pastoral communities and animal domestication in southern India.

Utnur

Telangana

An important southern Neolithic site associated with pastoral activity and ash-mound traditions.

A JOURNEY THROUGH TIME

Neolithic Timeline

c. 7000 BCE

Early food production

Farming and herding traditions developed in parts of the northwestern subcontinent.

c. 6000–4000 BCE

Farming spreads

Different regions developed their own agricultural, pastoral and settlement traditions.

c. 4000–2500 BCE

Regional Neolithic cultures

Pottery, polished stone tools, permanent settlements and food production became increasingly widespread.

c. 3000 BCE onward

New technologies and societies

In several regions, Neolithic traditions gradually interacted with or transitioned into later Chalcolithic and Bronze Age cultures.
