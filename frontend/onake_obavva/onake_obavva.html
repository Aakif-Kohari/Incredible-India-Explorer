Onake Obavva

The Defender of Chitradurga Fort — remembered as a powerful symbol of courage, resilience and Nari Shakti.

Weapon of Choice: The Onake

The Hero

Onake Obavva is remembered in Karnataka's cultural history as an extraordinary civilian defender associated with the 18th-century defense of Chitradurga Fort. According to the traditional account, she confronted infiltrating soldiers using an ordinary household rice-pounding pestle known as an onake.

Era

18th Century, traditionally associated with the siege of Chitradurga around 1779 CE.

Role

Remembered as an unsung civilian defender and cultural hero.

Weapon

A heavy wooden rice-pounding pestle called an onake.

Legacy

A symbol of Nari Shakti and Kannada regional pride.

Chitradurga Fort

The legendary episode is traditionally associated with Chitradurga Fort, locally known as Elusuttina Kote — the Fort of Seven Circles — and Kallina Kote, meaning Stone Fort.

Rocky Landscape

The fort occupies a dramatic rocky landscape in central Karnataka, associated with the Chinmuladri hill range and the Vedavati River valley.

Seven Defensive Rings

Its famous seven concentric defensive layers used walls, boulders, gates and natural terrain to make direct attacks difficult.

Strategic Entrances

Staggered and zigzag pathways helped reduce the effectiveness of cavalry and elephant-led assaults.

Water & Watchtowers

The fort incorporated reservoirs, water-management structures and numerous elevated defensive positions.

Historical Context

The Siege of Chitradurga

Eighteenth-century southern India was marked by struggles involving the Marathas, the Nizam of Hyderabad, the Nayakas and the Kingdom of Mysore under Hyder Ali.


Chitradurga was ruled by Raja Madakari Nayaka V, traditionally regarded as the final ruler of the Nayaka dynasty. Hyder Ali's forces eventually besieged the heavily fortified stronghold.


Traditional accounts describe repeated difficulties in taking the fort by direct assault, leading to attempts to identify vulnerable points and exploit intelligence about the fort.

The Story of Onake Obavva

According to the traditional narrative, an enemy scout noticed people using a narrow opening between the fort's rocks. The opening became known as Obavvana Kindi, or Obavva's Crevice.

Silent resistance

The account says that Obavva, while fetching water, heard unusual sounds near the crevice. Looking through the opening, she saw enemy soldiers attempting to enter one by one.


She reportedly seized a household pestle — the onake — and used it to strike the emerging soldiers. The traditional story emphasizes her silence, speed and determination, as she moved the fallen attackers aside before the next soldier appeared.


When her husband, traditionally identified as a sentry named Kahale Mudda Hanuma or Kalanayaka, returned, he discovered the situation and raised the alarm with his horn. The defenders were then alerted to the attempted infiltration.

Obavvana Kindi

The celebrated passage is traditionally identified not as a formal underground military tunnel but as a narrow natural fracture in the granite boulder landscape. Its association with Obavva has turned it into one of the most recognizable heritage features connected with the Chitradurga story.

Natural Fracture

The opening is associated with the fort's natural rocky terrain rather than a conventional tunnel system.

Local Use

Traditional accounts connect the passage with movement by families associated with the fort's sentries.

Historical Memory

The site preserves the physical setting associated with one of Karnataka's best-known heroic traditions.

Why It Matters

Historical Timeline

10th–12th Century CE

Early fortification traditions in the region are associated with earlier ruling powers, including the Chalukyas and Hoysalas.

15th–18th Century CE

The Nayakas of Chitradurga expanded and strengthened the fortification.

18th Century

Chitradurga became involved in the wider political and military conflicts of southern India.

1779 CE

The traditional chronology associates the siege and the story of Onake Obavva with this period.

1799 CE

The political history of the region changed dramatically following the defeat of Tipu Sultan and the subsequent British expansion.

2021 CE

The Government of Karnataka established an official observance of Onake Obavva Jayanti.

Legacy

Onake Obavva Jayanti

Karnataka commemorates Onake Obavva annually on November 11, celebrating her courage and contribution to cultural memory.

Obavva Pade

The name has also been associated with an all-women initiative of the Karnataka police focused on safety, self-defense and women's empowerment.

Popular Culture

Obavva has been represented in Kannada popular culture, including the historical film Nagarahavu (1972), in which she was portrayed by B. Saroja Devi.

Nari Shakti

Her story continues to be used as a cultural symbol of women's courage, resilience and determination.
