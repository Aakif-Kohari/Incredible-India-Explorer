Nilgiri Tahr (Nilgiritragus hylocrius)

The Nilgiri Tahr — locally known in Tamil and Malayalam as Varaiyadu ("Cliff Goat") — is India's only endemic mountain ungulate. A master cliff climber with specialized rubbery hoof pads, it inhabits the high-altitude Shola-grassland sky islands of Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

Introduction

The Cliff Sovereign of the Western Ghats

The Nilgiri Tahr (Nilgiritragus hylocrius) is a stocky ungulate with short, crisp dark brown or charcoal coat fur, bristly mane, and curved horns. As the only species in the monotypic genus Nilgiritragus, it represents a millions-of-years-old evolutionary lineage preserved in the high-altitude sky islands of South India.

Celebrated as the official State Animal of Tamil Nadu, the species is tightly bound to the unique Shola-grassland ecosystem above 1,200 meters elevation, acting as an ecological indicator of montane watershed health.

Key Highlights

  • Sole Endemic Goat: India's only endemic mountain ungulate.
  • Saddleback Bulls: Dominant adult males feature a distinct silvery lumbar saddle patch.
  • Project Nilgiri Tahr: ₹25-Crore conservation mission launched by Tamil Nadu in Oct 2022.
  • Highest Protection: Listed under Schedule I of India's Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

Taxonomy & Classification

Phylogenetic reclassification placed the Nilgiri Tahr into its own monotypic genus Nilgiritragus, proving it is genetically closer to sheep (Ovis) than true goats (Capra).

Habitat & Shola Ecosystem

Montane Grassland-Shola Mosaics

The Nilgiri Tahr is restricted to undulating high-altitude plateau grasslands bordered by stunted temperate evergreen Shola forests. High atmospheric humidity, frequent cloud cover, and cold nighttime temperatures characterize this unique biome.

Grasslands provide forage (tussock grasses such as Chrysopogon nodulibarbis), while bordering cliffs offer immediate refuge from predators like leopards, wild dogs (dholes), and tigers.

Sky Island Escarpments

Sheer precipices at 1,200m – 2,695m altitude for escape security.

High Elevation Climate

Cool montane climate with heavy monsoon rainfall exceeding 3,000mm.

Ecology & Behavior

Diurnal Foraging

Active during early morning and late afternoon grazing periods, retreating to rock crags during mid-day heat or heavy fog.

Sentinel Whistle

Herd sentries post up on elevated peaks, sounding a high-pitched alarm whistle that sends the entire group bounding up cliff walls.

Monsoon Rutting

Breeding peaks during the South-West Monsoon (July–August), leading to single kid births between January and February.

Sexual Dimorphism — Male vs Female

Nilgiri Tahrs display prominent sexual dimorphism. Fully mature adult males, known as Saddlebacks, differ dramatically from females and juveniles in weight, horn size, and lumbar patch coloration.

Conservation & Project Nilgiri Tahr

Project Nilgiri Tahr (Oct 2022)

Launched by the Tamil Nadu Forest Department with an allocation of ₹25 Crore, Project Nilgiri Tahr is a flagship initiative focusing on:

  • Radio telemetry collar tracking to study spatial movement and corridors.
  • Removal of exotic invasive plant species (Wattle, Eucalyptus, Lantana) restoring native grasslands.
  • Celebrating October 7th as Nilgiri Tahr Day in honor of E.R. C. Davidar.
  • Eco-tourism guidelines and community awareness programs.

Major Threats

  • Habitat Fragmentation: Isolated populations on sky islands limit genetic interchange.
  • Exotic Timber Plantations: Historical commercial forestry encroaching on montane grasslands.
  • Climate Warming: Shifting weather patterns altering high-altitude grassland ecology.
