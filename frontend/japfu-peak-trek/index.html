Japfu Peak Trek Scale the second highest peak of Nagaland (3,048m)

Embark on an exhilarating ascent from Kigwema village through dense sub-tropical forests to the summit of Japfu Peak. Witness the Guinness World Record-holding giant Rhododendron tree and breathtaking bird's-eye views of the emerald Dzukou Valley.

3,048 m Peak Elevation (10,000 ft)
15 km Total Traverse Distance
2 Days Expected Duration
Kigwema / Jakhama Starting Base Village

Discover the Japfu Peak Trek

Located in the Kohima district, the Japfu Peak trek is a mesmerizing expedition into the heart of Nagaland's pristine wilderness. Towering over the landscape, Japfu is renowned not just for its height, but for its rich biodiversity and commanding views.

The Summit Experience

Reaching the summit at 3,048 meters is exhilarating. Because it rises above the surrounding ridges, the peak serves as a natural watchtower. From here, trekkers get an unmatched panoramic perspective of the entire Dzukou Valley rolling out like a vast green carpet. On clear winter days, the distant snow-capped peaks of the eastern Himalayas are visible.

Major Attractions & Highlights

💡 Quick Trek Tips

  • Starting Point: The traditional Angami Naga villages of Kigwema or Jakhama. Both offer excellent base camps.
  • Best Season: October to April offers clear skies. Avoid monsoons as trails become highly slippery.
  • Permits: Inner Line Permits (ILP) are required for domestic tourists, and Restricted Area Permits (RAP) for foreigners.
  • Local Guides: Hiring a guide from Kigwema is highly recommended as the dense bamboo trails can be confusing.

Trek Route & Stage Breakdown

The trail begins as a gradual dirt path before shifting into a steep, relentless climb under thick canopies, eventually breaking above the tree line.

Forest Landscape, Flora & Fauna

The trek traverses diverse ecological zones, from dense broadleaf forests to high-altitude dwarf bamboo thickets.

🚨 Essential Safety Guidelines

The steep gradients and unpredictable weather require proper preparation:

1. Weather Variability

The weather at 3,000m is highly unpredictable. The summit is exposed to freezing winds. Always carry windproof and waterproof outer layers regardless of the season.

2. Water Scarcity

There are limited reliable water streams on the upper ridge. Carry at least 2-3 liters of water per person from the base village.

3. Leeches

If trekking during the shoulder monsoon months (September/May), the lower forest is heavily infested with leeches. Carry salt and wear protective leech socks.
