The Lameta Formation & India's Dinosaur Record

The Lameta Formation is India’s most famous geological horizon. Deposited in the Late Cretaceous (~70 to 66 Million Years Ago) across Central and Western India, this thin sedimentary package encapsulates the twilight of non-avian dinosaurs right before the cataclysmic Deccan Traps volcanic floods.

70–66 Ma Maastrichtian Stage
20–40 m Average Thickness
10+ Described Dinosaur Genera
10,000 km² Geographic Extent

Stratigraphy & Lithological Characteristics

The Lameta Formation consists of distinct sedimentary units deposited under semi-arid fluvial, lacustrine, and estuarine environments.

Layer 1 (Top)

Mottled Nodular Limestone

Characterized by abundant pedogenic calcretes and carbonate nodules. Represents ancient river floodplain palaeosols where dinosaur egg clutches are commonly fossilized.

Layer 2 (Middle)

Green Sandstone & Sandy Clay

Fine-to-medium grained glauconitic/calcareous sandstone laid down by meandering Cretaceous stream channels; primary source of articulated titanosaur bones.

Layer 3 (Lower)

Cherty Limestone & Jasperoid Beds

Silicified lacustrine limestone beds containing freshwater gastropods (*Physa prinsepii*), ostracods, charophyte algae, and fish scales.

Layer 4 (Base)

Basal Sandstone & Conglomerate

Coarse pebbly sandstone resting unconformably on Precambrian metamorphic granites or Jurassic Gondwana sedimentary rocks.

Geographic Extent of the Lameta Belt

Discontinuous Basins Across Central India

The Lameta Formation is not a single contiguous mountain range, but an array of patchy, flat-lying sedimentary basins extending across Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra.

  • Jabalpur Basin (MP): Lameta Ghat, Bara Simla, and Chui Hill (classic historical outcrops).
  • Narmada Valley (MP): Dhar, Bagh, Mandu, and Kukshi basins (massive nesting fields).
  • Western Gujarat: Balasinor (Raiyoli), Kheda, and Panchmahal districts (bone beds & nests).
  • Pranhita-Godavari & Nagpur (Maharashtra): Pisdura, Dongargaon, and Umrer (intertrappean beds).

🗺️ Palaeogeographic Setting

India was an isolated landmass separated from Africa and Madagascar, drifting northward at high tectonic speeds (15–20 cm/year) across the equator.

The Deccan Traps Volcanism & K-Pg Boundary

🌋 Infratrappean Stratigraphy

The Lameta Formation sits immediately beneath (infra) the oldest Deccan basalt lava flows, capturing the final thousands of years before catastrophic volcanic eruptions.

🔥 Intertrappean Lake Horizons

Between volcanic pulses, cooling lava surfaces formed freshwater lakes (intertrappeans), preserving micro-fossils, frogs, turtles, and abnormal dinosaur eggshells.

💥 The Dual Extinction Hypotheses

The Lameta record is critical to the global debate: Did toxic volcanic emissions from the Deccan Traps weaken Indian dinosaurs right before the Chicxulub asteroid impact?

Pioneering Discoveries & Research History

1828

Captain William Sleeman's Discovery

Sleeman discovered the first fossil dinosaur bones in Asia at Bara Simla hill in Jabalpur while inspecting artillery roads.

1877

Richard Lydekker Names Titanosaurus

Lydekker described *Titanosaurus indicus* based on caudal vertebrae from Jabalpur, establishing the foundation of titanosaur taxonomy.

1933

Huene & Matley Monograph

Friedrich von Huene and Charles Matley published a seminal monograph documenting dozens of carnivorous and sauropod taxa from Jabalpur.

2003 – Present

Modern Renaissance: Rajasaurus & Dhar Nests

Formal naming of *Rajasaurus narmadensis* (2003) and the 2023 discovery of 92 nesting sites solidified the Lameta Formation's international fame.
