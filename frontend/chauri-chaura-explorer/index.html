Chauri Chaura, 1922: The Day a Movement Was Called Off

A Clash at a Small Police Station That Changed the Course of India's Freedom Struggle

On the afternoon of 5 February 1922, a procession of Non-Cooperation volunteers in the small town of Chauri Chaura, in Gorakhpur district of the United Provinces (now Uttar Pradesh), clashed with police. The confrontation ended with the local police station set alight and 22 policemen killed inside it. Within days, Mahatma Gandhi suspended the nationwide Non-Cooperation Movement, arguing that a movement built on non-violence could not continue once it had produced violence of this scale.

📜 Local Political Background

By late 1921, the Non-Cooperation Movement launched by the Indian National Congress under Mahatma Gandhi's leadership had spread through much of rural India, including Gorakhpur district in the eastern United Provinces. Local Congress and Khilafat committees organised boycotts of foreign cloth, government schools, courts, and titles, alongside the promotion of khadi and Hindu-Muslim unity in support of the Khilafat cause.

Chauri Chaura was a small market town on the rail line between Gorakhpur and Deoria. Like much of the surrounding countryside, it had seen growing volunteer (swayamsevak) activity through 1921, including local meetings, processions, and appeals to villagers to join the boycott campaigns. Economic distress from high market prices and existing grievances against local police conduct added to tensions already present before the events of February 1922.

Non-Cooperation Activities in the Region

  • Volunteer Corps: Local Congress volunteers organised marches and public meetings across Gorakhpur's villages through 1921.
  • Liquor Shop Picketing: A common Non-Cooperation tactic nationwide, also practiced in the Chauri Chaura area.
  • Boycott Campaigns: Calls to avoid foreign cloth, government courts, and titles as acts of civil resistance.
  • Friction With Authorities: Local police had already clashed with volunteers over meetings and processions in preceding weeks.

⚡ Events Leading to the Confrontation

In early February 1922, volunteers connected to the local Non-Cooperation movement were arrested and produced before a magistrate in the area, sharpening resentment among the town's Congress and Khilafat volunteers. On 4 February, a meeting was held to plan further protest activity, and a decision was made to organise a procession the following day.

On 5 February, several thousand volunteers gathered for a procession through Chauri Chaura. Reports of the day describe the procession being confronted by a contingent of police, followed by exchanges that escalated from verbal confrontation to stone-throwing and, eventually, police firing on the crowd, which is understood to have caused casualties among the volunteers.

⚠️ A Note on This Section

Surviving accounts of the exact sequence of events on 5 February 1922 vary in detail across contemporary reports, the official Chauri Chaura trial records, and later historical scholarship. This page presents the broadly agreed outline of events; readers researching the incident in depth are encouraged to consult the cited sources for more detailed and sometimes differing accounts.

🔥 The Incident of 5 February 1922

Following the police firing on the procession, the crowd retreated initially but then turned back toward the police station, reportedly angered by the shooting of unarmed marchers. As the crowd advanced, the police inside the station retreated into the building. The crowd set fire to the police station, and the policemen trapped inside — accounts place the number at 22 — were killed, either by the fire or by the crowd as they attempted to escape.

The incident was one of the most violent single episodes connected to the Non-Cooperation Movement, standing in sharp contrast to the movement's foundational commitment to ahimsa (non-violence) as articulated by Gandhi and the Congress leadership.

Location Chauri Chaura Police Station, Gorakhpur District, United Provinces
Immediate Trigger Police firing on a Non-Cooperation procession

⚖️ Government Response, Arrests, and Trials

In the aftermath, colonial authorities launched a large-scale investigation and arrested hundreds of people from Chauri Chaura and surrounding villages in connection with the incident. Special tribunals were constituted to try those accused of involvement in the killings.

The trials that followed resulted in a large number of convictions, including capital sentences, though many of these were later commuted or overturned on appeal by the Allahabad High Court, which found the evidence and legal basis for a number of the original convictions insufficient.

Legal Aftermath at a Glance

  • Mass Arrests: Hundreds detained across Chauri Chaura and nearby villages in the following weeks.
  • Special Tribunal: Convened to try those charged in connection with the police station killings.
  • Death Sentences: A significant number of death sentences were initially handed down.
  • Appeal Outcome: The Allahabad High Court later commuted many sentences on appeal.

✋ Gandhi's Decision to Suspend the Movement

News of the Chauri Chaura killings reached Gandhi within days. On 12 February 1922, at a meeting of the Congress Working Committee in Bardoli, Gandhi moved for the suspension of the Non-Cooperation Movement, along with the planned mass civil disobedience campaign that had been due to begin in Bardoli itself.

Gandhi's position was that a movement rooted in non-violent resistance could not be sustained if it produced or tolerated violence of the kind seen at Chauri Chaura. He argued that the movement's discipline had broken down in a way that made continued mass action unsafe and inconsistent with its founding principles, and he undertook a fast in atonement for what he described as his own share of responsibility for the violence.

The Bardoli Resolution

The Congress Working Committee's resolution at Bardoli called off civil disobedience and mass Non-Cooperation activities across the country, redirecting the movement's energies toward "constructive work" — the promotion of khadi, temperance, and social reform — until conditions were judged suitable for resuming direct political action.

💬 Reactions Within the Nationalist Movement

Gandhi's decision to suspend the movement drew mixed reactions from within the nationalist ranks. Several senior Congress leaders, including Motilal Nehru and Chittaranjan Das, who were in jail at the time, expressed disappointment, feeling the movement was suspended at a point of considerable momentum.

Younger leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru later described the decision as difficult to understand at the time, even as they largely deferred to Gandhi's moral authority within the movement. The suspension is widely seen by historians as a significant factor behind the subsequent formation of the Swaraj Party by Congress members who favoured contesting council elections rather than continuing non-cooperation.

🏛️ Long-Term Historical Significance

Chauri Chaura is widely regarded by historians as one of the pivotal turning points of the Indian freedom struggle in the early 1920s. It marked the abrupt end of the first mass, all-India phase of civil resistance against British rule, and it illustrated the practical difficulty of sustaining strict non-violent discipline across a movement involving millions of participants with varied local grievances.

The episode also shaped Gandhi's later approach to mass movements — including the Civil Disobedience Movement of the 1930s — with greater emphasis placed on discipline, training of volunteers, and closely managed forms of protest such as the Salt March, partly in response to lessons drawn from Chauri Chaura.

The Non-Cooperation Movement: Before and After Chauri Chaura

Before (1920–Early 1922)

  • Nationwide mass movement combining boycotts, khadi promotion, and Khilafat solidarity.
  • Growing momentum toward planned mass civil disobedience in Bardoli.
  • Broad unity across Congress factions behind Gandhi's strategy.

After (Post-February 1922)

  • Movement suspended nationwide; focus shifted to "constructive work."
  • Congress leadership divided over strategy going forward.
  • Formation of the Swaraj Party by leaders favouring council entry.
