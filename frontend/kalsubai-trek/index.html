Discover Kalsubai Peak

Kalsubai Peak stands at an elevation of 1,646 meters in the Western Ghats (Sahyadris), making it the highest peak in the state of Maharashtra. Flanked by the Kalsubai Harishchandragad Wildlife Sanctuary, the peak attracts thousands of outdoor enthusiasts, especially during the monsoon season.

The trek commences at Bari village and goes through agricultural fields before entering forested slopes. The highlight of the trail is the vertical steel ladders installed on sheer basalt rock faces near the summit, providing a safe passage over vertical drops. At the summit, a tiny traditional temple dedicated to the deity Kalsubai stands on a rocky outcrop, offering sweeping 360-degree views of Bhandardara reservoir, Arthur Lake, and the rugged AMK (Alang-Madan-Kulang) fort ridges.

Major Attractions & Highlights

💡 Quick Trek Tips

  • Ladder Congestion: Weekend monsoon treks get highly crowded. Expect delays at the steel ladder sections due to single-file queues.
  • Hydration: Carry at least 2 liters of water. Simple tea and lemonade stalls are set up by villagers along the path.
  • Safety on Basalt: Basalt rock becomes extremely slippery when wet. Wear shoes with deep grooves.
  • Bhandardara Stays: Combine your trek with a lakeside camp at Bhandardara Arthur Lake post-trek.

Trek Route & Day-wise Highlights

The standard trail starts from Bari village flats, climbs steep forested ridges, negotiates four ladder sections, and reaches the summit shrine.

History & Local Legend

Kalsubai Temple is dedicated to a local deity of the same name. According to Sahyadri folklore, Kalsubai was a housemaid who left the village to escape domestic trouble and spent her final days at the peak, leading to the creation of the summit shrine.
