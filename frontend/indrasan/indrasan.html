Indrasan Explorer

Rising to 6,221 metres in the Pir Panjal range of Himachal Pradesh, Indrasan is one of the most challenging and least-climbed peaks in the Kullu Valley. Named after the Hindu god Indra, this remote peak stands as a sentinel over the ancient Lahaul and Spiti valleys, offering intrepid mountaineers a truly wild Himalayan experience far from commercialised trekking routes.

Geography, History & Significance

The Throne of Indra

Indrasan stands in the Lahaul-Spiti district of Himachal Pradesh, within the Pir Panjal range — the southernmost range of the western Himalaya. The peak derives its name from Lord Indra, the king of gods in Hindu mythology, who is believed to have his celestial throne atop this summit.

The mountain is flanked by the Chandra and Bhaga rivers, whose valleys provide the primary approach routes. Its slopes are covered in perpetual snow and ice, feeding glacial streams that eventually merge into the Chenab river system. The peak's remote location and difficult terrain have kept it relatively unknown compared to other Himachal peaks like Friendship Peak or Hanuman Tibba.

The first recorded ascent was made in 1963 by an Indo-German expedition, a team that also explored several other unclimbed Pir Panjal summits. The mountain remains a test piece for Himalayan mountaineers seeking a serious challenge without the extreme logistics of higher peaks.

Trekking Guide

Routes, Camps & Conditions

Essential information for planning a safe and rewarding Indrasan trek or summit attempt.

🥾

Base Route

The standard approach begins from Manali, driving to the road-head at Tandi or Keylong, then trekking up the Chandra river valley past grazing meadows and moraine fields to reach the base camp at approximately 4,200 m on the Chandra glacier system.

⛺

Camp Strategy

A two-camp structure is typical: Camp 1 at ~5,000 m on the glacier flat below the southwest face, and Camp 2 at ~5,600 m on the upper snowfield. Summit attempts begin before dawn from Camp 2 to take advantage of firm, frozen snow conditions.

🗓️

Best Season

June–September (post-monsoon-avoidance window in the Pir Panjal). The range receives significant snowfall in winter and early spring, making the approach extremely dangerous. Late summer offers the most stable weather and best visibility.

📋

Permits & Logistics

An Inner Line Permit from the District Magistrate's office in Keylong is required for trekking in the Lahaul-Spiti restricted zone. Forest department entry permits and a registration fee at the forest checkpost are mandatory. All supplies must be carried from Manali or Keylong.

Nearby Attractions

Explore the Surrounding Region

Discover remarkable destinations within reach of the Indrasan area.

🏔️

Chandra Tal

The enchanting crescent-shaped moon lake at 4,300 m, fed by glacial melt from the Bara Shigri glacier. A sacred site for both Buddhists and Hindus, surrounded by stark high-altitude desert.

~20 km away
🏜️

Key Monastery

The oldest and largest monastery in the Lahaul-Spiti valley, perched at 4,166 m on a hilltop overlooking the Spiti river. Over 1,000 years old, it houses rare Buddhist manuscripts and thangkas.

~80 km away
🏔️

Deo Tibba

A 6,001 m peak adjacent to Indrasan in the Pir Panjal range, popular among intermediate mountaineers for its relatively accessible approach from the Chandra valley.

~10 km away
🏯

Manali

The adventure capital of Himachal Pradesh at 2,050 m, gateway to Lahaul-Spiti, Rohtang Pass, and Solang Valley. Famous for its ancient Hadimba Temple and vibrant old town.

~150 km away
