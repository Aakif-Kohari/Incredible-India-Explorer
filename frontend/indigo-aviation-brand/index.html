IndiGo — India's Modern Aviation Brand

6E — On Time, Every Time

IndiGo began in 2006 with four aircraft and a single Delhi–Guwahati–Imphal route. Built on strict low-cost-carrier discipline — a single aircraft type, high utilisation, and an obsessive focus on punctuality — it overtook every legacy Indian carrier within six years to become the country's largest airline by market share, a position it still holds today.

🛫 Origin

IndiGo was founded in 2005 as InterGlobe Aviation, a partnership between Rahul Bhatia of InterGlobe Enterprises (a Gurgaon-based travel and hospitality group, holding roughly 51%) and Rakesh Gangwal (an Indian-origin aviation executive based in the US, through his company Caelum Investments, holding roughly 48%). Bhatia brought local market reach and capital; Gangwal — a veteran of United Airlines, US Airways, and Worldspan — brought deep airline-economics expertise. In June 2005, the fledgling airline placed a firm order for 100 Airbus A320s at the Paris Air Show, one of the largest aircraft orders in Indian civil aviation history at the time — a bold statement of intent before the airline had even flown a single passenger.

📈 Airline Growth

IndiGo received its first aircraft in July 2006 and commenced commercial operations on 4 August 2006, flying Delhi–Guwahati–Imphal. Unusually for a startup — most new airlines begin with used aircraft to save costs — IndiGo launched with six brand new Airbus A320s. Growth was rapid and disciplined: by 2010 it was already India's most profitable carrier. On 17 August 2012, just six years after its first flight, IndiGo overtook Jet Airways to become India's largest airline by passenger market share — a position it has not relinquished since.

Going Public

In October–November 2015, IndiGo's parent company InterGlobe Aviation went public in one of the largest IPOs in Indian aviation history, valuing the company at roughly $4 billion and funding further fleet expansion and modernisation.

✈️ Fleet Evolution

IndiGo's strategy has always centred on single-type fleet economics — operating almost exclusively Airbus A320-family aircraft to simplify maintenance, training, and spare-parts logistics. The fleet grew from 6 aircraft at the end of 2006 to 15 by the end of 2007, crossed 50 aircraft by February 2012, and now numbers over 430 aircraft. In 2011, the airline placed a then record-setting order for 180 A320neo-family jets worth roughly $15 billion. It received its first A320neo in 2016, and in 2019 placed its largest order ever — 300 A320neo family aircraft — cementing its position as one of Airbus's largest global customers. IndiGo also operates ATR 72-600 turboprops for shorter regional routes under India's regional connectivity scheme.

🗺️ Route Expansion

IndiGo's early years focused entirely on building a dense domestic network across major Indian cities. After completing five years of domestic operations, it received approval to launch international flights in January 2011, and opened its first international route — Delhi–Dubai — in September 2011. Through 2017–2018, the airline expanded further into the Middle East and Southeast Asia. Today, IndiGo operates over 2,700 daily flights to more than 130 destinations — around 94 domestic and 43 international — anchored by its primary hub at Indira Gandhi International Airport, Delhi, with major operating bases in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, and Kochi.

🎨 Brand Identity

IndiGo's visual identity is built around a distinctive blue-and-white palette and a bold, clean wordmark — a deliberate choice for clarity and consistency, whether on an app icon, a boarding pass, or a full aircraft livery. The airline's IATA code, 6E, features prominently across its branding. The overall visual language reflects the brand's core promise — simple, dependable, and unmistakably on time — and has remained remarkably consistent since launch, a rarity in an industry where many carriers rebrand every few years.
