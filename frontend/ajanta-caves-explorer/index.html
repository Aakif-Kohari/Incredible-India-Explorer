Ajanta Caves Explorer

A UNESCO World Heritage rock-cut complex near Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar), Maharashtra — home to the finest surviving Buddhist paintings and sculpture from ancient India.

📍 Maharashtra 🖼️ Ancient Murals 🏛️ UNESCO Site
Start Exploring ↓

History

Carved in two distinct bursts of activity, seven centuries apart, into the volcanic rock of a horseshoe-shaped gorge above the Waghur River.

Buddhist Heritage

Ajanta spans two major phases of Buddhist tradition, each with its own visual language.

Cave Architecture: Chaitya Halls vs. Viharas

The 30 caves fall into two architectural types, each serving a different purpose in monastic life.

Paintings

Ajanta preserves the most extensive surviving body of ancient Indian painting, offering an unmatched window into classical Indian art.

Sculptures

Alongside the murals, Ajanta's carved figures and reliefs are central to understanding early Indian sculptural style.

UNESCO Heritage

Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, recognized as a masterpiece of Buddhist religious art with lasting influence across Asia.

    Conservation

    Managed today by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), the caves face ongoing conservation challenges.
