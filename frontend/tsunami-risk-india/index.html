Anatomy of a Tsunami

How undersea megathrust fault slips displace thousands of cubic kilometers of ocean water and transform into high-energy coastal wave trains.

🌋

Megathrust Earthquakes

Vertical seafloor displacement at subduction boundaries drives massive water columns.

Tsunamis are generated when tectonic plates snap during undersea megathrust earthquakes. As the overriding plate rebounds upward, it displaces the overlying ocean column, initiating high-speed wave energy outwards.

🚀

Deep Ocean Propagation

Travels at jet-airliner speeds (~700–800 km/h) with low surface height.

In deep ocean waters (>4,000 m), tsunami waves feature long wavelengths (100–200 km) and small surface amplitudes (<1 m), moving at speeds matching commercial aircraft without losing energy.

🌊

Shallow-Water Shoaling

Wave speed drops, wavelength compresses, and amplitude surges shoreward.

As the wave enters shallow coastal waters, seabed friction slows the front to 30–50 km/h. The trailing wave mass compresses behind it, surging upward into destructive walls of water 5 to 30 meters high.

🏖️

Initial Water Drawdown

Trough arrival causes sea water to recede rapidly offshore.

If the wave trough strikes land first, ocean waters retreat hundreds of meters offshore within minutes, exposing reefs and sea floors—an urgent natural warning of an impending crest arrival.

🔄

Multiple Wave Trains

A tsunami arrives as a series of surges; the first is rarely the largest.

Tsunamis consist of multiple wave surges spaced 10 to 60 minutes apart. The second, third, or fourth wave train is frequently far more destructive than the initial surge arrival.

Andaman-Sumatra vs. Makran Subduction Zones

A comparative evaluation of India's two active subduction zone tsunami sources in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.

Andaman-Sumatra Subduction Arc

The primary threat zone for East Coast India and Andaman Islands, capable of generating mega-tsunamis exceeding Mw 9.0.

Parameter Subduction Arc Profile
Plate Boundary Indo-Australian Plate subducting beneath the Burma Microplate / Sunda Plate
Trench Length ~5,500 km arc length stretching from Myanmar to Indonesia
Seismic Capacity Mw 9.0 to 9.3 Megathrust events
Mainland Travel Time 2.0 to 3.0 hours to Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Odisha

Makran Subduction Zone

The primary tsunami threat for West Coast India in the Arabian Sea, extending off the coast of Pakistan and Iran.

Parameter Subduction Zone Profile
Plate Boundary Arabian Plate subducting beneath the Eurasian Plate
Trench Length ~900 km subduction line
Seismic Capacity Mw 8.0 to 8.5 Megathrust events (e.g. 1945 Makran earthquake)
Mainland Travel Time 1.5 to 3.5 hours to Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, and Karnataka

Tsunami Early Warning & Coastal Mitigation

How India operates real-time deep ocean tsunameters, seismic tracking, and UNESCO Tsunami Ready community resilience programs.

🛰️

INCOIS ITEWS Warning Center

Issues location-specific tsunami warnings within 10 minutes of undersea earthquakes.

The Indian Tsunami Early Warning System (ITEWS) at INCOIS Hyderabad operates 24x7 with real-time seismic tracking, ocean buoy networks, and pre-run hydrodynamic scenario models.

⚓

Bottom Pressure Recorders (BPRs)

Deep ocean seabed tsunameter sensors measuring sub-centimeter wave height changes.

Moored in deep ocean waters of the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea, BPR sensors detect passing tsunami waves on the ocean floor and transmit acoustic telemetry to satellite warning systems.

🏅

UNESCO Tsunami Ready Program

Community resilience accreditation in Odisha coastal villages.

Odisha villages (Venkatraipur and Noliasahi) became the first communities in the Indian Ocean region certified under the UNESCO Tsunami Ready program, equipped with public sirens and evacuation plans.

🌿

Coastal Mangrove Bio-Shields

Nature-based vegetation buffers dissipating tsunami wave energy.

Dense coastal mangrove systems (Pichavaram, Sundarbans) and Casuarina dune plantations act as natural wave attenuation barriers, slowing run-up velocity and protecting coastal settlements.

⚠️ Natural Tsunami Warning Signs & Evacuation Rules

  • Strong Ground Shaking: If you feel a powerful earthquake near the coast that lasts more than 20 seconds, evacuate inland or to high ground immediately.
  • Rapid Ocean Retreat: A sudden drawdown of water exposing reefs and sea floor is an urgent signal of an incoming tsunami crest within minutes. Do not go onto the beach to look at it.
  • Roaring Ocean Sound: A loud, roaring noise approaching from the sea like a train or jet engine signals immediate wave landfall.
  • Evacuation Protocol: Move at least 2 km inland or to an elevation of 15–20 meters above sea level. In dense urban areas, move to the 3rd floor or higher of reinforced concrete buildings. Stay away from the coast until official all-clear bulletins are issued by INCOIS.
