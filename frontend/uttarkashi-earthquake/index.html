Earthquake event

20 January 1991 — Uttarkashi, Garhwal

The earthquake occurred at 08:53:51 IST on 20 January 1991, with an epicenter near Uttarkashi in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttar Pradesh. It was one of the most destructive earthquakes in the Himalayan region during the 20th century.

Himalayan geology

Seismic characteristics of the Garhwal Himalayas

The 1991 Uttarkashi earthquake resulted from reverse faulting associated with the ongoing collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates. The Himalayan region remains seismically active due to the northward movement of the Indian plate at approximately 5 cm/year, which generates compressive stresses that are released episodically along thrust and reverse faults.

  • Plate convergence: ~5 cm/year northward
  • Fault type: Reverse fault / thrust faulting
  • Seismic gap: Part of the ongoing Himalayan seismic zone
  • Tectonic setting: Continental collision zone

Infrastructure and human impact

The earthquake caused widespread devastation in the Uttarkashi district and surrounding areas. Over 1,000 lives were lost, and thousands were injured. The tremors were felt across a vast region of the northwest Himalayas.

  • Death toll: 1,000+ fatalities
  • Injured: ~5,000+ persons
  • Homeless: ~200,000 people
  • Districts affected: Uttarkashi, Tehri, Chamoli, Rudraprayag
  • Felt in: Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh

Landslide connection

Landslides and secondary hazards

The 1991 Uttarkashi earthquake triggered widespread landslides across the Garhwal Himalayas, particularly in steep terrain and river valleys. The combination of strong ground shaking, unstable slopes, and heavy monsoon rains following the event caused numerous landslides that blocked roads, destroyed villages, and complicated rescue operations. Landslides were reported in areas as far as 25 km from the epicenter, with some dams formed by landslides creating additional flood risks.

  • Triggered by: Strong ground shaking (M 6.8)
  • Distribution: Widespread in steep Himalayan terrain
  • Impact: Blocked rivers, damaged infrastructure
  • Secondary hazard: Landslide-dam floods
  • Rescue challenges: Blocked access routes

Emergency response

Rescue and relief operations

The Indian Army, Air Force, and National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) launched large-scale rescue operations. Despite challenges posed by damaged terrain and ongoing aftershocks, relief reached affected areas within 24 hours.

  • Army columns: 20+ deployed for rescue
  • Air drops: Essential supplies to inaccessible villages
  • NDRF teams: 10+ stations established
  • Temporary shelters: ~50,000 erected
  • Medical camps: 20+ set up in worst-affected zones

Lessons for Himalayan communities

Learning from Uttarkashi for future resilience

The Uttarkashi earthquake provided critical lessons for earthquake preparedness in the Himalayas. Unreinforced masonry construction proved highly vulnerable, while properly designed temporary shelters and community preparedness saved lives. The event highlighted the need for building codes tailored to Himalayan geo-climatic conditions, community training in search and rescue, and integration of traditional knowledge with modern engineering.

  1. Unreinforced masonry: Highly vulnerable; retrofitting essential
  2. Community training: Search-and-rescue volunteers critical
  3. Building codes: Himalaya-specific requirements needed
  4. Traditional knowledge: Local wisdom on safe construction sites
  5. Early warning systems: Investment in seismic monitoring

Lasting impact

  • Improved seismic building codes for Uttarakhand
  • Establishment of National Earthquake Risk Evaluation Center
  • Increased public awareness of earthquake preparedness
  • Better coordination between armed forces and civil administration
  • Enhanced earthquake monitoring in the Himalayas
