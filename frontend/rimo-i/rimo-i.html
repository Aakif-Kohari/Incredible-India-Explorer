Rimo I Explorer

Standing tall at 7,385 meters in the remote Rimo Muztagh subrange of Ladakh, Rimo I is a formidable, ice-clad peak of global significance. Guarded by the Rimo Glacier and nearby Siachen region, it remains one of India's most challenging and rarely climbed alpine peaks.

Geography, Glaciers & History

The Rimo Massif

The Rimo massif is a group of remote peaks located in the Karakoram range, with Rimo I standing as the highest and most prominent summit. Situated in the Karakoram's easternmost subrange, the Rimo Muztagh, the massif is flanked by the massive Rimo Glacier to the west and north, and the high-altitude Depsang Plains to the east.

The name "Rimo" translates to "striped mountain" in the local language, referring to the striking patterns of snow and dark rock layers running across the steep slopes. Rimo I lies approximately 20 km northeast of the snout of the Siachen Glacier, placing it near one of the most strategically sensitive regions in South Asia.

Because of its highly remote location and geopolitical sensitivity, climbing expeditions to Rimo I have been few and far between. The peak is known for extreme wind storms, sub-zero cold, and severe avalanche hazards along its steep walls.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 7,385 m (24,229 ft)
  • Prominence: 1,438 m (4,718 ft)
  • Coordinates: 35°21′12″N 77°22′05″E
  • Climbing Difficulty: Grade VI (Extreme / Alpine)
  • First Ascent: July 28, 1988
  • Expedition Team: Indo-Japanese Joint Team
