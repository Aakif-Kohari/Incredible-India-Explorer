Asian Paints — India's Paint and Design Brand

Har Ghar Kuch Kehta Hai — Every Home Says Something

Founded in a rented Mumbai garage in 1942 by four childhood friends, Asian Paints grew from a wartime import-substitute venture into India's largest paint company by 1967 — a position it has never lost. Today it operates in 14 countries with 26 manufacturing facilities, having evolved from a paint-can business into a full home-decor and interior-solutions company.

🎨 Origin & Founding

Asian Paints was founded on 1 February 1942 in a small rented garage in Gaiwadi, Girgaon, Mumbai, by four childhood friends: Champaklal H. Choksey, Chimanlal N. Choksi, Suryakant C. Dani, and Arvind R. Vakil. The venture, first registered as "The Asian Oil & Paint Company" — a name reportedly picked at random from a telephone directory — began as a partnership firm, mixing paint largely by hand.

Its timing was fortunate: World War II and the 1942 Quit India Movement led the British colonial government to place a temporary ban on paint imports, leaving only foreign majors and Shalimar Paints in the Indian market. The founders seized the opening, and by 1945 the firm had grown enough to convert into a private limited company. By 1967, just 25 years after starting in that garage, Asian Paints had become India's largest paints manufacturer.

A Founder's Quiet Exit

In 1997, on his deathbed, Champaklal Choksey sold nearly all his remaining shares in the company he had co-founded 55 years earlier — a striking, unsentimental close to the founding story of a company that would go on to generate over ₹36,000 crore in annual revenue.

🪣 Product Categories

Decorative Paints

The company's founding business — interior and exterior wall paints, distempers, emulsions, and premium textured finishes like Royale Play.

Industrial Coatings & Chemicals

Protective and industrial finishing products for automotive, marine, and general industrial applications.

Waterproofing & Adhesives

A growing category of home-improvement products addressing damp-proofing and construction-grade bonding needs.

Home Décor & Interior Solutions

Wallpapers, modular kitchens, bath fittings, and — following the 2025 acquisition of White Teak — premium decorative lighting.

🖌️ Colour & Design Campaigns

Asian Paints' advertising history moved through several distinct eras. From the 1950s to the 1970s, its mascot Gattu — an impish boy created by legendary cartoonist R. K. Laxman — appeared in print ads under the line "Any surface that needs painting needs Asian Paints," becoming a beloved fixture of Indian advertising folklore, much like Amul's mascot.

In the early 2000s, the brand shifted from character-led recall to emotional storytelling with "Har Ghar Kuch Kehta Hai" ("Every Home Says Something"), created by Piyush Pandey at Ogilvy & Mather. The campaign reframed paint from a functional purchase to a personal, emotional expression of identity — and evolved into a TV series featuring celebrity homes, later continuing as "Where the Heart Is."

Colour as Thought Leadership

Beyond advertising, Asian Paints publishes ColourNext, an annual colour and design-trend forecast, positioning the brand as a design authority rather than just a manufacturer. Recent campaigns like "Mera Wala Mood" use face-scanning technology to match a viewer's expression to a personalised colour and home film — extending the "Har Ghar Kuch Kehta Hai" idea into the digital age.

📈 Indian Market Growth

Asian Paints established its first overseas subsidiary in 1978 and converted to a full public company (as Asian Paints Ltd.) by 1973, later listing on the BSE and NSE and joining both the SENSEX and NIFTY 50. Within India, the brand's distribution reach is vast: over 1.69 lakh retail touch-points and more than 70,000 active dealers, supported by 26 manufacturing facilities across 14 countries and products reaching over 60 markets worldwide. As of November 2024, Asian Paints commanded roughly 59% market share of India's decorative paints industry, maintaining the leadership position it first won back in 1967 — now against competitors including Berger Paints and Kansai Nerolac.
