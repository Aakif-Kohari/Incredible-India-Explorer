Fossil forest locations

The Geological Survey of India protects several National Geological Monuments for petrified wood. Other well-studied floras sit in Gondwana coalfields, Rajmahal intertrappean beds, and Deccan volcanic sediments.

Akal Wood Fossil Park, Rajasthan

A 21-hectare GSI monument near Jaisalmer (notified 1977). Lower Jurassic (~180 Ma) tree trunks lie in the Lathi Formation. The park is now managed with Desert National Park.

Tiruvakkarai Fossil Wood Park, Tamil Nadu

Villupuram district. Notified 1951. About 100 hectares of Cuddalore Sandstone hold horizontal silicified trunks, some tens of metres long, dated to the Miocene–Pliocene (~20 Ma).

Sathanur Fossil Wood Park, Tamil Nadu

Perambalur district. A Late Cretaceous gymnosperm trunk about 18 m long (Sathanur kalmaram), documented by GSI geologist M. S. Krishnan. Nearby villages hold similar logs.

Ghughua Fossil Park, Madhya Pradesh

Dindori district, on the Maikal range. A 1983 national park preserving Late Cretaceous plant fossils in Deccan intertrappean beds — trunks, fruits, seeds, and leaf impressions.

Rajmahal Hills, Jharkhand

Intertrappean beds of the Rajmahal Formation (Jurassic–Cretaceous) near Mandro, Sahibganj, yield the classic Ptilophyllum flora of cycad-like and conifer plants between lava flows.

Gondwana coalfields, Damodar valley

Permian Barakar and Raniganj measures (Jharkhand–West Bengal) are not standing petrified groves, but they lock the Glossopteris swamp forests that became India’s Gondwana coal.

Palaeobotany

Plant fossils

  • Petrified wood — silica replaced the original tissue, keeping growth rings and cell walls. Akal, Tiruvakkarai, Sathanur, and Ghughua are dominated by silicified trunks (conifers, palms, and dicots depending on the site).
  • Glossopteris — tongue-shaped Gondwana leaves, the index plant of Permian coal forests in the Damodar and other peninsular basins.
  • Ptilophyllum flora — Bennettitalean and cycad-like fronds of the Rajmahal intertrappeans, with conifers in the same beds.
  • Deccan intertrappean plants — palms, dicot woods, fruits, and seeds at Ghughua, recording vegetation that grew between lava flows near the end of the Cretaceous.
  • Tiruvakkarai woods — often assigned to gymnosperm and angiosperm form-genera (including Mesembrioxylon); trunks usually lack branches and leaves because they were flood-transported before burial.
Chronology

Geological age

  1. Permian (~299–252 Ma) — Gondwana coal measures; Glossopteris forests.
  2. Early Jurassic (~180 Ma) — Akal petrified wood, Lathi Formation, western Rajasthan.
  3. Jurassic–Cretaceous — Rajmahal traps and intertrappean plant beds of eastern India.
  4. Late Cretaceous (~100–66 Ma) — Sathanur gymnosperm wood; Ghughua Deccan intertrappean flora.
  5. Miocene–Pliocene (~20 Ma) — Tiruvakkarai trunks in Cuddalore Sandstone.
Palaeoecology

Ancient environments

Gondwana swamps

Permian peat-forming forests grew on a cooler southern supercontinent. Their leaves and wood became the coal seams of eastern and central India after burial and heating.

Jurassic coastal woods

Akal’s trees grew in a warm, humid setting near a seaway. Rapid burial and silica-rich groundwater turned trunks to stone; the same ground is now Thar Desert.

Volcanic pauses

Rajmahal and Deccan intertrappean plants colonised soils and lakes between lava flows — short windows of forest in an otherwise volcanic landscape.

Cenozoic rivers

Tiruvakkarai woods were carried by floods into coastal sandstone. The Cuddalore beds record tropical river and delta plains, not trees rooted where they now lie.

Why it matters

Scientific significance

Glossopteris on India, Africa, Australia, Antarctica, and South America was early evidence that those lands once formed Gondwana. Petrified parks show how climate and latitude have shifted: Jurassic humid forest at Akal versus today’s arid Jaisalmer. Deccan intertrappean plants date vegetation around the end-Cretaceous eruptions. GSI monuments at Tiruvakkarai, Sathanur, and Akal keep the wood in place for stratigraphy, palaeobotany, and public geoheritage.
