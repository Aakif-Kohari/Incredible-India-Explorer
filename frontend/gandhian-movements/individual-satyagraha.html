October 1940 – December 1941

The Individual Satyagraha

A moral protest affirming the right to free speech against India's forced participation in World War II, carefully designed by Mahatma Gandhi to avoid mass violence while maintaining national dignity.

The Global Crisis

World War II & The Congress Position

In September 1939, the British Viceroy, Lord Linlithgow, declared India a belligerent in World War II without consulting Indian leaders. The Indian National Congress resigned from provincial ministries in protest. While they sympathized with the fight against Fascism, they demanded a promise of complete independence as a condition for active cooperation.

When the British responded with the unsatisfactory "August Offer" (1940), Congress turned to Gandhi. Recognizing the volatile situation, Gandhi devised the Individual Satyagraha. He wanted to register a strong moral protest without launching a mass movement that might turn violent or completely paralyze the British war effort against Nazi Germany.

⚖️ Symbolic Resistance vs. Mass Agitation

Unlike the Non-Cooperation (1920) or Civil Disobedience (1930) movements which sought to halt all government machinery through mass participation, this campaign was strictly limited. Only individuals personally chosen by Gandhi were permitted to break the law by giving anti-war speeches.

The Chosen Few

The First Individual Satyagrahis

Gandhi personally vetted each participant to ensure absolute commitment to non-violence (Ahimsa) and constructive work.

1st: Acharya Vinoba Bhave

Chosen for his immaculate record of constructive work and spiritual discipline. He inaugurated the movement on October 17, 1940, at Paunar, delivering an anti-war speech before his peaceful arrest.

2nd: Jawaharlal Nehru

Selected as the second Satyagrahi to represent the political and international consciousness of the Congress. He was arrested on October 31, 1940, and sentenced to severe imprisonment.

3rd: Brahma Datt

An inmate of Gandhi's ashram, chosen as the third Satyagrahi. As the movement progressed, over 25,000 local Congress leaders, mayors, and elected officials courted arrest with the slogan "Delhi Chalo!".

Delhi Chalo

Mapping the Movement

The campaign began in the villages of central India, with Satyagrahis instructed to march towards Delhi while giving anti-war speeches until they were arrested.

📍 Mapped Locations (Text Alternative)

  • Paunar Ashram, Wardha: The starting point where Vinoba Bhave delivered the first anti-war speech.
  • Allahabad / Gorakhpur Region: Where Jawaharlal Nehru was apprehended while preparing for his Satyagraha.
  • Delhi: The symbolic destination of the marchers, giving birth to the movement's popular name, the "Delhi Chalo" movement.

Timeline of the Campaign

August 1940

The August Offer

Viceroy Linlithgow makes an offer that falls far short of independence, prompting Congress to reject it and ask Gandhi to lead a protest.

17 October 1940

Inauguration at Paunar

Vinoba Bhave initiates the Individual Satyagraha by addressing a village gathering against participating in the war effort.

January 1941

Expansion of the Campaign

The campaign expands into its multi-phase format. District and local Congress committees send their members to court arrest. Over 25,000 individuals are imprisoned across India.

December 1941

Release of Prisoners

With Japan entering the war (Pearl Harbor) and approaching Indian borders, the British release the Satyagrahis to seek Indian cooperation. Gandhi officially suspends the movement.

The Precursor

Transition to Quit India

While the Individual Satyagraha did not paralyze the British administration or secure immediate independence, it achieved its primary objectives. It successfully demonstrated India's refusal to be taken for granted, upheld the civil liberty of free speech, and reinvigorated the Congress ranks. Most importantly, it served as a disciplined rehearsal, preparing the political atmosphere for the massive, explosive Quit India Movement that would erupt just months later in August 1942.
