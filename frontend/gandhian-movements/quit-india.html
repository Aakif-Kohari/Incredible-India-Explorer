August 1942 · Gowalia Tank Maidan

The Quit India Movement

The definitive mass civil disobedience campaign launched by Mahatma Gandhi, demanding an immediate end to British rule and sparking unprecedented nationwide resistance and parallel governments.

The Turning Point

The Cripps Mission & "Do or Die"

By early 1942, World War II was dangerously close to India, with the Japanese rapidly advancing through Southeast Asia. To secure full Indian cooperation, the British sent the Cripps Mission to negotiate. However, the mission failed completely as it only offered dominion status after the war, not immediate independence.

Frustrated by British obstinacy, the Congress Working Committee met at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay on August 8, 1942. Here, the historic Quit India Resolution was passed. Mahatma Gandhi delivered a passionate speech, famously commanding the nation to "Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro)—meaning either free India or die in the attempt, abandoning the earlier strategy of limited individual protest.

🚩 The Leadership Vacuum

In the early hours of August 9, before the movement could be formally launched, the British executed "Operation Zero Hour," arresting Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, and the entire Congress leadership. Rather than crushing the movement, this sparked leaderless, spontaneous, and ferocious mass protests across the country.

The Shadows of Freedom

Underground Networks & Revolutionary Youth

With the senior leaders imprisoned, a new generation of fierce, underground revolutionary leaders took control, keeping the movement alive through sabotage and covert communications.

Aruna Asaf Ali

Dubbed the "Heroine of the 1942 movement," she bravely hoisted the Indian National Congress flag at the Gowalia Tank Maidan on August 9 amidst severe police tear-gassing.

Usha Mehta's Secret Radio

At just 22 years old, she organized the "Secret Congress Radio," broadcasting covert messages, news of protests, and patriotic songs from shifting locations in Bombay to evade British detection.

Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)

Escaped from the Hazaribagh Central Jail and formed the "Azad Dasta" (Free Band) in the Nepal terrains, conducting guerrilla warfare and sabotage against British infrastructure.

Parallel Governments

Interactive Map of the Resistance

Explore the regions where British authority completely collapsed and independent, parallel governments (Jatiya/Prati Sarkars) were successfully established by the people.

📍 Mapped Locations (Text Alternative)

  • Bombay (Gowalia Tank): The birthplace of the Quit India resolution and site of the first flag hoisting.
  • Ballia, Uttar Pradesh: Chittu Pandey established the first parallel government here, famously releasing arrested Congress leaders from jail.
  • Tamluk, Midnapore (Bengal): The "Jatiya Sarkar" was formed here. 73-year-old Matangini Hazra was martyred here while leading a procession holding the tricolor.
  • Satara, Maharashtra: The "Prati Sarkar" led by Y.B. Chavan and Nana Patil was the longest-lasting parallel government, running people's courts and organizing guerrilla bands (Toofan Sena).

Timeline of the August Kranti

March 1942

Failure of the Cripps Mission

British proposals for post-war dominion status are rejected. Gandhi famously calls it a "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank."

8 August 1942

The Quit India Resolution

Passed at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay, demanding immediate British withdrawal and initiating the "Do or Die" mandate.

9 August 1942

Operation Zero Hour

British authorities preemptively arrest all major national leaders, inciting leaderless mass uprisings, strikes, and the sabotage of railways across India.

1942 – 1944

Formation of Parallel Governments

British administration collapses in specific pockets. Independent people's governments run successfully in Ballia, Tamluk, and Satara.

May 1944

Gandhi's Release

Gandhi is released from the Aga Khan Palace due to failing health, marking the gradual cooling down of the intense underground resistance.

The Final Nail

Repression & Historical Impact

The British response to the Quit India Movement was ruthless. Over 100,000 people were arrested, massive fines were levied on entire villages, and police firing resulted in thousands of deaths. Despite this brutal repression, the movement proved to the British Crown that the cost of holding India by force had become insurmountable. The sheer scale of spontaneous mass participation—cutting across students, women, peasants, and workers—made it abundantly clear that complete independence was no longer a negotiation, but an inevitability.
