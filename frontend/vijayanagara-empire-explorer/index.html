Vijayanagara Empire

The Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE) was a South Indian empire renowned for its military strength, efficient administration, magnificent architecture at its capital Hampi, and a vibrant cultural legacy that shaped the Deccan for over three centuries.

Era: 1336–1646 CE Capital: Hampi Founder: Harihara I

Historical Overview

The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 CE by Harihara I and his brother Bukka Raya I of the Sangama Dynasty. It emerged as a formidable Hindu empire in the Deccan region, dedicated to preserving Hindu culture and traditions against the northward expansion of Islamic sultanates.

At its peak, the empire controlled almost all of Southern India, from the Krishna River in the north to the southern tip of the peninsula. The empire reached its zenith under the rule of Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529), often regarded as one of India's greatest monarchs. During his reign, the empire saw unprecedented military success, economic prosperity, and a golden age of art and literature.

The empire was known for its efficient administration, with a well-organized bureaucracy, a sophisticated revenue system, and extensive trade networks that connected it to Persia, Arabia, and Southeast Asia. The capital city, Vijayanagara (modern-day Hampi), was one of the largest and wealthiest cities in the world, described by Portuguese travelers as a place of immense grandeur.

The empire's decline began after the death of Krishnadevaraya, culminating in the catastrophic Battle of Talikota (1565), where a coalition of Deccan Sultanates defeated the Vijayanagara forces. The capital was sacked and left in ruins, marking the end of the empire's golden age, though a diminished Aravidu dynasty continued until 1646.

Timeline of the Empire

  1. 1336

    Founding of the Empire

    Harihara I and Bukka Raya I establish the city of Vijayanagara on the banks of the Tungabhadra River, founding the Sangama Dynasty.

  2. 1346

    Conquest of Madurai

    The Vijayanagara armies defeat the Madurai Sultanate, extending the empire's influence deep into the Tamil country.

  3. 1377

    Bukka Raya I's Reign

    Under Bukka Raya, the empire consolidates its power and extends its territory, establishing a strong administrative framework.

  4. 1485

    Saluva Dynasty

    Saluva Narasimha Deva Raya usurps the throne, ending the Sangama Dynasty and beginning the short-lived Saluva Dynasty.

  5. 1509

    Reign of Krishnadevaraya

    Krishnadevaraya ascends the throne, ushering in the golden age of the Vijayanagara Empire.

  6. 1529

    Death of Krishnadevaraya

    The greatest ruler of the empire passes away, leading to a period of gradual decline under his successors.

  7. 1565

    Battle of Talikota

    A coalition of Deccan Sultanates decisively defeats the Vijayanagara army. The capital Hampi is sacked and abandoned.

  8. 1646

    End of the Empire

    The Aravidu Dynasty, the last ruling house of Vijayanagara, comes to an end, marking the final dissolution of the empire.

Major Rulers

The empire was ruled by four main dynasties: Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva, and Aravidu.

Harihara I

r. 1336–1356 CE

Sangama Dynasty. The founder of the Vijayanagara Empire. He established the capital at Hampi and laid the foundation for a powerful Hindu kingdom.

Bukka Raya I

r. 1356–1377 CE

Sangama Dynasty. Brother of Harihara I. He expanded the empire's territories and organized its administrative structure.

Deva Raya II

r. 1424–1446 CE

Sangama Dynasty. Considered the greatest ruler of the Sangama line. He was a patron of arts and literature and successfully campaigned against the Bahmani Sultanate.

Krishnadevaraya

r. 1509–1529 CE

Tuluva Dynasty. The empire's greatest monarch. A brilliant military commander, patron of arts, and poet. His reign marked the golden age of Vijayanagara.

The Capital: Hampi

Hampi (historically known as Vijayanagara) was the magnificent capital of the empire, located on the banks of the Tungabhadra River in present-day Karnataka. At its peak, it was one of the largest and wealthiest cities in the world.

Portuguese travelers like Domingo Paes and Fernao Nuniz left vivid accounts of the city's grandeur. Paes wrote that the city was "the best-provided city in the world" with a population estimated between 500,000 and 1 million. The city was a major center of trade, dealing in diamonds, rubies, spices, and silk.

The ruins of Hampi are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, spread over 4,187 hectares. Key monuments include the Virupaksha Temple, the Vittala Temple with its iconic stone chariot, the Lotus Mahal, and the massive Elephant Stables.

Architecture

Vijayanagara architecture is a synthesis of earlier South Indian styles, characterized by grand temple complexes, massive gateways (Gopurams), and intricately carved pillars.

Vijayanagara Style

A blend of Chola, Pandya, and Hoysala styles, featuring massive enclosures, towering gopurams, and pillared halls (mandapas).

Virupaksha Temple

One of the oldest functioning temples in India, dedicated to Lord Shiva. Its 50-meter tall gopuram dominates the Hampi skyline.

Vittala Temple

Famous for its stone chariot and musical pillars that produce different notes when tapped. A masterpiece of Vijayanagara sculpture.

Secular Architecture

The empire also built palaces, audience halls, and water structures like the Lotus Mahal and Queen's Bath, showcasing Indo-Islamic influences.

Military Strength

The Vijayanagara Empire maintained a formidable military machine that was key to its survival against the Deccan Sultanates. The army was well-organized and included infantry, cavalry, war elephants, and a navy.

Army Size: According to Portuguese accounts, Krishnadevaraya's army consisted of over 700,000 infantry, 32,000 cavalry, 550 elephants, and a navy. The empire also employed Muslim horsemen (kshatriyas) for their superior cavalry skills.

Fortifications: The empire built massive fortifications, particularly around its capital Hampi. The city was surrounded by seven lines of fortifications, making it nearly impregnable to direct assault.

Artillery: The Vijayanagara armies used gunpowder and artillery, though they were often outmatched by the Sultanates in this regard. The Battle of Talikota was partly lost due to the superior artillery of the Deccan Sultanates.

Cultural Legacy

The Vijayanagara period was a golden age for South Indian culture, art, and literature. The empire was a great patron of Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, and Tamil literature.

Krishnadevaraya himself was a accomplished poet, composing the Telugu epic Amuktamalyada. The court was home to the Ashtadiggajas (eight celebrated Telugu poets), including the legendary Tenali Ramakrishna.

The empire also saw significant advancements in music, dance, and sculpture. The Carnatic music tradition flourished, and the bronze sculptures of this period are considered masterpieces of South Indian art.

The empire's legacy continues to influence South Indian culture, politics, and identity. The ruins of Hampi stand as a testament to the grandeur of this lost empire, attracting scholars and tourists from around the world.
