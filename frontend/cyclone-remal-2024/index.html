Field Profile · Bay of Bengal, Pre-Monsoon 2024

Cyclone Remal

The first named storm of the 2024 North Indian Ocean season made landfall between Sagar Island, West Bengal and Khepupara, Bangladesh on the night of 26 May — a slow, five-hour crossing that flooded the Sundarbans, cut power across the delta, and tested a warning system built after far deadlier storms.

01 / FORMATION & TRAJECTORY

Born from a warm, restless bay

A low-pressure area gathered over the southwest and west-central Bay of Bengal around 21–22 May, feeding on sea-surface temperatures running 1–2°C above normal. The India Meteorological Department flagged the genesis zone roughly five days out — an unusually long lead time that shaped everything that followed. The system concentrated into a depression on 24 May, was named Remal (Arabic for "sand," contributed by Oman) on becoming a cyclonic storm on 25 May, and reached severe cyclonic storm intensity as it curved north toward the West Bengal–Bangladesh coastline.

India's coast was spared a direct hit by a narrow margin: the eye came ashore closer to Bangladesh's Mongla, roughly 70 km from Canning — the West Bengal town where the word "cyclone" itself was coined by Henry Piddington in 1848.

02 / CYCLONE TIMELINE

Five days, hour by hour

From first warning to weakening into a depression over India's northeast, the storm's life cycle ran just under a week — but the most consequential window was the roughly five-hour landfall process on the night of 26 May.

23 MAY

Fishermen ordered off the water

IMD warns of a strengthening low and advises fishing fleets in the central and north Bay of Bengal to return to shore before the 23rd; a storm warning cage is mounted at Pamban port, Tamil Nadu.

24 MAY

Depression forms, name confirmed

The system concentrates into a depression over the central Bay of Bengal. IMD forecasts landfall between Digha (West Bengal) and Khulna (Bangladesh) around 26–27 May; a red alert is issued for South and North 24 Parganas.

25 MAY

Becomes Cyclonic Storm Remal, then intensifies

Named on entering the east-central bay, Remal strengthens through the day into a severe cyclonic storm. Its approach coincides with voting in the sixth phase of India's Lok Sabha elections in parts of West Bengal.

26 MAY, DAY

Coasts empty out

Kolkata airport suspends flights, suburban trains are curtailed, and NDRF teams take up positions in North and South 24 Parganas. PM Narendra Modi chairs a review meeting on preparedness.

26 MAY, ~20:30–21:00 IST

Landfall begins

The eye starts crossing the coast between Sagar Island and Khepupara, near Mongla. Sustained winds of 110–120 km/h gust to 135 km/h. The process continues for roughly five hours.

27 MAY, 01:30–05:00 IST

Storm centre over the coastal delta

Kolkata endures heavy rain, gusty wind, and waterlogging through the night. Around 260 mm of rain falls on the city in 24 hours; over 50 flights and hundreds of trees go down.

27 MAY, MORNING

Weakens to cyclonic storm

IMD downgrades Remal as it moves north-northeast into Bangladesh, sustained winds easing to 80–90 km/h. Attention shifts to relief, embankment checks, and restoring power.

27–28 MAY

Remnant rains soak the northeast

The weakening system tracks into Meghalaya, Assam, Tripura, Mizoram and Manipur as a depression, triggering landslides and flooding that add heavily to the eventual toll before it dissipates.

03 / WEST BENGAL IMPACT

The delta took the direct hit

South and North 24 Parganas — the low-lying, densely populated districts fringing the Sundarbans — bore the brunt on the Indian side. Kolkata, Howrah, Purba Medinipur and Jhargram absorbed heavy rain and wind under an orange alert.

04 / COASTAL FLOODING & STORM SURGE

Overtopped, not breached — mostly

Forecast surge heights of 3–4 metres along the Bangladesh coast, and a somewhat lower push into the Indian Sundarbans, combined with a landfall timed close to high tide. Embankment authorities in West Bengal ran emergency patchwork in weak spots in the days before landfall — a decision credited with preventing a larger catastrophe.

Overtopping, not collapse

No major embankment breach was recorded in the Indian Sundarbans, but river water overtopped weakened embankments at roughly 67 points in Gosaba alone, with further overtopping reported from Sagar, Namkhana, Patharpratima, Kultali, Sandeshkhali and Hingalgunge.

Saline water, standing for days

Tidal surge water lingered in low-lying delta areas for over 36 hours in places, pushing saline water into freshwater ponds and paddy fields — a slower-moving hazard to farming and drinking water that outlasted the wind by days.

Bangladesh side, harder hit

Roughly 61 km of embankments across Khulna, Satkhira and Bagerhat districts were damaged, flooding shrimp enclosures, cropland and villages — a reminder that the same surge behaves very differently a few tens of kilometres either side of the border.

Kolkata's inland flooding

Away from the coast, Kolkata's own drainage struggled with the rainfall rate rather than surge — parts of south Kolkata saw sustained waterlogging into the following evening as pumping stations worked through the backlog.

06 / EVACUATION OPERATIONS

Moving people before the water did

Evacuation on both sides of the border ran into the millions — one of the largest coordinated pre-landfall movements in the region since the 2020 Cyclone Amphan response.

07 / INFRASTRUCTURE & ENVIRONMENTAL EFFECTS

What broke, and what bent

The damage pattern was typical of a moderately intense but slow-moving landfall: extensive but largely repairable harm to power and telecom networks, sharper localised destruction of informal housing, and a slower environmental toll on the mangrove delta.

Power & telecom

Around 10,000 telecom towers across the region lost power or were damaged, cutting mobile service for millions. In India, roughly 2,500 houses were destroyed and 27,000 more damaged; over 1,200 power poles came down in West Bengal alone, and electrocution from downed lines caused most of the state's storm deaths.

Housing

Damage concentrated in kutcha (mud and thatch) housing on exposed islands — Mousuni, Ghoramara and similar Sundarbans settlements saw roofs torn off and walls collapse, while pucca (concrete) construction in Kolkata and inland towns mostly held.

Mangroves & the Sundarbans

The Sundarbans mangrove belt — shared by India and Bangladesh and a natural buffer against surge — absorbed much of the storm's energy, but prolonged saline inundation threatens freshwater ponds, aquaculture and the forest's own regeneration in the months that follow.

Agriculture & livelihoods

Standing summer crops, betel vine and shrimp enclosures were damaged across the coastal belt; a post-storm rapid needs assessment found roughly 30% of affected West Bengal households had less than a week's food stock, straining public distribution and Anganwadi supply chains.

08 / EARLY-WARNING SYSTEMS

Five days of notice, and what it bought

Remal is a case study in how far India's cyclone warning apparatus has come since the 1999 Odisha super cyclone, which killed close to 10,000 people with far less lead time.

Genesis-stage forecasting

IMD's Regional Specialised Meteorological Centre flagged the eventual genesis area roughly five days before the depression formed, and issued a pre-genesis track and intensity outlook on 23 May — a full day before Remal existed as a depression.

Colour-coded district alerts

Red alerts (South and North 24 Parganas) and orange alerts (Kolkata, Howrah, Purba Medinipur, Jhargram, Nadia) gave district administrations a graduated basis for evacuation and school-closure decisions days ahead of landfall.

Cyclone shelter network

Purpose-built multi-storey cyclone shelters along the West Bengal and Bangladesh coasts — expanded steadily since the 1990s — absorbed a large share of the roughly 10 million people evacuated on both sides of the border.

Where warnings still meet reluctance

Officials in Bangladesh noted that residents reluctant to leave livestock and homes often waited until the last minute — a persistent gap between forecast accuracy and last-mile compliance that no radar improvement fully closes.
