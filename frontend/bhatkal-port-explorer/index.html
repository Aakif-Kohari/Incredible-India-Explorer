Bhatkal

The Vijayanagara Empire's Coastal Gateway

Explore History

Historical Background

Bhatkal is a historic port town located in the Uttara Kannada district of Karnataka. Mentioned in ancient texts as 'Nitryas' or 'Batthecala', it served as a major secondary port for the Vijayanagara Empire during the 14th to 16th centuries. Because the primary port of Goa was often under Bahmani or Portuguese control, Bhatkal became the vital lifeline for the empire's international trade.

The town was famous for its wealthy merchant class and its strategic location, allowing the empire to import much-needed warhorses from Arabia and Persia while exporting rich Deccan textiles and spices to the wider Indian Ocean world.

Trade Routes

Bhatkal maintained extensive trade links with the Persian Gulf, particularly Hormuz, and the Red Sea. Portuguese traveler Duarte Barbosa noted in the 16th century that Bhatkal was a bustling hub where merchants from across the Middle East gathered to trade horses, copper, and pearls for Indian cotton, sugar, and spices.

Cultural Significance

Bhatkal is renowned for its unique architectural and cultural heritage. The town houses several historic Jain basadis (temples) that date back to the Vijayanagara period, showcasing exquisite Dravidian architecture. It is also home to the Murudeshwar Temple nearby, which boasts one of the tallest Shiva statues in the world. The local cuisine, known as 'Bhatkali', reflects a rich blend of Arab and coastal Indian culinary traditions.

Timeline

1300s

Bhatkal rises as a key port for the Vijayanagara Empire.

1500s

Portuguese attack and plunder the port multiple times to disrupt Vijayanagara trade.

1565

After the Battle of Talikota, the port's importance gradually declines.

1700s

Comes under the control of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan of Mysore.
