Mawryngkhang Trek The legendary bamboo trail of Meghalaya

Often dubbed the scariest trek in Meghalaya, the Mawryngkhang trail is an engineering marvel crafted entirely from bamboo by the locals of Wahkhen. It skirts along the edge of massive rock formations over sheer drops, offering unparalleled thrills and stunning valley views.

Discover the Mawryngkhang Trek

Located deep in the East Khasi Hills of Meghalaya, the journey begins at Wahkhen village, a place famous for its traditional music and pristine environment. The trek is a testament to the indigenous Khasi ingenuity, utilizing bamboo tied with cane ropes to construct paths along vertical cliff faces.

Geological Rock Formations

The defining feature of this trek is U Mawryngkhang—the "King of Stones". According to local folklore, this massive monolithic rock formation stands as a victorious warrior after a legendary battle of stones. The sheer verticality of the rock face plunging into the Umrew River valley makes it a dramatic geological spectacle.

💡 Quick Trek Tips

  • Starting Point: Wahkhen Village (approx. 50km from Shillong). The road to Wahkhen is scenic but requires a sturdy vehicle.
  • Entry Fee: A nominal entry fee is collected by the village council to maintain the bamboo structures.
  • Best Time: October to May. Avoid heavy monsoons as the bamboo becomes incredibly slippery and the river swells dangerously.
  • Acrophobia: Not recommended for individuals with a severe fear of heights, as much of the trail involves walking on suspended bamboo ladders.

Forest Ecosystem & The Bamboo Trail

The trail offers an immersive experience through Meghalaya's lush tropical ecosystem, crossing rivers, natural pools, and ascending sheer cliff walls.

🚨 Essential Safety Guidelines

While the bamboo path is remarkably sturdy and well-maintained by the local Khasi community, the trek's nature requires absolute caution:

1. Grip and Footwear

Walking on rounded bamboo stems can be tricky. Shoes with excellent rubber grip are required. Avoid flat-soled sneakers or sandals.

2. Single-File Movement

Many sections of the bamboo skywalk are narrow. Maintain a single-file line, allow returning trekkers to pass at wider junctions, and avoid crowding on suspended platforms.

3. Weather Awareness

Do not attempt the trek during severe downpours. The bamboo becomes extremely slick, and sudden flash floods in the Umrew river below can be dangerous.

⚠️ Leave No Trace

Wahkhen is one of the cleanest villages in India. The community is deeply connected to their ecosystem. Do not litter, play loud music, or pluck vegetation. Respect the sacred nature of the U Mawryngkhang rock.
