📜 Foundation

History of the Lalit Kala Akademi

The Lalit Kala Akademi (National Academy of Art) is India's national academy of fine arts, established in New Delhi on 5 August 1954 by the Government of India. It was inaugurated by the then Minister of Education, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, and registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 on 11 March 1957.

It is the youngest of the three national Akademies founded in independent India — after the Sangeet Natak Akademi (performing arts, 1952) and the Sahitya Akademi (letters, 1954) — and is dedicated solely to the visual arts. The Akademi was born of Jawaharlal Nehru's vision of a national cultural identity, with the state supporting not just art but artists themselves.

"The Akademi must work to preserve the glorious traditions of the past and enrich them by the work of our modern artists. It must also seek to improve standards and refine public taste." — Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, Inaugural Address, 1954

Mandate & Objectives

  • To encourage and promote study and research in the creative arts such as painting, sculpture, and graphics.
  • To promote and propagate an understanding of Indian art both within the country and abroad.
  • To coordinate the activities of State Lalit Kala Akademies and regional art organisations.
  • To encourage cooperation among artists and the development of art associations.
  • To organise exhibitions, seminars, conferences, and exchange programmes between schools of art.

Headquarters & Regional Centres

The Akademi is headquartered at Rabindra Bhawan, Ferozeshah Road, New Delhi, and is funded by the Union Ministry of Culture. It operates regional centres at Bhubaneswar, Chennai, Garhi (Delhi), Kolkata, Lucknow, and Shimla, along with a North-East Regional Centre at Agartala.

⏳ Milestones

Journey Through the Decades

1954

Foundation

Lalit Kala Akademi is established in New Delhi on August 5th, inaugurated by Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.

1955

First National Exhibition

The inaugural National Exhibition of Art (NEA) is held, and the first National Awards are conferred.

1956

Introduction of Gold Plaque

The Akademi Gold Plaque is introduced to recognise exceptional merit in the National Exhibition.

1968

Triennale India

The first Triennale India, a major international exhibition of contemporary art, is organised in New Delhi.

1990s

Regional Expansion

Establishment of regional centres in Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, and Bhubaneswar to decentralise art promotion.

2024

70th Anniversary

The Akademi celebrates 70 years of promoting Indian visual arts with special retrospectives and the 64th National Exhibition.

📋 Recognition

Eligibility & Honour Structure

The Lalit Kala Akademi confers several levels of national recognition in the visual arts. Each honour recognises distinguished, sustained, and original achievement.

🏅 National Award

Conferred annually on artists whose works are selected in the National Exhibition of Art. Winners receive a plaque, a shawl, and a cash purse of ₹2 lakh. In the 64th National Exhibition (2024–25), 20 artists were honoured, with the awards conferred by the President of India.

💎 Akademi Fellowship (Ratna)

The highest honour in the fine arts conferred in India. Given to eminent artists for their lifetime achievements in the visual arts, it carries a citation, a plaque, and a purse. The first Fellow, elected in 1955, was painter Jamini Roy.

🎨 Eligible Art Forms

Original works of painting, sculpture, printmaking (graphic art), and ceramics — together with applied arts, and, in recent editions, newer media such as installation and digital art.

🇮🇳 Who Can Participate

Entries to the National Exhibition of Art are open to artists across India of any age or background. Submissions must be original works, not previously shown in the NEA.

🖌️ Core Discipline

Painting — The Heart of the Akademi

Painting has been at the core of the Lalit Kala Akademi since its founding. The Akademi's constitution originally defined the "creative arts" as painting, sculpture, and graphics, and painters have formed the largest body of its Fellows and National Award winners.

Modern Masters

The Akademi's first Fellow, Jamini Roy (1955), drew on Bengali folk traditions, while Nandalal Bose (1956) brought the Bengal School's lyrical line into the national canon.

Diverse Traditions

From Bengal School wash paintings and Santiniketan naturalism to the Baroda and Bombay modernists, the Akademi's exhibitions celebrate the extraordinary diversity of Indian painting.

🗿 Core Discipline

Sculpture — Three Dimensions of Expression

Sculpture has enjoyed equal standing in the Akademi's vision, honouring India's deep sculptural heritage — from temple bronzes and stone carving to modern experiments in metal, wood, and mixed media.

Pioneering Sculptors

Fellows include D. P. Roy Choudhury (1962), Sankho Chaudhuri, and Prodosh Dasgupta (1982), founders of the sculptors' collective.

🖨️ Core Discipline

Printmaking — The Graphic Arts

Printmaking, or graphic art, was one of the three "creative arts" named in the Akademi's original 1954 constitution. The Akademi championed the medium through dedicated print biennales.

Master Printmakers

Jeram Patel (Fellow 2007) revolutionised Indian printmaking. K. G. Subramanyan (Fellow 1985) worked across painting, printmaking, and glass.

🏺 Core Discipline

Ceramics — Earth, Fire & Form

Ceramics has grown into a celebrated category of the Lalit Kala Akademi's honours, recognising artists who transform earth and fire into sculptural and functional art.

Studio Pottery & Sculpture

Ceramic artists explore glazes, slips, and firing techniques to create both wheel-thrown vessels and hand-built sculptural forms.

🎯 Process

Selection Process

1

Call for Entries

The Akademi issues an annual call inviting original works of painting, sculpture, printmaking, ceramics, and other visual media.

2

Open Submission

Artists submit their works along with documentation. Submissions must be original and not previously exhibited in the NEA.

3

Screening Juries

Regional and zonal juries of eminent artists, critics, and curators screen entries and forward shortlisted works.

4

National Selection Committee

A national jury selects the works for display and shortlists the most outstanding exhibits for awards.

5

General Council Approval

Recommendations are placed before the Akademi's General Council and Executive Board for final approval.

6

Award Ceremony

National Awards are conferred at an official ceremony, traditionally graced by the President of India.

🏆 Legends

Notable Awardees & Fellows

Since 1955, the Akademi has honoured the giants of modern Indian art. Filter by discipline to explore their contributions.

🖌️

Jamini Roy

Fellow · 1955 · Painting

The first Fellow of the Lalit Kala Akademi, whose folk-inspired, flat-lined paintings of Bengal became a cornerstone of Indian modernism.

🎨

Nandalal Bose

Fellow · 1956 · Painting

The master of Santiniketan, whose works adorned the original handwritten manuscript of the Indian Constitution.

🗿

D. P. Roy Choudhury

Fellow · 1962 · Sculpture

Pioneering sculptor and first principal of Government College of Art, Kolkata, known for his monumental public sculptures.

🖌️

M. F. Husain

Fellow · 1978 · Painting

The most celebrated painter of modern India, whose dynamic, colour-drenched canvases defined the Bombay Progressive movement.

🖨️

K. G. Subramanyan

Fellow · 1985 · Painting & Printmaking

Artist, theorist, and educator whose experiments in painting, printmaking, and glass inspired generations.

🗿

Himmat Shah

Fellow · 2021 · Sculpture

A sculptor and printmaker celebrated for his contemplative terracotta heads and decades-long creative practice.
