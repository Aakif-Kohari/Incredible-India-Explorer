Ellora Caves Explorer

History

Excavated in overlapping phases between roughly the 6th and 10th centuries CE, Ellora's caves record three faiths carved into the same cliff face.

Buddhist Caves (1–12)

The earliest group at Ellora, built as monasteries and prayer halls for Buddhist monks.

Hindu Caves (13–29)

The largest and most elaborately carved group, dedicated mainly to Shiva and Vishnu.

Jain Caves (30–34)

The final phase at Ellora, marked by intricate, smaller-scale shrines to the Tirthankaras.

Kailasa Temple

Cave 16 — the largest monolithic rock-cut structure in the world, carved top-down from a single basalt outcrop.

Rock-Cut Engineering

How ancient artisans carved entire temples out of solid rock — with no room for error.

UNESCO Heritage

Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, recognized for its architectural achievement and its testimony to religious tolerance in ancient India.
