Language Origins & Script

An Austroasiatic Linguistic Island

Unlike the surrounding Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman languages, the Khasi language belongs to the Mon-Khmer branch of the Austroasiatic language family. Spoken primarily by the Khasi people of Meghalaya, it forms a crucial part of the state's cultural identity and is spoken by over 1.6 million people.

The Writing System: Historically an oral language, early attempts were made to write Khasi using the Bengali-Assamese script. However, in 1841, Welsh missionary Thomas Jones introduced the Roman (Latin) script, which remains standard today. The Khasi alphabet consists of 23 letters: it excludes the letters c, f, q, v, x, z but uniquely features the special characters ï (for the /i/ sound) and ñ (for the palatal nasal /ɲ/).

📚 Khasi at a Glance

  • Classification: Austroasiatic > Mon-Khmer > Khasi-Khmuic > Khasi.
  • Script: Roman script (introduced by Thomas Jones in 1841).
  • Native Name: Ka Ktien Khasi (meaning "The Khasi Language").
  • Alphabet Size: 23 letters (retains ï and ñ).
  • Region: Meghalaya (Khasi & Jaintia Hills districts), parts of Assam and Bangladesh.
  • Status: Associate official language in Meghalaya; recognized as a major literary language.
Common Greeting

How to Greet Someone in Khasi

Khublei
Pronunciation: [khoob-lay]

"Khublei" is the most important and versatile word in Khasi. It serves as a greeting (Hello / Welcome), a departure phrase (Goodbye), and an expression of gratitude (Thank you). Its literal meaning translates closely to "God bless you."

Cultural Heritage

Matrilineal Customs & The Living Root Bridges

The Khasi society is traditionally matrilineal, meaning children take the mother's clan name (*Kur*), and the youngest daughter (*Ka Khadduh*) is designated as the custodian of the ancestral property. This social setup ensures women hold a highly respected place in family and societal structures.

The Khasi Hills are also famous for the ingenious **Living Root Bridges** (*Jingkieng Jri*), which are hand-woven from the aerial roots of the *Ficus elastica* (rubber fig tree). These biological structures grow stronger over time and can withstand the heavy monsoonal rainfall of the region, which is home to Mawsynram and Cherrapunji, the wettest places on Earth.

Traditional religious beliefs are centered on a supreme deity and are practiced alongside Christianity. Festivals such as **Shad Suk Mynsiem** (the dance of peaceful hearts) and **Nongkrem Dance** celebrate the harvest season and showcase the rich, colorful traditional attire and music of the Khasi people. Additionally, their deep respect for nature is embodied in **Law Kyntang** (sacred groves), which are protected forests where cutting trees or plucking plants is strictly prohibited.
