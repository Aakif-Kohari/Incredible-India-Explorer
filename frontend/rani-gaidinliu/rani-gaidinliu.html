Rani Gaidinliu

Explore the life and movement of Rani Gaidinliu, a remarkable Naga leader who resisted British rule and advocated for self-rule in Northeast India.

01

A Voice for Self-Rule

Rani Gaidinliu became one of the prominent figures of resistance against British rule in Northeast India.

Born in 1915 in the present-day Tamenglong region of Manipur, Gaidinliu joined the Heraka religious and social movement while she was still young.

Her movement became connected with opposition to British authority and a vision of preserving Naga cultural identity and self-rule.

02

Her Story

Follow the key stages of Rani Gaidinliu's journey.

01 / 05

Early Life

Rani Gaidinliu was born on 26 January 1915 in Nungkao village, in the present-day Tamenglong area of Manipur.

02 / 05

Joining the Heraka Movement

As a young girl, Gaidinliu joined the Heraka movement led by her cousin Haipou Jadonang, which sought to strengthen indigenous religious and cultural traditions.

03 / 05

Resistance to British Rule

After Jadonang's execution in 1931, Gaidinliu continued the movement and became an important symbol of resistance against British authority.

04 / 05

Years in Prison

Gaidinliu was arrested by the British authorities in 1932 and spent many years in prison. Her imprisonment did not erase her importance as a symbol of resistance.

05 / 05

A Lasting Legacy

After independence, Gaidinliu continued working for the welfare and cultural identity of her people. She was honoured with the Padma Bhushan in 1982.
