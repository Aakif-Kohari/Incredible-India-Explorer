Trek Overview

Sikkim's Wildest Walk to Kangchenjunga

The Green Lake Trek is the classic North Sikkim approach to the world's third-highest mountain, following the Zemu valley deep inside Khangchendzonga National Park.

📍 Trek Location

The trek lies in North Sikkim, India, within Khangchendzonga National Park — a UNESCO Mixed World Heritage Site on the border region with Tibet. Green Lake sits in the upper Zemu valley directly below the massive north face of Mount Kangchenjunga (8,586 m).

🚩 Starting Point

The trailhead is Lachen (2,750 m), a traditional Lepcha village around 120 km — roughly a six-hour mountain drive — from Gangtok. The walk proper begins at Zema, the confluence of the Zemu Chu and Thangu Chu rivers just beyond Lachen.

🏔️ Mountain Landscape

The route climbs from subtropical river valleys into alpine wilderness ringed by giants: Kangchenjunga, Siniolchu (6,888 m) — often called the most beautiful peak in the Himalaya — Simvo, Kirat Chuli, Nepal Peak, and Tent Peak surround the green lake amphitheatre.

📜 Expedition Heritage

In the 1920s and 1930s this was the staging ground for early Kangchenjunga attempts by pioneers such as George Mallory's reconnaissance era teams and Paul Bauer's German expeditions, who based themselves near Green Lake and the Zemu Glacier.

Permits & Responsible Travel

Plan With Safety and Respect

🛂 Restricted Area Permits

The entire route lies in a protected area near the international border. Indian trekkers need an Inner Line Permit plus a trekking permit issued through the Sikkim Tourism Department, and foreign nationals require additional Protected Area Permit clearances arranged in advance through a registered local operator.

🥾 Fitness & Acclimatisation

This is a demanding camping expedition with multiple days above 4,000 m. Build stamina for 5–7 hour walking days over boulders and moraine, schedule acclimatisation halts at Yabuk, and descend immediately if altitude sickness symptoms appear.

🌿 Leave No Trace

Khangchendzonga National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Carry all waste back out, avoid damaging fragile alpine meadows, use established campsites, and respect the sacred status the mountains hold for local Lepcha and Bhutia communities.

🧭 Go With Local Experts

The trail is indistinct in places and landslides frequently reshape sections. Travel with certified guides, registered porters, and current weather advisories — independent trekking is not permitted on this route.

⚠️ High-Altitude Safety Notice

The Green Lake Trek is a serious remote-area expedition, not a weekend hike. This page is for educational exploration only — always rely on official permit guidance, trained trek leaders, and up-to-date local conditions before planning any real-world trekking decisions.
