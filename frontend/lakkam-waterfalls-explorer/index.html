📖 Introduction

The High-Range Cascade

A Hidden Gem of Munnar

Lakkam Waterfalls is located near Marayoor in the Idukki district of Kerala, approximately 35 km from the popular hill station of Munnar. Situated within the Anaimalai Hills — the highest range in South India — it represents one of Kerala's most elevated and pristine waterfalls.

The falls lie within the Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary buffer zone, surrounded by unique shola-grassland ecosystems and tea estates that define the Munnar high-range landscape.

Water Source & Geography

The waterfall is fed by the Pambar River, which originates from the Anamudi peak (2,695 m) — South India's highest mountain. The river flows through the Eravikulam National Park before cascading at Lakkam.

Geologically, the falls occur where the river crosses a laterite escarpment formed by the tectonic uplift of the Western Ghats, creating this spectacular multi-tiered cascade.

  • Type: Multi-tiered cascade

  • River: Pambar (tributary of Amaravati)
  • Rock type: Laterite and charnockite

🌧️ Seasonal Variation

When to Visit

Peak

Monsoon (Jun–Sep)

Waterfall at its most magnificent with full volume. Shola forests lush and green. Frequent mist creates dramatic atmosphere. Trails can be slippery.

Best

Post-Monsoon (Oct–Feb)

Ideal visiting period with healthy water flow and pleasant weather. Clear skies offer excellent views of surrounding peaks. Perfect for trekking.

Low

Summer (Mar–May)

Water flow reduces to a gentle stream. Some upper tiers may dry. Hotter days though cooler than plains. Tea estates remain green.

🌳 Surrounding Ecosystem

High-Range Biodiversity

🌲

Shola Forests

Stunted evergreen forests in valleys above 1,800m. Unique to the Western Ghats with 80% endemic plant species.

🐐

Nilgiri Tahr

Endangered mountain ungulate found only in Western Ghats. Eravikulam NP has the world's largest population.

🦅

High-Altitude Birds

Endemic species including Nilgiri pipit, Nilgiri laughingthrush, and black-and-orange flycatcher.

🌸

Neelakurinji

The legendary Strobilanthes kunthiana blooms once every 12 years, covering hillsides in violet-blue.

🐾

Rare Mammals

Brown palm civet, Nilgiri marten, lion-tailed macaque, and Indian giant squirrel inhabit the shola forests.

🌾

Montane Grasslands

Rolling grasslands on ridges alternate with shola valleys. Critical for watershed conservation.

🛖 Cultural Heritage

The Muthuvan Tribe & Sacred Groves

Guardians of the Forest

The Muthuvan tribe has inhabited the high ranges of the Anaimalai Hills for centuries. Their traditional knowledge of the shola forests, medicinal plants, and sustainable hunting-gathering practices is deeply intertwined with the landscape surrounding Lakkam.

Sacred Groves (Kavu)

Scattered throughout the Marayoor region are 'Kavus' or sacred groves. These are patches of pristine forest protected by local communities for religious and ecological reasons, serving as vital micro-reservoirs of biodiversity and water sources for streams like the Pambar.

❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to swim at Lakkam Waterfalls?▼

Swimming is generally discouraged due to strong undercurrents, slippery rocks, and sudden changes in water volume. It is best enjoyed from the designated viewing platforms.

Do I need a permit to visit?▼

For regular day visits to the main viewing area, a standard entry ticket (₹25) is sufficient. However, if you plan to trek deep into the Chinnar buffer zone or Eravikulam, special forest department permits are required.

Can I visit Lakkam Waterfalls in a regular car?▼

Yes, the road up to the parking area is motorable and accessible by standard sedans. However, the last few kilometers involve ghat roads with hairpin bends, so an experienced driver is recommended.
