Language Origins & Script

A Bodo-Garo Legacy

Garo, known to its own speakers as A·chikku (the language of the A·chik, or "hill people"), belongs to the Bodo-Garo branch of the Tibeto-Burman family within the wider Sino-Tibetan language group. It is the principal language of the Garo people, concentrated in the Garo Hills districts of Meghalaya, with speaker communities also in Assam, Nagaland, Tripura, and northern Bangladesh.

Writing System: Garo is written today primarily in a Roman-based orthography, introduced through 19th-century Baptist missionary work and standardized alongside early Garo-language Bible translations. Some materials have historically also used the Bengali-Assamese script, and a modern indigenous script effort known as A-Chik Tokbirim exists, though the Roman alphabet remains the language's dominant and most widely used writing system.

📚 Garo at a Glance

  • Classification: Sino-Tibetan > Tibeto-Burman > Sal > Bodo-Garo > Garo.
  • Script: Roman script (Latin alphabet), primary; historical Bengali-Assamese use.
  • Native Name: A·chikku; speakers self-identify as A·chik or Mande.
  • Region: Garo Hills, Meghalaya, and neighbouring Assam, Nagaland, Tripura, and Bangladesh.
  • Status: Associate official language in Meghalaya; UNESCO-listed as vulnerable.
Common Greeting

How to Greet Someone in Garo

Namengama?
Pronunciation: [na-meng-ga-ma]

"Namengama?" is the everyday Garo greeting, literally asking "How are you?" — the standard way Garo speakers open a conversation. The common reply is "Namengaba" ("I am fine").

Cultural Heritage

Garo Culture & the Garo Hills

The Garo people are traditionally organized around a matrilineal system, in which property and clan lineage pass through the mother's line, and the youngest daughter (nokna) typically inherits the ancestral home. This social structure remains a distinctive feature of Garo society in the Garo Hills of Meghalaya today.

The Garo Hills' best-known cultural celebration is Wangala, the "100 Drums Festival," a post-harvest thanksgiving dedicated to Saljong, the Garo sun god associated with fertility and a good harvest. Traditionally rooted in the indigenous Songsarek faith, Garo cultural life today blends these older customs with a predominantly Christian religious identity, all expressed and passed down through the A·chikku language.
