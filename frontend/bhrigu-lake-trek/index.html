Bhrigu Lake Trek

Trek profile

Everything you need at a glance

Bhrigu Lake sits high above Manali in Kullu district. The trek is known for quickly changing landscapes: forest and pasture give way to broad alpine meadows and high Himalayan terrain.

📍

Location

East of Rohtang Pass, Kullu district, Himachal Pradesh, India.

⛰️

Difficulty

Moderate for acclimatised trekkers; high altitude makes pacing important.

🥾

Distance

Roughly 20–26 km of trail walking depending on the chosen itinerary and access point.

🗓️

Duration

Usually planned as a 3–4 day trek, with the exact schedule set by the operator and conditions.

☀️

Best season

Late spring through autumn, commonly May to October when the route is open and conditions allow.

🚐

Starting point

Gulaba is a common starting point; Vashisht-side approaches are also associated with the trek.

Trail plan

Route & itinerary

The route below follows the common Manali–Gulaba approach. Treat timings as planning guidance rather than a fixed schedule: weather, trail conditions, fitness and local restrictions can change the day.

1

Day 1 · Gulaba → Rauli Kholi

Forest to open meadow

Start from the Gulaba area and climb through mixed forest and grazing country. The trail opens into broad meadows with views toward the surrounding Pir Panjal peaks.

  • Acclimatisation
  • Meadows
  • Mountain views
2

Day 2 · Rauli Kholi → Bhrigu Lake → Rauli Kholi

Summit lake day

Climb above the meadow line toward Bhrigu Lake. The final approach becomes more exposed and the terrain can hold snow or ice depending on the season. Return to camp after spending time at the lake.

  • High altitude
  • Alpine terrain
  • Lake viewpoint
3

Day 3 · Rauli Kholi → Gulaba

Descent to the roadhead

Retrace the trail through meadows and forest to Gulaba. Descending slowly helps protect knees and reduces slips on loose or wet ground.

  • Descent
  • Forest trail
  • Road access
4

Optional buffer day

Weather & acclimatisation reserve

A fourth day can be useful for weather delays, a slower itinerary, acclimatisation or a longer sightseeing plan around Manali.

  • Flexible
  • Weather buffer
  • Safer pacing

Lake information

A sacred alpine lake above Manali

Bhrigu Lake, also written Brighu Lake, is a high-altitude lake in the Kullu district. Official tourism information places it at about 4,235 metres, while trek resources often round the elevation to around 4,300 metres.

The lake is associated with Maharishi Bhrigu in local tradition. Its position east of Rohtang Pass and its surrounding high mountain landscape make it a prominent destination in the Manali trekking circuit.

Route highlights

What makes the trek memorable

01

Rapid landscape change

Move from forest and lower grazing areas into open alpine meadows in a relatively short climb.

02

Himalayan panoramas

Higher sections open views toward the surrounding ranges and the mountain terrain above Manali.

03

Seasonal snow

Snow patches can remain on the upper route even as lower meadows become green, changing route conditions.

04

Sacred landscape

The lake carries cultural and spiritual significance through traditions connected with Maharishi Bhrigu.

05

Manali gateway

The trek combines naturally with a Manali stay, making the region a practical base for pre- and post-trek travel.

06

Wide open meadows

Large meadow sections distinguish Bhrigu from routes that spend more of their time in narrow valleys or moraine.

Responsible trekking

Plan for altitude, weather and local rules

Acclimatise

Do not rush the climb. Allow time at altitude and pay attention to headache, nausea, unusual fatigue or breathlessness.

Check access

Opening periods, road access, permits and local restrictions can change. Confirm current rules before leaving Manali.

Carry layers

Upper-route weather can change quickly. Carry waterproof outerwear, warm layers, sun protection and suitable footwear.

Leave no trace

Carry waste back, avoid damaging alpine vegetation and keep the lake and surrounding meadows clean.

Use local expertise

A local guide or registered trekking operator can provide current information about weather, trail conditions and access.

Respect sacred places

Treat the lake and nearby cultural sites respectfully, and follow guidance from local communities.
