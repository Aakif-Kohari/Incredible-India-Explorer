MTR The Journey of a Bengaluru Tiffin Room into a Global Food Brand

Born as a modest coffee club near Bangalore's Lalbagh Road, Mavalli Tiffin Rooms (MTR) grew from a beloved South Indian restaurant into MTR Foods — one of India's most trusted packaged-food brands, born out of crisis and carried across the world by four generations of the Maiya family.

🏛️ Est. 1924, Bengaluru 🍚 Birthplace of Rava Idli 📦 Pioneer of Packaged South Indian Mixes 🌍 Exported to the Gulf, US & UK

Brand Identity & Name

"MTR" stands for Mavalli Tiffin Rooms, named after the Mavalli locality near Lalbagh, Bengaluru, where the restaurant found its lasting home in 1960.

Brand Origin & History

1924: Three Brothers, One Coffee Club

In 1924, three brothers — Parameshwara Maiya, Ganappayya Maiya and Yagnanarayana Maiya — who had left their village near Udupi, Karnataka in search of opportunity, opened a modest eatery called the Brahmin Coffee Club near Lalbagh Fort Road in Bangalore, serving filter coffee, idlis and dosas.

The restaurant built a reputation for hygienic, high-quality vegetarian food and a strict cash-only, no-favouritism policy — VIPs and ordinary diners waited in the very same line. In 1960, the restaurant relocated and was renamed Mavalli Tiffin Rooms, the name it carries to this day.

"MTR's aim has always been to serve good food consistently — food that makes customers return."
— As recounted by the Maiya family, MTR's founding lineage
Founders of MTR

The Maiya Brothers

  • Parameshwara Maiya: Co-founder, established the original Brahmin Coffee Club in 1924.
  • Ganappayya Maiya: Co-founder, ran the restaurant alongside his brothers in its early years.
  • Yagnanarayana Maiya: Joined the business in 1929 and led its culinary innovations, including the invention of rava idli.
  • Sadananda Maiya: Third-generation family member who founded MTR Foods Ltd out of the 1975 restaurant crisis.
  • Harishchandra Maiya: Took over the restaurant business in 1994, continuing the family's restaurant legacy.

Udupi Culinary Roots

The Maiya brothers carried the vegetarian, temple-influenced cooking traditions of coastal Udupi, Karnataka into Bangalore, shaping MTR's filter coffee, idli and dosa recipes from day one.

1940s
🍚

Rava Idli: Born from Necessity

When Japan's occupation of Burma cut off South India's rice supply during World War II, Yagnanarayana Maiya substituted semolina for rice — creating the now-beloved rava idli almost by accident.

1950s
🍰

Chandrahara, the Sunday Special

A French-puff-pastry-inspired layered dessert, served only on Sundays and later renamed "Chandrahara" after a nearby cinema's film, became a beloved MTR trademark treat.

Packaging Evolution

From Crisis Pivot to Retort-Pouch Technology

MTR's packaged-food story began almost by accident. When the 1975 Emergency-era Food Control Act made restaurant operations unprofitable, the Maiya family began selling spice blends and roasted flour mixes in simple packets — both to save employee jobs and to keep the MTR name alive.

What started as packets of sambar and rasam powder grew into a full packaged range. By 1998, MTR Foods had adopted retort-pouch technology — sealed, shelf-stable pouches that let ready-to-eat curries and rice travel across India and overseas without refrigeration, alongside the launch of MTR's Softy ice cream cone.

Brand Expansion Timeline

From Bengaluru to the World

  • 1983: MTR Foods Ltd. formally established by Sadananda Maiya.
  • 1984: Expansion into Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh.
  • 1994: Business reorganised for nationwide distribution across India.
  • 2007: Acquired by Norway's Orkla Group for approximately $80 million.
  • Today: MTR products and restaurants reach Singapore, Dubai, Kuala Lumpur, London, Seattle and beyond, with MTR Foods the first Indian company to earn HACCP food-safety certification.
