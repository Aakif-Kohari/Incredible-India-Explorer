👑 Founder of the Mauryan Empire

Chandragupta Maurya

The visionary sovereign who overthrown Nanda tyranny, repelled Seleucid imperialists, and forged ancient India's first pan-subcontinental empire (c. 321 – 297 BCE).

At a Glance: Key Historical Metadata

Full Name: Chandragupta Maurya (Sandrokottos / Androcottus in Greek texts)
Dynasty: Maurya Dynasty (Founder)
Historical Era: Ancient India / Iron Age (4th Century BCE)
Predecessor: Dhana Nanda (Nanda Empire)
Successor: Bindusara (Son)
Primary Seat: Pataliputra (Modern Patna, Bihar)

Early Life & Mentorship of Chanakya

Historically verified details about Chandragupta's origins are sparse, though epigraphic and literary records agree on his rise to prominence in the mid-4th century BCE. Ancient traditions differ on his exact background:

  • Buddhist Traditions (Mahavamsa & Digha Nikaya): Describe Chandragupta as belonging to the noble Moriya clan of Pipphalivana.
  • Greco-Roman Accounts (Justin & Plutarch): State that he was a man of humble origin who met Alexander the Great in the Punjab during Alexander's Indian campaign around 326 BCE.
  • Later Sanskrit Traditions (Mudrarakshasa & Puranas): Associate him with the court of the Nanda dynasty at Pataliputra before his exile.

Central to Chandragupta's rise was his partnership with Chanakya (Kautilya / Vishnugupta), a legendary Brahmin scholar from Taxila. Recognizing Chandragupta's leadership potential, Chanakya mentored him in military strategy, statecraft, and political philosophy—principles later compiled into the masterwork Arthashastra.

📜 Distinction of Historical Sources

Historians carefully separate contemporary evidence—such as Megasthenes' fragments and Greco-Roman records—from medieval plays like the 6th-century Mudrarakshasa. While later literature provides rich narratives, established history rests on archaeological edicts and contemporary classical accounts.

Rise of the Empire & Territorial Extent

Prior to Chandragupta's rise, Northern India was divided into independent Mahajanapadas, dominated in the east by the wealthy but oppressive Nanda Empire under King Dhana Nanda, while the northwest suffered instability following Alexander's departure.

Around 321 BCE, Chandragupta and Chanakya built a coalition of regional forces, overthrew Dhana Nanda, and captured Pataliputra. Over the next decade, Chandragupta expanded Mauryan sovereignty westwards across the Indo-Gangetic plains, Punjab, Malwa, and Gujarat.

Administration & Military Organization

🏛️

Central Governance

The Emperor held supreme executive authority, supported by a Mantriparishad (Council of Ministers). Administrative departments were headed by specialized Adhyakshas overseeing trade, agriculture, metallurgy, and mints.

🗺️

Provincial System

The vast realm was partitioned into major provinces governed by viceroys (often royal princes called Kumara). Major provincial seats included Taxila (Northwest), Ujjain (West), Tosali (East), and Suvarnagiri (South).

⚔️

Military Power

According to Megasthenes and Pliny, Chandragupta maintained a massive standing army: 600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry, and 9,000 war elephants, governed by a war board of six committees.

🕵️

Espionage & Intelligence

As documented in Chanakya's Arthashastra, an extensive network of secret agents (Gudhavapurushas) provided internal security, monitored provincial officers, and gathered public feedback.

Greek Connections & The Seleucid Treaty (305 BCE)

Following the breakup of Alexander's empire, Seleucus I Nicator (founder of the Seleucid Empire) sought to reconquer Alexander's eastern Indian satrapies around 305 BCE. Chandragupta met Seleucus in battle near the Indus River.

The war concluded with a historic peace treaty that redefined ancient Eurasian diplomacy:

  • Territorial Cession: Seleucus ceded the satrapies of Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Balochistan), Paropamisadae (Kabul), and Aria (Herat) to the Mauryan Empire.
  • War Elephants: In exchange, Chandragupta gifted 500 trained war elephants to Seleucus, which played a decisive role at the Battle of Ipsus (301 BCE) in Phrygia.
  • Diplomatic Alliance (*Epigamia*): The treaty recognized a matrimonial alliance between the royal houses and established permanent diplomatic channels.
  • Megasthenes' Embassy: Seleucus dispatched Megasthenes as ambassador to Pataliputra. His travelogue, Indika, remains a primary classical description of Mauryan social life, city fortifications, and governance.

Later Life, Jain Tradition & Historical Legacy

According to prominent Jain historical traditions (recorded in texts like the Rajavali-kathe and epigraphs at Shravanabelagola):

In his later years, facing a severe 12-year famine in South Bihar, Chandragupta abdicated the throne around 298–297 BCE in favor of his son Bindusara. He embraced asceticism under the guidance of Jain Acharya Bhadrabahu.

Chandragupta traveled south to Shravanabelagola in modern-day Karnataka, where he spent his final days practicing Sallekhana (voluntary holy fasting unto death) on the hill now named Chandragiri in his honor.

🏛️ Unifier of Subcontinental History

Chandragupta Maurya is remembered as the first sovereign to unify nearly the entire Indian subcontinent under a central administration, establishing trade networks, security, and administrative models that endured for centuries under Ashoka and later dynasties.
