India Open Badminton

From the inaugural 2008 edition in Hyderabad to the modern Super 750 spectacle — journey through India's premier international badminton tournament. Discover champions, iconic matches, and the shuttlecock legends who made history on Indian courts.

Tournament History

A Rising Star on the World Tour

The India Open badminton tournament was first held in 2008 in Hyderabad as part of the BWF Grand Prix circuit. Organised by the Badminton Association of India (BAI), it quickly grew from modest beginnings into a premier international event.

By 2011 the event was elevated to BWF Super Series level. In 2018, it was placed in the prestigious Super 750 tier — the second-highest category — guaranteeing elite fields and significant world ranking points. The 2020 edition was cancelled due to COVID-19, but the tournament returned strongly in 2022 with packed arenas and record-breaking viewership.

🏆 At a Glance

  • 2008 — Inaugural edition held in Hyderabad
  • BWF Super 750 — Second-highest tier since 2018
  • 5 Disciplines — MS, WS, MD, WD, XD
  • Prize Money — US$1,350,000 (2024)
  • Indian Champions — Saina Nehwal, Srikanth Kidambi, Lakshya Sen, P.V. Sindhu
  • Primary Venue — Siri Fort Sports Complex, New Delhi
Trophy

The India Open Trophy

The India Open trophy is awarded to the winners of each discipline at India's premier BWF Super 750 event.

🏆

Trophy Overview

The India Open trophy represents the pinnacle of achievement in Indian badminton. Winners across all five disciplines — Men's Singles, Women's Singles, Men's Doubles, Women's Doubles, and Mixed Doubles — receive the trophy along with prize money and world ranking points.

  • Organised by — Badminton Association of India (BAI)
  • BWF Tier — Super 750 (since 2018)
  • Prize Money — US$1,350,000 (2024)
  • World Ranking Points — 11,000 (winner)
🏸

Tournament Format

The India Open follows BWF regulations with a 32-player/draw format in each discipline. The tournament spans five days — qualifying rounds on Wednesday, main draw from Thursday, and finals on Sunday.

  • Draw Size — 32 players per discipline
  • Qualifying — Wednesday (day before main draw)
  • Main Draw — Thursday to Saturday
  • Finals — Sunday (5 matches)
🥇

Prize Money Distribution

Prize money is distributed across all rounds, with the highest share going to winners and runners-up in each discipline.

  • Winner — US$96,750
  • Runner-up — US$46,200
  • Semi-finalists — US$18,900 each
  • Quarter-finalists — US$7,800 each

Major Matches

Unforgettable Showdowns

Iconic finals and epic encounters that defined the India Open legacy.

2015 — Men's Singles Final

Srikanth vs. Son Wan Ho

Kidambi Srikanth defeated Korea's Son Wan Ho 21-18, 21-14, becoming the first Indian man to win the India Open title.

2017 — Women's Singles Final

Sindhu vs. Sun Yu

P.V. Sindhu defeated China's Sun Yu 21-11, 21-14 in a dominant display, delighting the home crowd.

2018 — Men's Singles Final

Srikanth vs. Chou Tien Chen

Kidambi Srikanth defended his title with a thrilling 21-18, 19-21, 21-16 win over Chinese Taipei's Chou Tien Chen.

2019 — Women's Singles Final

Tai Tzu-ying vs. Carolina Marin

An epic clash between two of the sport's greatest artists. Tai Tzu-ying prevailed 21-15, 21-16 against Olympic champion Carolina Marin.

2022 — Men's Singles Final

Lakshya Sen vs. Viktor Axelsen

India's rising star Lakshya Sen defeated world No. 1 Viktor Axelsen 21-16, 17-21, 22-20 in a three-set thriller.

2024 — Men's Doubles Final

Satwik-Chirag vs. Aaron-Wooi Yik

Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty claimed the India Open title at the Indira Gandhi Indoor Stadium.

Olympic Connections

From Indian Courts to the Five Rings

India Open champions who went on to represent India at the Olympic Games.

2012

Saina Nehwal — London Bronze

Saina Nehwal won bronze in Women's Singles — India's first Olympic badminton medal. A three-time India Open champion.

2016

P.V. Sindhu — Rio Silver

P.V. Sindhu won silver in Women's Singles at Rio, becoming only the second Indian woman to win an individual Olympic medal.

2020

Sindhu — Tokyo Bronze

P.V. Sindhu added a bronze at Tokyo, becoming the first Indian shuttlecock player to win two individual Olympic medals.

2022

Thomas Cup Glory

Lakshya Sen, Satwik-Chirag and the Indian team won the Thomas Cup for the first time — a historic moment for Indian badminton.

2024

Paris Campaign

Lakshya Sen reached the semi-finals in Paris, while Satwik-Chirag made the quarter-finals — India's best-ever Olympic badminton campaign.

Milestones

Defining Moments in Indian Badminton

Key events that shaped the sport's trajectory in India.

2008

Inaugural Edition

The first India Open is held in Hyderabad. Lee Hyun-il (Korea) wins men's singles; Jiang Yanjiao (China) wins women's singles.

2011

Super Series Elevation

The India Open is elevated to BWF Super Series status, attracting top-32 ranked players globally.

2012

Saina's Olympic Bronze

Saina Nehwal wins bronze at London Olympics, sparking a nationwide badminton boom.

2015

Srikanth Breaks Through

Kidambi Srikanth becomes the first Indian man to win the India Open.

2017

Indian Double Delight

Srikanth (MS) and Sindhu (WS) both win the India Open — first time two Indian singles players win in the same year.

2018

Super 750 Status

The India Open is placed in the BWF World Tour Super 750 category, the second-highest tier globally.

2022

Thomas Cup & Comeback

India wins the Thomas Cup for the first time. Lakshya Sen stuns Viktor Axelsen at the India Open.

2024

New Venue, New Era

The tournament moves to the 14,000-capacity Indira Gandhi Indoor Stadium. Prize money reaches $1.35M.
