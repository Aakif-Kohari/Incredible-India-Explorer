Maritime History

Built in 1650 AD by Shivappa Nayaka of Keladi, Bekal Fort has a rich maritime history intertwined with the complex coastal politics of the region. It served as a vital stronghold for defending the coastal towns and maritime trade routes of the Malabar Coast.

Over the centuries, the fort saw the rise and fall of several powers, shifting from the Nayakas to the legendary Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan of Mysore, before finally falling into the hands of the British East India Company after the fall of Tipu Sultan in 1799.

Coastal Defence

Unlike many Indian forts that served as administrative centers or palaces, Bekal Fort was constructed purely for defensive purposes. Its location and structure were designed to give its occupants an unparalleled advantage against naval attacks from the Arabian Sea.

The fort features strategically placed gun holes built at different elevations to defend against enemies attacking from naval ships, small boats, and ground infantry alike.

Architecture

The fort is built from locally sourced laterite stone and rises up to 130 feet above sea level. Key architectural features include:

  • Keyhole Shape: The fort is distinctly shaped like a giant keyhole, seamlessly integrating with the rugged coastal terrain.
  • Water Magazine: An underground water tunnel/magazine designed to keep ammunition safe from enemy fire.
  • Laterite Ramparts: Massive walls featuring intricate pathways that allow soldiers to patrol the entire perimeter of the fort.
  • Hanuman Temple and Mosque: Signifying the varied cultural history, an ancient Hanuman temple and an old mosque built by Tipu Sultan exist at the entrance.

Watch Towers

The towering Observation Tower, built by Tipu Sultan, is one of the most iconic features of the fort. Located at the center of the fort, this massive cylindrical watchtower provides a breathtaking 360-degree view of the coastline and the Arabian Sea.

This tower allowed sentries to spot approaching enemy armadas from miles away, giving the garrison ample time to prepare their coastal defenses.

Film Shooting Locations

Bekal Fort gained massive mainstream popularity across India when it was featured in Mani Ratnam's acclaimed 1995 film Bombay.

The soulful song 'Tu Hi Re' (Uyire), composed by A.R. Rahman, was shot amidst the pouring rain against the spectacular backdrop of the fort's observation tower and the turbulent Arabian Sea, immortalizing Bekal Fort in Indian cinema.

Interesting Facts

  • No Palaces: The fort contains no palaces, mansions, or administrative buildings, emphasizing its purely military purpose.
  • Laterite Rock: The laterite stone used to build the fort appears golden brown during sunset, creating a mesmerizing glow.
  • Special Tourism Area: In 1992, the Government of India declared Bekal Fort as a special tourism area to promote its heritage.

Location & Map

Bekal Fort is located in the Kasaragod district of Kerala, roughly 16 kilometers south of Kasaragod town along the state's picturesque northern coastline.
