Taragarh Fort

History

Constructed in 1354 upon a steep hillside, Taragarh Fort (Star Fort) remains one of the most impressive structures in Bundi. Unlike many other forts in Rajasthan that were heavily influenced by Mughal architecture, Taragarh Fort represents a pure, unadulterated form of Rajput architectural style.

Over the centuries, the fort served as a major defensive bastion for the Chauhan dynasty. Although parts of it are in ruins today, its massive scale and steep incline continue to awe visitors, whispering tales of medieval chivalry and warfare.

Builder

The fort was constructed by Rao Bar Singh in 1354. Under his vision, the fort was designed not just as a royal residence but as an impenetrable military stronghold capable of withstanding prolonged sieges.

Architecture

The architecture of Taragarh Fort is characterized by its rugged charm and strategic layout. Key architectural features include:

  • Three Massive Gateways: The entrance is guarded by three monumental gateways: Lakshmi Pol, Phuta Darwaza, and Gagudi ki Phatak.
  • Chauhan Baori: A large stepwell with distinct Rajput carvings.
  • Bhim Burj: A massive battlement on which the famous cannon named Garbh Gunjam (Thunder from the Womb) was mounted.
  • Rani Mahal: The Queens' palace, known for its stained glass windows and murals (now mostly faded).

Water Reservoirs

One of the most remarkable engineering feats of Taragarh Fort is its extensive network of water reservoirs. Carved directly into the solid rock of the hillside, these reservoirs were designed to harvest and store rainwater.

During times of crisis or prolonged sieges, these reservoirs ensured that the garrison had an uninterrupted water supply, making the fort nearly self-sufficient and incredibly difficult to conquer by attrition.

Defence System

The fort's defense mechanisms were built taking full advantage of the treacherous local topography. The steep incline made it nearly impossible for enemy war elephants to breach the walls.

The walls are incredibly thick and crisscross the hillside. Along the ramparts, numerous bastions provided elevated vantage points. The famous Bhim Burj bastion was the centerpiece of this defensive network, housing the mighty Garbh Gunjam cannon.

Interesting Facts

  • Star Fort: When viewed from below, the fort's layout on the conical hill supposedly resembles a star, hence the name Taragarh.
  • Garbh Gunjam: The cannon mounted on Bhim Burj was so loud that the artillerymen had to jump into a nearby water tank upon firing it to prevent their eardrums from bursting.
  • Kipling's Inspiration: Writer Rudyard Kipling was so fascinated by Taragarh that he famously described it as the work of "goblins rather than men."
