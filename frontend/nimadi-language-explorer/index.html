Language Origins & Script

A Rajasthani-Branch Language of the Nimar

Nimadi (also spelled Nimari or Nemadi) is a Western Indo-Aryan language spoken by roughly 2.3 million people (2011 census), concentrated in the Nimar region of southwestern Madhya Pradesh — principally the districts of Khargone, Barwani, Khandwa, and Burhanpur. The Nimar region sits between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, straddling the fertile valleys of the Narmada and Tapti rivers.

Linguistically, Nimadi is classified within the Rajasthani group of Indo-Aryan languages. The pioneering linguist George Grierson, in his early-20th-century Linguistic Survey of India, described it as essentially a form of the Malvi dialect of Rajasthani, though with "marked peculiarities of its own" shaped by long contact with Gujarati, Bhili, and Khandeshi. Modern sociolinguistic surveys find it roughly 62–77% lexically similar to standard Hindi.

Writing System: Nimadi is written in the Devanagari script, the same script used for Hindi, though there is no single standardized Nimadi orthography — published materials generally adapt Devanagari conventions to the language's regional pronunciation and vocabulary.

📚 Nimadi at a Glance

  • Classification: Indo-European > Indo-Aryan > Western > Rajasthani > Nimadi.
  • Script: Devanagari.
  • Native Name: निमाड़ी (Nimāḍī); also called Nimari or Nemadi.
  • Region: Nimar (Narmada-Tapti valley), Madhya Pradesh.
  • Speakers: ~2.3 million (2011 Census of India); ISO 639-3: noe.
  • Status: Ethnologue "Vigorous" (EGIDS 6a) — actively used at home and in the community.
Common Greeting

How to Greet Someone in Nimadi

राम राम
Pronunciation: [raam raam]

"Ram Ram" is the everyday folk greeting used across the Nimar and wider Malwa-Rajasthani cultural belt, exchanged as a warm, informal "hello" between villagers and acquaintances — a regional custom that predates and runs alongside the more formal, pan-Indian "Namaste."

Folk & Cultural Heritage

Nimadi Folk Culture & the Narmada Valley

Nimadi's literary tradition grew primarily out of oral culture — lok gathas (folk epics) and geets (songs) passed down through community performance and storytelling, often drawing on local myths and the daily rhythms of agrarian life along the Narmada. A landmark written work is Ammar Bol, Mahadeo Prasad Chaturvedi's rendering of the Bhagavad Gita into Nimadi verse, considered the language's foundational epic. Poet Gaurishankar Sharma is remembered for verses evoking the spiritual presence of the Narmada River itself.

The region's cultural life is marked by celebrations such as the annual Nimar Utsav in Maheshwar, a multi-day festival of Nimadi songs, folk dance, and theatre, and the tribal Bhagoriya festival, a harvest-season celebration among Bhil communities that prominently features Nimadi songs and dance. In 2025, writer Jagdish Joshila received the Padma Shri for his contributions to Nimadi prose and literature, a marker of the language's ongoing cultural recognition.
