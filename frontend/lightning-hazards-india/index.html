Anatomy & Physics of a Lightning Discharge

How graupel-ice particle collisions inside convective thunderclouds generate millions of volts leading to explosive electric arcs.

⚡

Graupel & Ice Electrification

Collisions between falling soft hail and rising ice crystals strip electrons.

In freezing cloud zones (-10°C to -20°C), heavy graupel particles collide with ascending ice crystals in violent updrafts. The collisions transfer electrons, charging graupel negatively and ice crystals positively.

🌩️

Cloud-Ground Charge Dipole

Positive charges gather at cloud tops, negative charges at bases, inducing ground charge.

Updrafts carry positive ice crystals to the anvil, while negative graupel settles at cloud bases. This negative base layer induces a shadow of strong positive charge on the ground surface below.

📉

Stepped Leader & Upward Streamer

Ionized channels step downward from clouds to meet ground streamers.

When voltage exceeds air insulation (~3M volts/meter), a stepped leader of ionized air branches downward. As it nears the ground, positive upward streamers rise from tall objects to meet it.

🔥

Return Stroke Superheating

Surge current reaches 30,000 K—5x hotter than the surface of the Sun.

Connecting the circuit releases a massive current pulse (30,000–100,000 Amperes) surging upward to the cloud. Air channel superheating to 30,000 K causes explosive acoustic expansion (thunder).

⚠️

Ground Current & Step Potential

Over 50% of rural Indian casualties are caused by ground current spreading through soil.

When lightning strikes trees or soil, current radiates outward. The potential difference between a person's feet (step potential) forces deadly current through the body, causing mass human and livestock casualties.

Why India Experiences High Lightning Activity

Key meteorological factors driving intense pre-monsoon convective thunderstorms across India's plateau and hill regions.

🌡️

Pre-Monsoon Extreme Heat

April–June surface temperatures exceeding 40°C–45°C trigger severe CAPE thermal instability.

Intense solar radiation during pre-monsoon summer months heats dry land surfaces across central and eastern India, creating explosive Convective Available Potential Energy (CAPE) and powerful updrafts.

💨

Maritime vs. Continental Air Collision

Humid Bay of Bengal air masses collide with hot dry continental winds.

Low-pressure systems draw warm, moisture-laden maritime air from the Bay of Bengal into eastern and central India, where it collides with dry continental air, creating sharp moisture gradients and severe squall lines.

⛰️

Topographic & Orographic Lift

Himalayan foothills, Chota Nagpur plateau, and Western Ghats force rapid updrafts.

Abrupt terrain elevation changes along the Meghalaya plateau, Himalayan foothills, and Eastern Ghats force incoming moist air upward, accelerating cloud growth into towering cumulonimbus thunderheads.

⛈️

Nor'westers (*Kalbaishakhi*)

Violent pre-monsoon squall lines sweeping over Odisha, West Bengal, and Bihar.

Originating over the Chota Nagpur Plateau in the afternoon, these violent squall lines travel southeastward across Bengal and Odisha, bringing damaging hail, gale winds, and high-density lightning strokes.

Early Warning Systems & Structural Protection

How IITM Pune, IMD, and CROPC leverage sensor networks, mobile warnings, and structural earthing to save lives.

📱

IITM Damini Mobile App

GPS-based automated warnings 30 to 45 minutes in advance for a 20–40 km radius.

Developed by IITM Pune and IMD, the Damini app tracks real-time lightning location sensors, sending push alerts to mobile phones in affected rural districts before strikes occur.

📡

Lightning Location Networks (LLN)

Over 80 ground-based electromagnetic sensor towers across India.

Operated by IITM Pune, this sensor network detects VLF/LF electromagnetic radiation emitted by lightning strokes, triangulating strike location and flash density in real time.

🛡️

CROPC Lightning Resilient Campaign

National initiative targeting an 80% reduction in lightning deaths.

A joint initiative by CROPC and IMD aimed at installing village warning sirens, earthing rural school roofs, and educating farming communities across high-risk states.

⚡

Franklin Rods & Earthing Pits

Structural air terminals diverting strike current safely into ground pits.

Installing copper air terminals on elevated roofs connected via heavy down-conductors to chemical grounding pits dissipates strike energy into the earth, preventing structural fires and shock.

⚡ Life-Saving Lightning Safety Rules & The 30/30 Evacuation Protocol

  • The 30/30 Rule: Count the time between seeing lightning and hearing thunder. If it is 30 seconds or less (indicating storm within 10 km), take shelter immediately. Stay indoors for 30 minutes after the last thunder.
  • Lightning Safety Position (Crouch): If caught in an open field with no shelter, crouch down low on the balls of your feet with heels touching, tuck your head between your knees, and cover your ears. Minimise contact with the ground (DO NOT lie flat).
  • Avoid Tall Isolated Trees: Never stand under tall isolated trees, open sheds, or telephone poles. Side flash and ground step potential under trees cause most outdoor deaths.
  • Avoid Water & Metal Objects: Get out of lakes, rivers, paddy field water, and stay away from metal fences, tractors, and bicycles.
  • Indoor Safety: Unplug electrical appliances, avoid corded phones, stay away from metal windows/plumbing, and do not take baths or showers during an active thunderstorm.
