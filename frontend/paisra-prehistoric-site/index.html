Prehistoric Paisra Site

A Lower Palaeolithic Open-Air Settlement in the Kharagpur Hills

📍 Paisra, Munger District, Bihar, India

Paisra, set on the northern fringe of the Kharagpur hills in Bihar, is one of the few Lower Palaeolithic sites in India where the actual living surface of early hominins has been excavated, revealing tool-working areas alongside evidence interpreted as a possible hut arrangement.

Location

Paisra lies on the northern edge of the Kharagpur hills in Munger district, Bihar, along a small seasonal stream that once provided water and raw material to prehistoric groups.

The site sits at the interface of the hill terrain and the surrounding plains, a setting typical of many Lower Palaeolithic open-air camps in the region.

Regional Context

Paisra forms part of a wider cluster of Palaeolithic localities across the Kharagpur hills and the middle Ganga plain, linking Bihar into the broader story of Lower Palaeolithic occupation across peninsular and eastern India.

Its stream-side setting and hill-margin location echo patterns seen at other Son and Belan valley sites, helping researchers compare hominin land-use across the wider Bihar–Jharkhand region.

Archaeological Discoveries

🏕️ Living Surface

Excavations exposed an in-situ Lower Palaeolithic occupation surface, a rarity that lets researchers study tool-making activity where it actually happened.

⭕ Possible Hut Feature

An arrangement of stones and post-hole-like features has been interpreted by excavators as a possible windbreak or hut base.

🪨 Knapping Floors

Clusters of debitage and unfinished pieces mark spots where toolmakers sat and worked raw stone into finished tools.

🧊 Raw Material Sources

Nearby stream gravels supplied the quartzite pebbles and cobbles used as the primary raw material for tool production.

Stone Tools

🪓 Handaxes

Bifacially worked handaxes form the core of the Acheulian toolkit recovered at the site.

⚒️ Cleavers

Large flake-based cleavers, shaped for heavy-duty cutting and chopping tasks.

🔪 Scrapers & Cores

Flake scrapers and worked cores show the range of activities carried out at the settlement.

Site Chronology

Lower Palaeolithic
Initial Occupation

Early hominin groups settle near the stream at Paisra, drawn by water and quartzite raw material.

Occupation Phase
Tool Production & Habitation

Repeated visits form the knapping floors and the possible hut arrangement identified at the site.

1980s
Systematic Excavation

Archaeological excavations at Paisra expose the in-situ occupation surface and associated tool assemblages.

Present
Ongoing Research Value

Paisra remains a key reference site for understanding Lower Palaeolithic settlement behaviour in eastern India.
