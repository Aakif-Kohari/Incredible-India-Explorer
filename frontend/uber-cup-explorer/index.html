The Uber Cup Trophy

Donated by Betty Uber

The championship is named after England's Betty Uber, a pre-War great who proposed a women's world team event in 1950 - mirroring the men's Thomas Cup - and personally donated the trophy.

Crafted by Mappin & Webb

Made by London's famed silversmiths Mappin & Webb, the cup stands about 20 inches high: a female player poised on a shuttlecock atop a rotating globe mounted on a plinth.

Inaugural Championship 1956-57

Presented at the federation's 1956 annual general meeting, the trophy was first contested at Lytham St Annes, Lancashire, in 1956-57, where the United States became the first champions.

World Women's Team Championship

Sanctioned today by the Badminton World Federation (BWF), it is the women's counterpart to the Thomas Cup and the premier team prize in women's international badminton.

Tournament History

Conceived by Betty Uber in 1950 and first played in 1956-57, the Uber Cup spent its early decades on a three-year cycle before merging its schedule with the Thomas Cup in 1984. Since then it has been contested every two years, with ties decided over rubbers between national teams.

  • Early format: three singles and four doubles per tie.
  • Since 1984: three singles and two doubles per tie - identical to the Thomas Cup.
  • Most successful nation: China with 16 titles through 2024, ahead of Japan (6), South Korea (3), Indonesia (3) and the United States (3).
  • Reigning champions: South Korea, who beat China 3-1 in the 2026 final at Horsens, Denmark.
  • Global reach: champions have come from only Asia and North America so far, with Denmark the most successful European side as perennial runners-up.

India in the Uber Cup

Debut: 1957

India entered the very first Uber Cup in 1956-57 and immediately announced itself with a semi-final finish - one of only four nations to contest the last four that year.

Record: 10 appearances

Through the 2026 cycle India has appeared in ten editions, with a best result of the semi-finals - reached in 1957, 2014 and 2016.

Hosts: New Delhi 2014

The Siri Fort Sports Complex staged the Thomas & Uber Cup Finals for the first time in India in May 2014 - the edition that produced India's greatest Uber Cup run.

Pioneers: 2014 bronze team

Saina Nehwal and P.V. Sindhu swept both singles against Japan in the semi-final; Jwala Gutta, Ashwini Ponnappa, P.C. Thulasi and teammates earned India's first-ever medal in the event.
