Historical Overview

Khambhat, historically known as Cambay, is an ancient trading port located in the Anand district of Gujarat. Situated at the head of the Gulf of Khambhat, it has served as a crucial maritime gateway since the Harappan period. The port gained immense prominence during the medieval era, particularly under the Gujarat Sultanate and the Mughal Empire, acting as a primary link between the Indian subcontinent and the wider Indian Ocean trade network.

The city was famously visited by renowned travelers such as Marco Polo (1293), Ibn Battuta (1346), and Niccolò de' Conti (1420), who documented its immense wealth, bustling markets, and sophisticated urban planning. Khambhat was particularly renowned for its skilled artisans, especially in the cutting and polishing of agate and carnelian stones, an industry that continues in some form to this day.

Maritime Trade Connections

🌍 Arabia & Middle East

Dhows from the Persian Gulf and Arabian Peninsula regularly docked here, exchanging Arabian horses, dates, and pearls for Indian textiles and spices.

🌏 Southeast Asia

Maritime routes connected Khambhat to the ports of Java, Sumatra, and Malacca, facilitating the spread of Indian culture and the trade of exotic woods and resins.

🏛️ Roman & Greek

Historical texts like the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea suggest early connections, exporting agate beads and onyx to the Mediterranean world.

Major Exports

  • Agate & Carnelian Beads: The most famous export, crafted from local stone deposits and exported globally.
  • Cotton & Silk Textiles: Finely woven fabrics from the Gujarat hinterlands were highly sought after in European and Middle Eastern markets.
  • Indigo: Cultivated in the fertile plains of Gujarat, this valuable dye was a major export commodity.
  • Spices & Opium: Transshipment hub for spices from Malabar and opium produced in the Malwa region.

Archaeological Significance

The Gulf of Khambhat has been the subject of significant underwater archaeological surveys. In 2001, marine surveys revealed anomalous structural remains submerged under 30-40 meters of water. While some controversial claims date these to 7500 BCE, mainstream archaeologists associate the region's rich maritime heritage with the nearby Harappan sites like Lothal, which served as the ancient port city of the Indus Valley Civilization, located just a short distance away.

The ruins of the old city of Khambhat itself, along with ancient mosques, Jain temples, and the remnants of the European factories, provide a layered archaeological record of its diverse history.

Historical Timeline

2400 BCE

Lothal (nearby) flourishes as a major Harappan port city.

1293

Marco Polo visits Khambhat, describing its great commerce and wealth.

1500s

Portuguese arrive and attempt to control the trade, leading to conflicts with the Gujarat Sultanate.

1780

The port is ceded to the British East India Company during the Second Anglo-Maratha War.
