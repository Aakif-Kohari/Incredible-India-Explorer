Discover Valley of Flowers Trek

The Valley of Flowers is a legendary Himalayan alpine valley nestled in Chamoli district, Uttarakhand. Discovered accidentally by British mountaineers in 1931, this high-altitude glacier basin is famous for its exceptional biological diversity and endemic wildflower blooms. Formed by glacial movement, the valley basin is flanked by steep mountain ridges and the meandering Pushpawati River.

Declared a National Park in 1982, the valley remains strictly protected, with grazing banned to protect its delicate alpine turf. In 2005, it was recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Trekkers cross mist-shrouded forests, rustic log bridges, and subalpine valleys to reach this floral sanctuary.

Major Attractions & Highlights

💡 Quick Trek Tips

  • Permit Requirements: Permits are issued on the spot at the Ghangaria checkpost and are valid for three days.
  • Overnight Bans: Camping or staying overnight inside the core Valley of Flowers is strictly forbidden. Trekkers must return to Ghangaria before evening gates close.
  • Nearby Hotspots: Joshimath, Badrinath temple (25 km from Govindghat), Mana Village, and Auli ski slopes are excellent nearby excursions.
  • Best Season: Mid-July to early September is the peak flowering period, triggered by heavy monsoons.

Trek Route & Day-wise Highlights

The standard route begins at Govindghat, climbs up to the Ghangaria village base camp, and proceeds into the National Park.
