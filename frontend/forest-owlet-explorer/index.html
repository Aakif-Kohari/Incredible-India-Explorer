Forest Owlet (Athene blewitti)

Presumed extinct for 113 years until its miraculous rediscovery in 1997 in Maharashtra, the Forest Owlet is a small, stocky diurnal owl endemic to the dry deciduous teak forests of Central India.

Scientific Name: Athene blewitti
IUCN Status: Endangered
Habit: Diurnal & Crepuscular Hunter
Distribution: Central India (MH, MP, Odisha)

Introduction

The Lazarus Bird of India

The Forest Owlet (Athene blewitti, formerly Heteroglaux blewitti) was originally collected in 1872 by F. R. Blewitt. After 1884, no verifiable sightings occurred for over a century, leading ornithologists to consider it extinct.

In November 1997, American ornithologist Pamela Rasmussen and her team rediscovered a living individual in the Satpura foothills near Shahada, Maharashtra — generating worldwide excitement in avian conservation.

Key Highlights

  • Stocky Plumage: Dark brown upperparts, heavily barred breast, and bright yellow iris.
  • Day Hunter: Perches prominently on dead branches in full sunlight to hunt rodents and lizards.
  • Tail Wagging: Rapid side-to-side tail flicking when excited or territorial.
  • Teak Specialist: Dependent on old-growth hollow teak trees for nesting cavities.

Distribution

Geographic Range

Endemic to dry deciduous forests of Central India across three main states:

  • Maharashtra: Melghat Tiger Reserve, Shahada, Tansa Wildlife Sanctuary.
  • Madhya Pradesh: Khandwa, Burhanpur, Narmada Valley forests.
  • Odisha: Isolated eastern range records in teak pockets.

Estimated Wild Population

~250 – 1,000

Extremely fragmented population living in isolated forest patches.

Habitat

Inhabits open dry deciduous forests dominated by teak (Tectona grandis), sal (Shorea robusta), and bamboo thickets in undulating terrain up to 400 m elevation.

Old-Growth Teak Cavities

Requires large mature trees with natural hollows for nesting and roosting.

Open Deciduous Canopy

Prefers patchy forest edges where clear perches provide unobstructed hunting views.

Riparian Pockets

Forest patches near seasonal streams with abundant rodent and reptile prey.

Diet

Active carnivorous predator capable of capturing prey equal to or larger than its own body size:

Grasshoppers & Beetles
Lizards & Skinks
Field Mice & Rodents
Small Tree Frogs

Behaviour

Diurnal Hunting Habit

Unlike most nocturnal owls, the Forest Owlet actively hunts during daytime mornings and late afternoons. It perches openly on bare branches, scanning the grass for movement.

Vocalization & Bobbing Display

Produces a sharp "kew-kew" contact call and bobbing head movements paired with rapid side-to-side tail wagging when agitated or guarding territory.

Conservation Status

IUCN RED LIST ENDANGERED (EN)
Wildlife Protection Act (India): Schedule I
CITES: Appendix I

Threats

Illegal Teak Felling

Removal of large hollow nesting trees destroys crucial breeding cavities required for raising chicks.

Forest Fires & Fuelwood Clearing

Uncontrolled seasonal fires destroy understorey vegetation and deplete rodent/insect prey populations.

Pesticides & Superstition

Agricultural pesticide buildup in rodents, alongside illegal trapping driven by superstition.

Key Protected Areas

Melghat Tiger Reserve

Primary stronghold in Maharashtra with protected dry teak forest reserves.

Tansa Wildlife Sanctuary

Thane district forest reserve harboring rediscovered owlet breeding pairs.

Satpura National Park

Madhya Pradesh forest complex offering extensive contiguous habitat protection.

Interesting Facts

02

03

Feathered Talons: Has thick white feathering extending all the way down to its yellow talons.

04

Fierce Hunter: Can overpower lizards and mice heavier than its own 240g body weight.
