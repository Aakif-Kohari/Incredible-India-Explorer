Harike Wetland

Discover Punjab's largest wetland, formed at the confluence of the Beas and Sutlej rivers. A globally recognized Ramsar Site and winter refuge for thousands of migratory waterbirds.

Heritage & Geography

History & the Beas-Sutlej Confluence

Formation

Harike Wetland was created in 1953 following the construction of the Harike Barrage, forming a vast man-made lake at the meeting point of two great rivers.

Beas-Sutlej Confluence

The wetland lies exactly where the Beas River merges into the Sutlej, making it one of the most significant riverine confluences in northern India.

Ramsar Site

Designated a Ramsar Site of International Importance in 1990, Harike is recognized worldwide for its role in wetland conservation and biodiversity.

Avifauna

Birdlife of Harike

One of India's premier destinations for migratory waterbirds.

Indian Skimmer

Endangered

A rare riverine bird species that breeds on the sandbanks of the Harike wetland.

Bar-headed Goose

Least Concern

Arrives in large flocks after an extraordinary high-altitude migration over the Himalayas.

Greylag Goose

Least Concern

One of the most abundant winter visitors, gathering in the open waters in large numbers.

Northern Pintail

Least Concern

A graceful duck species commonly spotted dabbling in the shallow marshy edges.

Conservation

Protecting Harike

Harike's ecological importance has driven decades of conservation effort amid growing water and agricultural pressures.

  • Wildlife Sanctuary Status: Declared a Wildlife Sanctuary in 1982 to safeguard its rich biodiversity.
  • Weed Control: Ongoing efforts to manage invasive water hyacinth that threatens open water habitats.
  • Anti-poaching Patrols: Regular monitoring to protect birds and the endangered Indus River Dolphin.
  • Community Awareness: Local eco-tourism and education programs to promote wetland stewardship.
Biodiversity

Wetland Biodiversity

The mix of open water, reedbeds, and islands makes Harike a rich, diverse ecosystem.

  • Fish Diversity: Home to over 50 fish species that sustain both wildlife and local fisheries.
  • Indus River Dolphin: One of the few sites in India where this critically endangered freshwater dolphin is found.
  • Aquatic Vegetation: Reed beds and floating vegetation provide nesting grounds for resident and migratory birds.
Did You Know?

Interesting Facts

Rare Freshwater Dolphin

Harike is one of the only places in India where the endangered Indus River Dolphin can still be spotted in the wild.

Migratory Hotspot

Over 200 bird species, including many long-distance migrants from Siberia and Central Asia, winter at Harike every year.

Largest Wetland in Punjab

Spanning roughly 4,100 hectares, Harike is the largest man-made wetland in northern India.
