Aipan

A ritual folk art of the Kumaon region, drawn in white rice paste over a red geru ground for auspicious spaces, festivals, and life-cycle ceremonies.

The tradition

More than decoration

01

What it is

Aipan is a ritualistic folk-art tradition associated especially with Kumaon in Uttarakhand. Its designs combine geometric forms, dots, flowers, nature-inspired elements, and deity-linked symbols.

02

Where it comes from

The strongest regional association is Kumaon, with the tradition practiced across Uttarakhand. It is commonly found on thresholds, floors, walls, and ritual spaces.

03

What it means

Motifs are tied to particular rituals and occasions. Aipan can mark auspiciousness, welcome, worship, protection, and important moments in family life.

Materials

Red ground, white lines

A traditional Aipan surface is prepared with geru, a red ochre or clay-based ground. The designs are then drawn with rice paste, commonly called biswar or bisvar. The high-contrast palette makes the geometric and symbolic lines immediately recognizable.

Geru · red ochre ground Rice paste · white drawing medium Finger-drawn lines · traditional technique

When it appears

Designed for the occasion

Aipan designs vary with the ritual, deity, or life event. Sources from the National Institute of Design's D'Source archive and the Government of Uttarakhand describe its use across festivals, pujas, weddings, and life-cycle ceremonies.
