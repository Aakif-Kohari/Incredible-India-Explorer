Origins & Historical Roots

How a simple street stall outside Dadar Railway Station birthed Mumbai's most celebrated street dish in 1966.

The Birth of a Street Icon (1966)

In 1966, Ashok Vaidya opened a modest food stall on Platform 1 outside Dadar Railway Station in Mumbai. Dadar was the central transit node for tens of thousands of textile mill workers commuting daily on the Mumbai Suburban Railway.

Vaidya combined the classic Maharashtrian batata vada (spiced potato fritter) with soft Portuguese-style pav bread roll and a punchy dry red garlic chutney, creating a portable, filling meal that could be eaten on the go.

Textile Mills & Cultural Transformation

During the 1970s and 1980s, the decline and closure of Mumbai's textile mills in Girangaon led many former mill workers to open street food stalls across the city.

Simultaneously, local political and socio-cultural movements championed street vendors serving traditional Maharashtrian food. Today, Vada Pav is widely recognized as the democratic food symbol of Mumbai's resilient spirit.
