1. Foothills & Terai-Shiwaliks

300m – 1,500m

The gateway to the mountains, where dense Sal forests and fertile river plains meet the outer Siwalik ridges.

Geography & Climate

Subtropical climate, heavy monsoon rainfall, swampy Terai eco-tones, and lush river valleys like the Doon Valley.

Communities & Culture

Home to the Tharu, Van Gujjar, and Raji peoples. Renowned for pastoral heritage and deep forest knowledge.

Tharu Van Gujjar Raji

Daily Life & Traditions

Terraced paddy cultivation, sugarcane farming, timber craft, and seasonal transhumance where pastoralists move livestock towards summer pastures.

2. Mid-Altitude Towns & Lesser Himalayas

1,500m – 3,000m

The temperate heartland of the Himalayas, defined by fragrant Deodar forests, steep step-terraces, and vibrant Pahari villages.

Geography & Climate

Temperate pine and oak ecosystems, cool summers, crisp winters with mild snowfall, and mist-shrouded valleys.

Communities & Culture

Garhwali, Kumaoni, Himachali, and Pahari communities with rich folk epics (Pandav Lila) and deity governance traditions.

Garhwali Kumaoni Himachali

Architecture & Agriculture

Kath-Kuni earthquake-resistant wood-and-stone timber interlocking structures, apple orchard cultivation, and Harela festival celebrations.

3. High-Altitude Villages & Greater Himalayas

3,000m – 5,000m

Beyond the tree-line lie wide alpine meadows (Bugyals), where hardy mountain communities thrive alongside yaks and snow peaks.

Geography & Climate

Sub-alpine meadows, severe winter frost, intense solar radiation, and steep granite passes (La).

Communities & Culture

Bhotia, Kinnauri, Monpa, and Sherpa high-altitude lineages with centuries of mountain trade expertise.

Bhotia Kinnauri Monpa

High-Altitude Adaptation

Buckwheat and barley cultivation during short summer windows, spinning raw sheep wool into heavy Thulma blankets, and Yak pastoralism.

4. Glacial & Trans-Himalayan Cold Deserts

Above 5,000m

The high plateau realm of Ladakh, Spiti, and Zanskar — an arid expanse of glaciers, ancient monasteries, and nomadic spirit.

Geography & Climate

Arid cold desert environment, sub-zero winters (-30°C), glacial river basins, and brilliant high-altitude skies.

Communities & Culture

Changpa nomadic pastoralists, Ladakhi, and Spitian Buddhists. Rich heritage of Buddhist monasteries (Gompas) and chortens.

Changpa Ladakhi Spitian

Nomadic Life & Pashmina

Living in Yak-hair Rebo tents on the Changthang plateau, rearing Changthangi Pashmina goats, drinking butter tea (Gur Gur Chai), and celebrating Losar.
