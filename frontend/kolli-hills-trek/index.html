Discover Kolli Hills

The Kolli Hills (Kolli Malai), situated in the Eastern Ghats of Tamil Nadu, are famous for their pristine natural beauty, spice plantations, and deep historical lineage. Feared in antiquity for the protective guardian deity Kolli Pavai, the hills have preserved their ancient green cover and tribal heritage.

The defining trek of Kolli Hills starts at the ancient Arapaleeswarar Temple (built in the 1st century CE during the Sangam era) and descends into a deep forest gorge. Trekkers climb down 1,028 steep, winding stone steps to reach the bottom of the spectacular 300-foot Agasagangai Waterfalls. Surrounded by sub-tropical evergreen trees, rare medicinal herbs, and plantations of black pepper and pineapple, this trek offers a refreshing but physically demanding adventure.

Major Attractions & Highlights

💡 Quick Trek Tips

  • Step Safety: The 1,028 steps are concrete but steep. Make sure to hold the handrails and descend carefully, especially post-monsoon when moss grows.
  • Water Bathing: Swimming directly under the main waterfall stream is dangerous due to strong currents. Bathing is safe in the designated side pools.
  • Pineapple Farms: Kolli Hills are famous for sweet pineapples. Try fresh pineapple slices sold by local tribal farmers at viewpoints.
  • Hill Driving: Reaching Semmedu from the plains requires negotiating 70 hairpin bends. Drive slowly and blow horn at curves.

Trek Route & Gorge Highlights

The trail starts from Arapaleeswarar Temple checkpost, enters the gorge entrance, climbs down the 1,028 steps, and returns via the same steep route.

History & King Valvil Ori

Kolli Hills are deeply integrated into Sangam literature. The region was ruled by King Valvil Ori around 200 CE, one of the seven great patrons of ancient Tamil arts, celebrated for his archery and support of local poets.
