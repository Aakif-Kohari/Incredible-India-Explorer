Potti Sriramulu — The Sacrifice that Redrew India's Map

"Amarajeevi" — The Immortal Being

A committed Gandhian who first gave his life's work to the freedom struggle and the eradication of untouchability, Potti Sriramulu later undertook a fast unto death demanding a separate Telugu-speaking state. His death on 15 December 1952, after 58 days of fasting, forced the Indian government's hand and directly led to the formation of Andhra State in 1953 — the first state in independent India created purely on linguistic lines, and the template for the States Reorganisation Act of 1956.

📖 Biography

Potti Sriramulu was born on 16 October 1901 in Madras (present-day Chennai) into a Telugu-speaking family that had settled in the city. After the early death of his parents, he trained as an engineer and briefly worked with the Great Indian Peninsula Railway in Bombay, a stable professional path he ultimately left behind to join Mahatma Gandhi's Sabarmati Ashram.

At the ashram, Sriramulu immersed himself in Gandhian ideals — truth, non-violence, self-reliance, and social reform. He became especially devoted to the eradication of untouchability, spending years working directly among Dalit communities, promoting temple entry rights, and living an austere, service-oriented life that earned him deep respect within the Congress movement.

Why "Amarajeevi"?

After his death, Sriramulu came to be known as Amarajeevi — "the immortal being" — a title reflecting how his self-sacrifice was seen not as an end but as the spark that gave permanent shape to the aspirations of Telugu-speaking people.

🕊️ Role in the Freedom Movement

Sriramulu was an active participant in India's independence struggle well before his famous fast. He took part in the Salt Satyagraha of 1930, courting arrest and imprisonment as part of the broader Civil Disobedience Movement launched by Gandhi. He also participated in the Quit India Movement of 1942, again facing detention by British authorities.

Beyond direct political agitation, Sriramulu's contribution to the freedom movement was inseparable from his social work — he believed swaraj (self-rule) was incomplete without social reform, and dedicated himself to uplifting marginalized communities as part of the same moral project as national independence.

  • Trained and lived at Gandhi's Sabarmati Ashram, adopting a life of simplicity and service.
  • Participated in the 1930 Salt Satyagraha (Dandi March-inspired civil disobedience).
  • Joined the 1942 Quit India Movement and was imprisoned by British colonial authorities.
  • Worked extensively on anti-untouchability campaigns and temple-entry movements for Dalits.

⚖️ The Fast Unto Death & Its Impact on Modern India

After independence, demand grew for a separate state for Telugu speakers, to be carved out of the Madras Presidency. When the central government repeatedly deferred the decision, Sriramulu began an indefinite fast unto death on 19 October 1952 at Bezwada (present-day Vijayawada), demanding the formation of Andhra State.

The fast continued for 58 days. As Sriramulu's health deteriorated and public tension mounted across the Telugu-speaking districts, his death on 15 December 1952 triggered widespread protests and unrest. Faced with this pressure, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru's government announced the creation of a separate Andhra State, which came into existence on 1 October 1953 — comprising the Telugu-speaking districts of the former Madras Presidency.

A Template for the Nation

The formation of Andhra State became a precedent that could not be ignored. It directly informed the establishment of the States Reorganisation Commission and the subsequent States Reorganisation Act of 1956, which redrew the internal map of India largely along linguistic lines — a framework that still defines Indian state boundaries today.

🏛️ Legacy

Andhra State (1953)

The first state in independent India created explicitly on linguistic lines, directly resulting from Sriramulu's sacrifice.

States Reorganisation Act, 1956

Nationwide redrawing of state boundaries along linguistic lines, using Andhra as its founding precedent.

Amarajeevi Potti Sriramulu District

A district in present-day Andhra Pradesh (formerly Nellore/SPSR Nellore-adjacent naming) honors his memory.

National Commemoration

Sriramulu appears on Indian postage stamps and is remembered annually on his death anniversary across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
