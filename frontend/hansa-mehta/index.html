Hansa Mehta: Architect of Equality

Freedom fighter, educator, Constituent Assembly member, and pioneer who changed "all men" to "all human beings" in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

📅 1897 - 1995 ⚖️ Pioneer of Women's Rights

Shaping Equality in India and the World

Hansa Jivraj Mehta (1897-1995) was one of India's most remarkable reformers whose influence extended from the Constituent Assembly debates in Delhi to the drafting chambers of the United Nations. Her insistence on changing "all men are born free" to "all human beings are born free" in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) stands as one of the most consequential edits in human rights history.

Multi-Dimensional Legacy

Freedom Fighter: Active participant in independence movement with Gandhi and Sarojini Naidu
Constituent Assembly: Member who shaped fundamental rights and women's equality
UN Delegate: Vice-Chair of UN Commission on Human Rights, key UDHR drafter
Educator: Vice-Chancellor of MS University Baroda and SNDT Women's University
Reformer: Pioneer of Hindu Code Bill and women's legal rights
Internationalist: President of All India Women's Conference, UNESCO contributor

Hansa Mehta's life represents a remarkable synthesis of nationalist commitment and internationalist vision. She believed that India's independence must be accompanied by a fundamental transformation in women's status, and she worked tirelessly at both national and global forums to realize this vision.

Early Life & Family Heritage

Born on July 3, 1897, in Surat, Gujarat, Hansa Mehta was fortunate to be born into one of Gujarat's most progressive families. Her father, Manubhai Mehta, was the Dewan (Prime Minister) of Baroda State, and her grandfather was the noted social reformer and educationist Nandshankar Mehta, author of the first Gujarati novel.

Formative Influences

Education & Intellectual Development

Hansa received an exceptional education that was rare for women of her time. She graduated from Women's College in Baroda in 1918 with a degree in philosophy, and then traveled to London in 1919 to study at the London School of Economics and the University of London.

Academic Achievements

Freedom Movement Participation

Hansa Mehta's entry into the freedom movement was influenced by her meeting with Mahatma Gandhi and Sarojini Naidu. She became an active Congress worker, participating in civil disobedience movements and facing imprisonment multiple times for her nationalist activities.

Key Contributions

Constituent Assembly Role

Hansa Mehta was one of the 15 women members of the Constituent Assembly that drafted India's Constitution between 1946 and 1950. She served on the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee and played a pivotal role in shaping the constitutional guarantees of equality and non-discrimination.

Constitutional Contributions

Women's Rights Advocacy

Hansa Mehta was one of India's most important feminist thinkers and activists. She advocated for comprehensive legal reforms including the Hindu Code Bill, which sought to give women equal rights in marriage, inheritance, and divorce.

Major Advocacy Areas

International Human Rights Work

Hansa Mehta's most globally significant contribution was her work at the United Nations on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR). As India's delegate and Vice-Chair of the UN Commission on Human Rights (1948-1950), she played a crucial role in shaping one of the most important documents in human history.

Educational Reform Leadership

Beyond her political and human rights work, Hansa Mehta was a pioneering educator who transformed Indian higher education. She served as Vice-Chancellor of two major universities and shaped educational policy at the national level.

University Leadership

Enduring Legacy

Hansa Mehta's legacy continues to shape India's constitutional framework, educational system, and commitment to gender equality. Her contributions to the UDHR ensure that her influence extends globally to every nation that upholds human rights.
