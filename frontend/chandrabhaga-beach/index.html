Overview

Where a vanished river meets the Bay of Bengal

Chandrabhaga Beach lies on the eastern coast of Odisha, in Puri district, roughly three kilometres east of the Konark Sun Temple and about 35 km from the town of Puri along the scenic Puri–Konark Marine Drive. It takes its name from the Chandrabhaga River, which once flowed past the temple and emptied into the sea here; the river has since dried to little more than a seasonal trickle, but the site it left behind remains one of the most photographed stretches of coastline in eastern India.

The beach is a long, open run of golden sand backed by casuarina groves, facing directly east across the Bay of Bengal — the same orientation that made the adjoining temple famous for catching the first light of dawn. A small fishing community, with roots tracing back to migrants from coastal Andhra Pradesh, still works the shoreline, and a lighthouse marks the point where the old river mouth met the sea.

Heritage Connection

Part of the Konark heritage corridor

Chandrabhaga sits on a short, walkable stretch of coast that strings together some of Odisha's most significant sacred sites — the same route pilgrims have followed for centuries between Puri and Konark.

1

Chandrabhaga Beach

Starting point · Bay of Bengal shoreline

Traditionally the pilgrims' bathing point before entering the Sun Temple precincts — a ritual cleansing that preceded worship at Konark.

2

Konark Sun Temple

3 km west · UNESCO World Heritage Site (1984)

Built around 1250 CE under King Narasimhadeva I, the temple is conceived as a colossal stone chariot for the sun god Surya, mounted on 24 carved wheels and drawn by seven horses.

3

Ramachandi Temple

~7 km south · River-mouth shrine

Dedicated to the goddess Ramachandi, set where the Kusabhadra river meets the sea — a common stop for travellers continuing along the marine drive toward Puri.

4

Kuruma

~8 km from Konark · Archaeological site

A village where Buddhist-era artefacts have been unearthed, pointing to older layers of religious history beneath the Sun Temple's later fame.

5

Puri Jagannath Temple

~35 km southwest · Char Dham pilgrimage site

The corridor's anchor destination, commonly combined with Chandrabhaga and Konark in a single day's temple-and-coast itinerary.

Cultural Significance

Mythology, healing, and the worship of Surya

Two intertwined legends give the beach its name and its sanctity — both centred on the sun god Surya, to whom the neighbouring temple is dedicated.

Legend One

Samba's cure

According to the Skanda and Narada Puranas, Samba — son of Lord Krishna — was afflicted with leprosy. He performed penance to Surya at the mouth of the Chandrabhaga river and was cured, establishing the site as a place of healing and solar worship, and, by tradition, the spiritual origin of the Sun Temple itself.

Legend Two

Chandrabhaga's sacrifice

A second account tells of Chandrabhaga, a sage's daughter pursued by Surya himself. To protect her chastity, she fled into the river and gave her life rather than yield. In tribute, the river — and later the beach — carried her name, and the sun god is said to have blessed its waters.

Magha Saptami

Chandrabhaga Mela

A seven-day fair honouring Surya, held on the seventh day of the full-moon fortnight of Magha (Jan–Feb). Thousands take a ritual dawn dip believed to cleanse sin, echoing the Samba legend.

1–5 December, annually

International Sand Art Festival

National and international sand artists sculpt large-scale works directly on the beach, run by the Odisha Tourism Development Corporation.

Since the early 1980s

Konark Dance Festival

A classical dance festival staged against the Sun Temple backdrop, drawing visitors to the wider Konark–Chandrabhaga area each winter.

Natural Features & Activities

Golden sand, casuarina groves, and a living reef

Blue Flag shoreline

Chandrabhaga became India's first Blue Flag–certified beach, an eco-tourism label from the Foundation for Environmental Education recognising water quality and clean-beach management.

Sunrise coastline

Facing due east, the beach is renowned for panoramic sunrises and sunsets over the Bay of Bengal — the draw for photographers, meditators, and early risers alike.

Chandrabhaga coral reef

Offshore, the Chandrabhaga coral reef is counted among India's notable living coral formations, part of the marine resources that make this stretch of coast ecologically distinct.

Casuarina groves

Tall stands of casuarina trees line the dunes, offering shade and giving the beach a distinct silhouette against the open sky.

Chandrabhaga Lighthouse

A working lighthouse stands near the old river mouth, marking the point where the Chandrabhaga once emptied into the sea.

Fishing community

A settlement of fisherfolk, with roots in coastal Andhra Pradesh, works the beach near the dried river bed, adding a living, working layer to the landscape.
