Prem Kumar Sahgal

Military Secretary to Subhas Chandra Bose, Commander of the 2nd Infantry Regiment, and a central figure in the Red Fort Trials that galvanized the Indian independence movement.

🎖️ Quick Facts

Organization: Indian National Army (INA)
Rank/Role: Colonel, Military Secretary
Key Theater: Mount Popa, Burma

Spouse: Captain Lakshmi Sahgal (Rani of Jhansi Regiment)
A Soldier's Journey

Early Life & The British Indian Army

Born on March 25, 1917, in Lahore into a prominent family (his father was a judge at the Lahore High Court), Prem Kumar Sahgal received a privileged education before choosing a military career. He attended the Indian Military Academy in Dehradun and was commissioned into the British Indian Army, serving with the 2nd Battalion, 10th Baloch Regiment.

During World War II, Sahgal's regiment was deployed to Malaya to defend against the Japanese advance. Following the disastrous Fall of Singapore in 1942, Sahgal became a Prisoner of War (POW). Witnessing the collapse of British military invincibility and moved by the call for Indian liberation, he made the decisive choice to join the first INA under Captain Mohan Singh.

🇮🇳 Netaji's Confidant

When Subhas Chandra Bose took over the INA in 1943, Sahgal's organizational brilliance caught his attention. Sahgal was appointed Military Secretary at the Supreme Headquarters of the Azad Hind Fauj, playing a crucial role in troop mobilization.

Command & Conflict

Commanding the 2nd Infantry Regiment

Moving from a staff role to active combat, Colonel Sahgal took command of the INA's 2nd Infantry Regiment. In early 1945, his forces were assigned the grueling task of defending the area around Mount Popa in central Burma to cover the retreat of the main INA and Japanese forces against the advancing British Fourteenth Army.

Despite facing overwhelming odds, severe supply shortages, and constant Allied aerial bombardment, Sahgal’s regiment fought a tenacious defensive campaign. Eventually, outmaneuvered and cut off from supplies, Sahgal and his men were forced to surrender to British forces in April 1945.

⚔️ The Burma Theater

The defense of Mount Popa stands as a testament to the sheer willpower of the INA. Sahgal's tactical deployments successfully delayed Allied forces long enough for Netaji to safely relocate the Azad Hind government headquarters from Rangoon.

⚖️ A United Front

The joint trial of Sahgal (Hindu), Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim), and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh) became a powerful symbol of secular unity. The rallying cry "Lal Qila se aayi awaz, Sahgal, Dhillon, Shah Nawaz!" echoed across the subcontinent.

The Turning Point

The Red Fort Trials (1945–1946)

At the end of the war, the British charged Sahgal, Dhillon, and Khan with "waging war against the King-Emperor" and murder, putting them on trial at the Red Fort in Delhi. The trial was meant to humiliate the INA and discourage mutiny.

Instead, the INA Defence Committee (led by Bhulabhai Desai) argued that the Azad Hind government was a legitimate state, and its soldiers were fighting a legal war of liberation, not committing treason. The intense public sympathy and the resulting unrest in the British Indian military (culminating in the 1946 Naval Mutiny) forced the British Commander-in-Chief to remit their sentences, marking the beginning of the end for the British Raj.

Timeline of Major Events

1917

Birth

Born in Lahore, British India.

1942

POW and the First INA

Captured after the Fall of Singapore and joins the Indian National Army.

1943 – 1945

Military Secretary & Combat Command

Serves as Netaji's Military Secretary, then commands the 2nd Infantry Regiment at Mount Popa.

1945 – 1946

The Red Fort Trials

Stands trial for treason at the Red Fort. He is convicted but released due to massive public pressure.

1947 & 1992

Later Life

Marries Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan in 1947. Lives quietly in Kanpur until his death in 1992.
