The grid is a stylised loom, not a literal map — each tile's position echoes that state's real position in India (north→south, west→east) with the northeastern states set apart, the way a weaver keeps a border thread apart from the field.

Sources & image credit

Regional textile facts (fabric, weaving technique, motifs, drape, occasions) are compiled from widely documented public knowledge of Indian handloom traditions, including craft documentation by India's Ministry of Textiles and D'Source (IDC, IIT Bombay).

Photographs for 12 of the 14 states are sourced directly from Wikimedia Commons, freely licensed under Creative Commons (CC BY-SA). Every photo caption in the map panel and the gallery below links straight to that image's Commons file page, where the original photographer and exact licence version are recorded — click "Wikimedia Commons ↗" under any photo to see it.

Bihar and Andhra Pradesh currently show an original SVG motif illustration instead of a photo, because no confidently-matched, freely-licensed photo for those two states' specific weaves was found during sourcing — flagged here rather than risk mis-crediting an image. The same original illustrations also appear as a graceful fallback everywhere else, in case a Commons link ever moves.

Good next places to look for the two missing photos, or to add more detail images later:
