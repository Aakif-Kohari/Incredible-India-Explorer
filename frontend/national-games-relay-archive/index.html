Origins

From Olympic trials
to a national relay

The Games began as the Indian Olympic Games, first held in Delhi in 1924 to select athletes for the Paris Olympics that year. The earliest editions rotated between Lahore, Allahabad and a handful of other cities before the event was formally renamed the National Games at the 9th edition, hosted by Bombay in 1940.

The 1948 Lucknow Games carried special weight — the first edition held in a newly independent India, the same year the country won hockey gold at the London Olympics. For decades after, the Games ran on an inconsistent schedule, shaped by funding and logistics as much as sport.

Everything changed in 1985, when New Delhi hosted the first modern National Games — rebuilt on Olympic lines with an athletes' village, a formal opening ceremony, and a rotating state-host model that continues today.
