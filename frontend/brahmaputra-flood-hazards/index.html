Basin Geography

One River, Three Countries, One Valley

The Brahmaputra begins beside Mount Kailash in Tibet, slices through the Himalaya in Arunachal Pradesh, braids across the entire width of Assam, and finally merges with the Ganges in Bangladesh — one of Earth's great transboundary rivers.

🏔️ The River's Journey

The Yarlung Tsangpo rises from glaciers near Mount Kailash in Tibet, flows east for over 1,200 km, then plunges through the Tsangpo gorge — the deepest on Earth — into Arunachal Pradesh as the Siang (Dihang). At Sadiya it is joined by the Dibang and Lohit rivers, takes the name Brahmaputra ("Son of Brahma"), and enters the Assam valley near Dibrugarh before crossing into Bangladesh as the Jamuna.

🗺️ A Transboundary Basin

The basin drains roughly 580,000 km² across China (Tibet), Bhutan, India and Bangladesh. In India it collects water from Arunachal Himalaya in the north and the Meghalaya plateau, Naga hills and Karbi Anglong in the south — so nearly every drop that falls in Northeast India funnels through the narrow Assam valley.

🌊 A Braided Giant

In Assam the channel widens from about 1 km at Sadiya to as much as 10–18 km downstream — one of the world's largest braided rivers, split around mid-channel sandbars and islands including Majuli, the world's largest inhabited river island. Its slope flattens sharply after leaving the mountains, so it deposits enormous volumes of silt and constantly shifts its banks.

⚖️ Lifeline & Destroyer

The same river that drowns fields also creates them — annual silt renews farmland, feeds wetlands and beels that sustain fisheries, and shapes Kaziranga's grasslands. Ferries cross it, cities like Guwahati and Dibrugarh grow along it, and Assamese culture celebrates it — yet each monsoon tests every settlement on its floodplain.

Preparedness

Flood Preparedness Information

Official warnings save lives. Know the alerts to follow, and what to do before, during and after a flood wherever you live or travel in the basin.

☎️ Emergency Contacts & Alerts

  • 112 — All-India emergency helpline (police, fire, medical)
  • 1078 — NDMA national disaster helpline
  • ASDMA / State Emergency Operations Centre — district control rooms issue flood bulletins during monsoon
  • CWC flood forecasts — Central Water Commission level & inflow advisories for major stations
  • NDRF / SDRF — rescue battalions deployed ahead of severe flood waves

Sign up for local SMS alerts where offered, follow your district administration's verified handles, and keep a go-bag ready through June–September if you live in a low-lying or riverine (char) area.

⚠️ Educational Notice

This page is for educational exploration only. It does not replace official advisories. During any real flood event always follow instructions from ASDMA, the district administration, CWC bulletins and emergency services — never enter floodwater on foot or by vehicle, and never shelter near embankments during breaches.
