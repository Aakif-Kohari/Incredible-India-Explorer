The Man Behind the Movement

A Leader Shaped by the Forest

Komaram Bheem (c. 1901 – 1940) remains a towering figure in the history of tribal resistance in India. Hailing from the Gond community in the dense forests of the Adilabad region (present-day Telangana), his life was a testament to the struggle against systemic exploitation.

During the rule of the Nizam of Hyderabad, tribal communities faced severe oppression, including forced labor (Vetti), bans on traditional hunting, and land grabbing by landlords and moneylenders. Bheem organized the Gonds to resist these injustices, famously declaring that the forest, water, and land belong to those who live with them.

His rebellion was not just a fight for survival, but a profound assertion of indigenous identity, self-governance, and ecological harmony.

The Core Philosophy

Jal · Jangal · Zameen

Three ideas, one unified demand for dignity and self-determination.

Right to Water

The Nizam's administration imposed restrictions on tribal access to local water bodies, tanks, and streams. For the Gond community, water was not a commodity but a sacred, shared resource essential for life, agriculture, and livestock. Bheem's resistance demanded the unconditional right of tribes to access and manage their traditional water sources.

Right to the Forest

Colonial and Nizam-era forest laws criminalized traditional tribal practices like hunting, gathering forest produce, and shifting cultivation. The forest was the Gond's home, pharmacy, and marketplace. Bheem fought against the forest department's exploitation and the criminalization of indigenous livelihoods.

Right to Land

Feudal landlords (Deshmukhs) and moneylenders (Sahukars) systematically seized tribal lands through deceitful debt traps. Bheem's movement actively resisted this land alienation, asserting that the land belongs to those who have cultivated and protected it for generations, not to absentee landlords.
