✍️ Script & Phonetics

Korku Devanagari Script

Understanding how the historically oral Korku language is written today.

📜

Oral Roots & Devanagari Adoption

Korku has no script of its own and was historically an oral language, passed down through folk songs and storytelling. Very few Korku speakers today are literate in the language.

Where Korku is written, it uses the Devanagari script (देवनागरी) in the everyday "Balbodh" style — the same script used for Hindi and Marathi, the dominant languages of the surrounding region. Literacy programmes, a Korku dictionary, grammar and translated texts now exist in this script.

Devanagari Script Rendering: कोरकू भाषा
Latin Phonetic Notation: Korku Bhasha
IPA International Phonetic: [koːrkuː bʱaːʂaː]
🎙️

Unique Phonetic Features

  • Tone Distinction: Uniquely among Munda languages, Korku syllables show a contrast between high and low tone — most other Munda languages rely on predictable stress instead.
  • Agglutinative Structure: Korku is a highly suffixing language; grammatical functions that English expresses with separate words are folded into verb suffixes.
  • Subject-Object-Verb Order: Like all Munda languages, Korku follows strict SOV sentence order, e.g. "Iñj dukanaʈen saːkaɾ sasaːba" ("I store-from sugar bring.will").
  • Isolated Position: Korku is the westernmost Munda language, geographically surrounded by Dravidian-speaking Gondi communities, with its closest linguistic relatives (Mundari, Santali) far to the east.

🌳 Linguistic Family Lineage

Austroasiatic Language Family Context

Korku belongs to the North Munda branch of the Austroasiatic family — a family whose other branches stretch across Southeast Asia.

Austroasiatic Language Tree Classification

Austroasiatic
Mon-Khmer Branch Khmer, Vietnamese, Mon (Southeast Asia)
Munda Branch (India) ⭐ North Munda: Korku, Santali, Mundari, Ho
South Munda Kharia, Juang, Sora, Gutob

Munda Cognates: The Word for "Man"

Though Korku is geographically cut off in central India, its core vocabulary still traces back to shared North Munda roots:

Language Term Meaning
Korku Man (root of "Korku" = people)
Mundari Man
Santali Man

This shared root — recorded in Grierson's Linguistic Survey of India — is strong evidence that Korku, though isolated by a thousand kilometres of Dravidian- and Indo-Aryan-speaking territory, remains firmly part of the North Munda family alongside Mundari and Santali.

Korku Culture, Faith & Traditions

Discover the beliefs, festivals and forest life of the Korku people of the Satpura hills.

🛖

Muthwa Dev Village Shrines

Muthwa Dev is the most revered deity among the Korku, with a shrine at the centre of nearly every village, believed to watch over the community and guard against evil spirits at night.

🎭

Meghnath & Dussehra

Korku legend holds that Ravana asked Lord Mahadev to create the Korku people; his son Meghnath is revered as a guardian deity, giving Korku Dussehra celebrations a distinctive local character.

🌾

Jiroti Festival

Celebrated during the monsoon month of Shravan, this important Korku feast honours Jiroti Devi with an evening of communal worship and shared meals, followed by "Jiroti Kar" the next day.

🌲

Life in the Melghat Forests

The thickly forested Melghat region remains the cultural heart of the Korku in Maharashtra, where clan exogamy customs (marrying outside one's own gotra) and forest-based livelihoods continue to shape daily life.
