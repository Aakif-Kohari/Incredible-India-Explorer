Formation & Timeline

Six days, Gulf of Thailand to the Deccan interior

Michaung began as a modest low over the Gulf of Thailand, crossed the Malay Peninsula, and re-organised in the Bay of Bengal — a "monsoon depression" track typical of late-season North Indian Ocean storms.

30 Nov 2023

Low-pressure area forms

A low-pressure area consolidates over the Gulf of Thailand and drifts west across the Malay Peninsula into the Andaman Sea / southeast Bay of Bengal.

1–2 Dec 2023

Depression, then deep depression

The system organises into a depression over the southeast Bay of Bengal on 2 December and steadily deepens as it tracks north-west toward the Indian coastline.

3 Dec 2023

Named "Michaung"

The depression intensifies into a cyclonic storm and is named Michaung (pronounced roughly "Migjaum," contributed by Myanmar, meaning strength and resilience) — the sixth named storm of the 2023 season.

4 Dec 2023 — Peak rainfall over Chennai

Severe cyclonic storm, record downpour

Michaung intensifies further while grazing the Tamil Nadu coast. Chennai logs roughly 151 mm of rain in the 24 hours to 8:30 am — among the highest 24-hour totals recorded for that date — and the following 24-hour spell is even heavier, flooding most of the city.

5 Dec 2023 — Landfall

Crosses the coast near Bapatla

Michaung makes landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Kavali, near Bapatla, around early afternoon, with sustained winds near 90–100 km/h gusting higher. It weakens to a cyclonic storm by that evening.

6 Dec 2023

Weakens and dissipates

Moving inland toward Telangana, Michaung degrades into a depression and then a well-marked low, dissipating over the Deccan Plateau.

Regional Impact

Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh

Michaung's rain band battered Tamil Nadu for two days before the storm itself crossed into Andhra Pradesh — so the two states experienced very different disasters from the same system.

Tamil Nadu · rain-bound, not landfall

Chennai and its northern suburbs

Michaung never made landfall in Tamil Nadu, but its outer bands parked over Chennai, Tiruvallur, Chengalpattu and Kanchipuram for close to two days, dropping some of the heaviest rain the region had recorded in years.

Extreme Rainfall

A slow-moving rain engine, not a wind event

Michaung's damage in Tamil Nadu came almost entirely from rainfall volume and duration rather than wind speed — the storm crept along the coast for nearly two days before making landfall further north.

Urban Flooding

Why Chennai floods when it rains hard

Chennai sits on a flat coastal plain crossed by three shallow rivers and a network of natural wetlands and tanks that historically absorbed monsoon surges. Decades of dense construction over those wetlands and drainage channels — much of it in low-lying areas — has steadily reduced the city's capacity to shed water quickly.

When Michaung's rain bands stalled over the city for nearly 36 hours, stormwater had nowhere to go: streets became channels, low-lying neighbourhoods turned into shallow lakes, and pumping crews needed more than a day and a half of continuous work to bring visible relief. City officials said floodwaters had receded from roughly three-quarters of Chennai only by the Wednesday after landfall.

Local commentary at the time described the episode as a reminder that unmanaged urban growth, not just extreme weather, decides how badly a city floods.

Many residents drew direct comparisons to Chennai's catastrophic December 2015 floods — a sign of how familiar this pattern of monsoon-season urban flooding has become for the city.

Infrastructure Impact

What stopped working

Chennai airport

Its runway flooded and operations were suspended; services resumed the next morning once rain eased and water was cleared.

Rail & road

More than 140 trains were cancelled across the affected region alongside roughly 40 flights in Andhra Pradesh; waterlogged roads stranded vehicles for days.

Power & communications

High winds and waterlogging brought down trees onto power and telecom lines in both states, causing widespread and prolonged outages.

Industry

Major electronics and auto plants around Chennai — including Foxconn, Pegatron and Hyundai facilities — suspended shifts as flooding disrupted access and logistics.

Agriculture

Standing paddy and horticultural crops in coastal Andhra Pradesh were badly damaged by wind and flooding just ahead of harvest.

Schools & institutions

Schools, colleges and many government offices across Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry and Odisha were shut for one or more days.

Evacuation & Relief

Getting people out of the water

Both states pre-positioned evacuations and disaster-response teams before landfall, while Chennai's post-flood response leaned heavily on boat rescues and relief camps.

NDRF teams deployed regionwide

The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) deployed roughly two dozen teams across Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, with about 19 teams concentrated in Chennai and neighbouring Thiruvallur district alone. Crews used rubber and fibre boats to pull residents — including a pregnant woman who needed help delivering her baby — from flooded homes, while fishermen were warned off the water for days before landfall.

Disaster-Management Response

Funding, assessment and the fight over relief

The aftermath became as politically contentious as the storm itself, with Tamil Nadu arguing central relief fell far short of the damage assessed on the ground.
