Purple Frog (Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis)

Also known as the Mahabali Frog or Indian Purple Frog, this bizarre subterranean amphibian features a pointed snout, bloated purple-grey body, and spent over 100 million years evolving in isolation in the Western Ghats.

Scientific Name: Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis
IUCN Status: Endangered
Lifestyle: Subterranean (Fossorial)
Distribution: Western Ghats (Kerala & TN)

Introduction

The Underground Marvel of Sahyadri

The Purple Frog (Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis) was officially described in 2003 by Indian scientist S.D. Biju and Franky Bossuyt of Vrije Universiteit Brussel. Its discovery was hailed as a "once-in-a-century" event in herpetology.

DNA analysis revealed that its closest living relatives are the Sooglossidae frogs of the Seychelles Islands, providing direct geological evidence of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana breaking apart over 100 million years ago.

Key Highlights

  • Ancient Lineage: Coexisted with dinosaurs over 100 million years ago.
  • Pointed Snout: Distinctive pale white protrusion for burrowing and feeding on subterranean termites.
  • Transient Surface Life: Emerges from underground burrows for only 2 to 3 weeks a year during monsoons to mate.
  • Suctorial Tadpoles: Tadpoles possess specialized mouthparts that cling to torrential waterfall rocks.

Scientific Name & Etymology

The generic name Nasikabatrachus is derived from the Sanskrit word nasika ("nose") and Greek batrachos ("frog"), referring to its sharp pointed snout. The species epithet sahyadrensis refers to the Sahyadri (Western Ghats) mountain range.

Habitat

Restricted to loose, moist soil layers beneath leaf litter in secondary forests, coffee plantations, and evergreen hill slopes near torrential seasonal mountain streams of the Western Ghats (elevations 60–1,100 m).

Subterranean Soil Layers

Burrows 1 to 3 metres deep underground using specialized hard spade-like hind legs.

Monsoon Stream Margins

Emerges near fast-flowing seasonal rain streams during pre-monsoon showers.

Termite Mound Pockets

Resides near underground termite nests for a continuous food supply.

Distribution

Western Ghats Range

Endemic to South-western India. Found in isolated pockets south of the Palghat Gap across Kerala (Idukki, Ernakulam, Palakkad, Wayanad) and Tamil Nadu (Anamalai, Meghamalai).

Habitat Availability

< 1,000 km²

Total estimated area of occupancy across fragmented stream catchments.

Behaviour

Fossorial Secret Life

Spends roughly 50 weeks of the year completely hidden underground. Uses its pointed snout and specialized tongue to feed on subterranean termites without opening its mouth widely.

Monsoon Mating Emergence

During pre-monsoon rains in May/June, males call from shallow burrows near stream banks producing a distinctive "quack-like" sound to attract females.

Life Cycle

1. Pre-Monsoon Mating

Amplexus occurs near turbulent streams. The female lays 3,000+ eggs in rock crevices submerged in fast-flowing water.

2. Torrential Suctorial Tadpole

Tadpoles hatch with a large oral sucker disk to anchor onto wet rock faces, feeding on algae in swift currents for 100 days.

3. Subterranean Adult

Metamorphosed froglets develop stout digging limbs and burrow underground to begin their adult fossorial life.

Conservation Status

Threats

Dam Construction & Stream Alteration

Check dams and hydroelectric barrages submerge breeding stream rocks and alter water velocity essential for suctorial tadpoles.

Agricultural Soil Compaction

Conversion of natural forests to cardamom and coffee plantations compacts soil layers, destroying underground burrow tunnels.

Monsoon Shift & Climate Change

Erratic monsoon timing disrupts the tight 2-week window available for mating and egg deposition.

Interesting Facts

01

Gondwana Witness: Its closest relatives live 3,000 miles away in the Seychelles Islands, proving continental drift.

02

Proposed State Amphibian: Conservationists in Kerala have campaigned to declare the Purple Frog as the official State Amphibian.

03

Termite Specialist: Uses a flaccid, groove-tipped tongue to swallow subterranean termites whole.

04

Waterfall Rock-Climber: Its tadpoles use sucker mouths to cling to slick, vertical waterfall rocks without getting washed away.
