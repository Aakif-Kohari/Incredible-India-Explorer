Lion-tailed Macaque (Macaca silenus)

Recognized by its striking silver-white mane and black tufted tail, the Lion-tailed Macaque is one of the world's most endangered and distinct arboreal primates, found exclusively in the dense rainforest canopies of South-western India.

Scientific Name: Macaca silenus
IUCN Status: Endangered
Habitat: Tropical Evergreen Canopy
Distribution: Western Ghats, India

Overview

Ecological Significance

The Lion-tailed Macaque (Macaca silenus), locally known as the Wandering Monkey or Sillu, is an Old World monkey endemic to the Western Ghats of South India. It acts as an umbrella species for the rainforest canopy — protecting the Macaque safeguards the broader biodiversity of tropical evergreen forests.

Unlike many other macaque species that adapt well to human-modified habitats, the Lion-tailed Macaque is a specialized arboreal frugivore that avoids open ground and relies heavily on continuous high-canopy evergreen forests.

Key Highlights

  • Endemic to India: Found only in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka.
  • Arboreal Specialist: Spends over 99% of its time high up in rainforest crowns.
  • Unique Feature: Silver-grey mane framing its dark face like a lion's mane.
  • Seed Disperser: Plays a vital role in forest regeneration by dispersing seeds of over 100 plant species.

Habitat

The Lion-tailed Macaque is confined to upper canopy layers of primary tropical wet evergreen rainforests and broadleaf Shola-forest mosaics in the Western Ghats, residing at elevations between 100 and 1,800 metres above sea level.

High Canopy Tops

Requires tall, contiguous trees (like Cullenia exarata, Ficus spp., and Artocarpus) for feeding, sleeping, and group travel.

High Humidity & Rainfall

Thrives in areas receiving annual rainfall between 2,500 mm and 5,000 mm with dense foliage and rich epiphyte growth.

Shola-Forest Mosaics

Utilizes valleys and ridge evergreen pockets where native fruiting trees remain abundant throughout seasonal changes.

Distribution

Geographic Range

The species is severely fragmented into isolated populations across three South Indian states:

  • Kerala: Silent Valley National Park, Periyar Tiger Reserve, Neyyar & Shendurney Wildlife Sanctuaries.
  • Tamil Nadu: Anamalai Tiger Reserve, Kalakad Mundanthurai Tiger Reserve (KMTR), Meghamalai.
  • Karnataka: Kudremukh National Park, Sharavathi Valley, Sirsi-Honnavar forest range.

Population Status

~2,500 – 4,000

Estimated total wild individuals remaining across fragmented forest pockets in the Western Ghats hotspot.

Physical Characteristics

Silver Mane

A striking ruff of silver-white hair surrounding the head from cheeks down to the chin, giving it a majestic lion-like appearance.

Glossy Black Coat

The body skin and fur are glossy black, providing effective camouflage within dense, shadowed rainforest tree crowns.

Lion-like Tufted Tail

A medium-length tail (25–38 cm) terminating in a black hair tassel, similar to the tail of an adult lion.

Cheek Pouches

Equipped with expansive cheek pouches that allow rapid gathering and temporary storage of fruits while foraging.

Size & Weight

Head-to-body length ranges from 42 to 61 cm. Males weigh 6–10 kg, while females are smaller at 4–6 kg.

Opposable Thumbs

Strong hands and feet with fully opposable digits built for precision grasping of branches, seeds, and slippery fruits.

Diet

The Lion-tailed Macaque is primarily a frugivorous omnivore, consuming fruits, seeds, and invertebrates harvested from the forest canopy.

Behaviour & Social Structure

Social Troop Dynamics

They live in multi-male, multi-female social groups typically numbering between 10 and 20 individuals. Troops follow a matrilineal hierarchy led by dominant adult females and an alpha male who defends the group's territory.

Inter-troop interactions involve loud, reverberating territorial calls known as "whoop calls" that carry across dense canopy distances up to 1 kilometre.

Arboreal & Diurnal Habits

Active strictly during daylight hours (diurnal), troops wake at dawn, spend hours foraging in upper branch layers, groom each other to strengthen social bonds, and sleep together high up in sturdy tree forks at night.

Conservation Status

IUCN RED LIST ENDANGERED (EN)
Wildlife Protection Act (India): Schedule I (Highest Protection)
CITES: Appendix I (International trade strictly prohibited)

Threats

Canopy Fragmentation

Roads, power lines, and tea/coffee estates bisect contiguous forests, forcing macaques to descend to the ground where they face dog attacks and vehicle collisions.

Deforestation & Dams

Hydroelectric projects and reservoir submersions have historically destroyed vast swathes of low-altitude evergreen valleys.

Poaching & Human Conflict

Illegal hunting for bushmeat or traditional remedies, alongside conflict near commercial agriculture, threatens small isolated troops.

Interesting Facts

01

Living Indicator: Presence of Lion-tailed Macaques indicates a healthy, un-fragmented rainforest ecosystem with mature fruiting canopy.

02

Canopy Leaper: They can leap distances of over 5 metres between tree crowns without descending to the forest floor.

03

Low Birth Rate: Females give birth to a single infant only once every 3 to 5 years, making population recovery extremely slow.

04

Silent Valley Symbol: The campaign to protect the Lion-tailed Macaque in the 1970s led to the historic preservation of Silent Valley National Park.
