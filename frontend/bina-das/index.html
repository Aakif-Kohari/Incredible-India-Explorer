The Student Revolutionary

Bina Das

Life Span: Aug 24, 1911 – Dec 26, 1986 Affiliation: Chhatri Sangha / Jugantar Dal Honours: Padma Shri (1960)

A defining figure of student-led militant anti-colonial resistance and women's revolutionary participation in British India.

Early Life

Bina Das was born on August 24, 1911, in Krishnanagar, Nadia district, Bengal (now West Bengal) into an intellectually progressive, nationalist Brahmo family.

  • Family Background: Her father, Beni Madhab Das, was a renowned educator and reformer who taught at Ravenshaw College. Her mother, Sarala Devi, was an ardent social reformer who ran Punya Ashram in Calcutta.
  • Early Influence: Bina grew up surrounded by patriotic literature and reformist ideals. Her elder sister, Kalyani Das (Bhattacharjee), was also an activist who played a pivotal role in organizing youth organizations.

Education & Political Awakening

Bina received her early schooling at St. John’s Diocesan Girls’ Higher Secondary School in Calcutta.

  • Chhatri Sangha: While pursuing her Bachelor of Arts at Diocesan College and Bethune College, she joined Chhatri Sangha (Women Students' Association).
  • Cadre Training: Chhatri Sangha provided women with training in physical conditioning, martial defense, lathi play, and underground political agitation.
  • Underground Links: Through Chhatri Sangha, Bina connected with the revolutionary group Jugantar, collaborating with activists like Kamala Dasgupta and Suhasini Ganguly.

The 1932 University of Calcutta Incident

On February 6, 1932, the annual Convocation of the University of Calcutta was convened at the Senate Hall. The Chancellor and Governor of Bengal, Sir Stanley Jackson, was addressing the assembly.

[Kamala Dasgupta / Jugantar] ──> Procures Revolver ──> [Bina Das] │ (Fires 5 shots at Dais) ▼ [Disarmed & Arrested]

The Attack

  • The Weapon: Bina procured a five-chambered revolver with the assistance of fellow revolutionary Kamala Dasgupta.
  • The Approach: Concealing the weapon underneath her academic convocation gown, 21-year-old Bina walked down the aisle toward the dais to receive her degree.
  • The Shots: At close range, she drew the firearm and discharged five rounds toward Governor Jackson. The governor ducked to evade the bullets, and Vice-Chancellor Lt. Col. Hassan Suhrawardy lunged forward to physically restrain and disarm her.
  • The Capture: Bina was wrestled to the ground and taken into custody. No fatal injuries were sustained by the governor.

Trial & Imprisonment

The British administration initiated a high-profile, expedited trial under emergency tribunal rules.

  • The Famous Statement: In a five-page written confession, Bina stated: "My object was to die, and if to die, to die nobly fighting against this despotic system of Government."
  • Sentencing: Bina was convicted of attempted murder and sentenced to nine years of rigorous imprisonment.
  • Withheld Degree: The University of Calcutta canceled her academic credentials and withheld her BA Honours degree.

Later Political Life & Post-Independence

  • Quit India Movement: Released early from prison in 1939, Bina joined the Indian National Congress. She was arrested again during the Quit India Movement (1942) and imprisoned until 1945.
  • Parliamentary Career: She was elected to the Bengal Provincial Legislative Assembly (1946), and later served in the West Bengal Legislative Assembly (1947 to 1951).
  • Memoirs: She penned significant autobiographical works in Bengali, including Shrinkhal Jhankar (The Clanking of Chains).

Women in Bengal's Revolutionary Movement

Bina Das belonged to an extraordinary cohort of women in 1930s Bengal who challenged both colonial authority and conservative social expectations:

  • Armed Vanguard: Alongside Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Datta, and Santi Ghose & Suniti Choudhury, Bina demonstrated tactical autonomy.
  • Organizational Core: Networks like Chhatri Sangha and Dipali Sangha served as training grounds, creating institutional pathways for female leadership.

Timeline
