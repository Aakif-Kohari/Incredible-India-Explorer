Chaul

The Medieval Konkan Stronghold of Maritime Trade

Historical Background

Chaul is an ancient port town located in the Raigad district of Maharashtra, near the modern town of Revdanda. For centuries, it was one of the most important natural harbors on the west coast of India. Known to Greek and Roman geographers as 'Symylla' or 'Tiz', it thrived as a major center for the spice and textile trade.

During the medieval period, Chaul was ruled by the Bahmani Sultanate and later the Ahmadnagar Sultanate. Its strategic location made it a highly contested prize among the regional Indian powers, the Portuguese, and later the Marathas. The port witnessed intense naval battles, notably between the Mamluk Ali Hussain and the Portuguese in the 16th century.

Trade Importance

Chaul was a vital hub for Arab merchants who traded extensively in pepper, cardamom, and textiles. After the Portuguese captured Goa in 1510, they sought control over Chaul to monopolize the Arabian Sea trade routes. The port handled massive volumes of imports, particularly Arabian war horses, which were essential for the cavalry of the Deccan Sultanates.

Arabian Sea Network

Direct links to Aden, Hormuz, and the Persian Gulf.

Horse Trade

Import of thousands of Arab and Persian horses annually.

Major Monuments

  • Revdanda Fort: A massive coastal fort built by the Portuguese in the 16th century, featuring impressive bastions facing the sea.
  • Korlai Fort: Located across the creek, guarding the harbor entrance. It features the historic Korlai Portuguese Creole church.
  • Chaul City Walls: Remnants of the ancient fortified city that once surrounded the port.

Historical Timeline
