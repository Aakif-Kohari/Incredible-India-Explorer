The Trophy & Significance

The Vijay Hazare Trophy, officially known as the Ranji One-Day Trophy, is the premier List A cricket tournament in India. Contested annually by state teams from the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI), lifting the glittering trophy is the ultimate benchmark of limited-overs excellence in domestic Indian cricket.

The tournament serves as the primary breeding ground for identifying explosive openers, versatile all-rounders, and tactical white-ball bowlers destined for national team selection.

The Namesake: Vijay Hazare

Named in honor of Vijay Samuel Hazare, one of India's greatest pre-and-post independence cricket legends, captain, and prolific batsman. Hazare was renowned for his immense concentration, technically flawless stroke play, and historic achievement of scoring two centuries in a single Test match against Australia in Adelaide (1947–48).

He led India in its first-ever Test match victory against England in 1952 at Madras. Naming the national one-day championship after him immortalizes his remarkable contribution to Indian batting grit and leadership.

Tournament History & Format

Inception (2002–03)

Established to introduce regional and state teams to limited-overs 50-over cricket, mirroring international One Day International (ODI) standards.

Group & Knockout Format

Teams are divided into elite groups and plates, competing in a rigorous round-robin league phase followed by high-stakes knockout quarter-finals, semi-finals, and the grand finale.

List A Status

Every match played in the tournament holds official List A status, ensuring comprehensive domestic statistical tracking for all participating players.

Champions Timeline & Notable Winners

2010s Era

2000s Era

The formative years of the tournament where traditional domestic giants adapted quickly to the 50-over white-ball format.
