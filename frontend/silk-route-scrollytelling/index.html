Historical context

Not one road, but a route network

The Silk Roads were a changing network of trade, pilgrimage, diplomatic, and cultural corridors. This story shows one India-connected narrative path, not a single fixed road.

Source note: Historical route geography varies across centuries. This feature uses a simplified educational route and clearly labels it as a narrative reconstruction.

Goods and cultural exchange

What moved along the corridors?

Goods

Textiles, spices, precious stones, horses, salt, wool, silk, manuscripts, coins, and craft objects moved through different route segments.

Ideas

Merchants, monks, scholars, and envoys helped carry Buddhism, art styles, scripts, languages, and scientific knowledge across regions.

People

Caravan leaders, artisans, pilgrims, rulers, translators, and monastic communities shaped exchange beyond simple trade.

Data & Sourcing Notes

This feature is a simplified educational scrollytelling map. Silk Route paths changed by century, climate, politics, and terrain. Source basis includes UNESCO Silk Roads material describing trade routes, merchants, Buddhism's spread, Indian connections through Himalayan corridors, and the need to treat Silk Roads as networks rather than a single road.
