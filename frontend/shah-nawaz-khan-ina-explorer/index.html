BIOGRAPHY

From British Indian officer to INA commander

Shah Nawaz Khan was born in 1914 and served as an officer in the British Indian Army. During the Second World War he became a prisoner of war in Southeast Asia and joined the Indian National Army, the force associated with Subhas Chandra Bose and the Provisional Government of Free India.

Within the INA, Khan became one of its senior battlefield commanders. His wartime service placed him among the officers who fought against British imperial forces in the Burma theatre and northeastern India.

“

The INA experience turned military service into a direct political and anti-colonial project.

Historical context

INA CAMPAIGNS

Service on the Burma and northeastern fronts

The INA fought alongside Japanese forces during the Burma campaign and the 1944 offensive toward northeastern India. Khan became a senior commander during this phase.

01

Joining the INA

After becoming a prisoner of war in Southeast Asia, Khan joined the INA rather than return to service under the British Indian command.

02

Burma theatre

INA units operated in Burma as part of the wider Japanese offensive against Allied forces.

03

Imphal–Kohima offensive

The INA participated in the 1944 campaign toward India's northeastern frontier. The offensive ultimately failed to achieve its objectives.

04

Senior battlefield command

Khan was among the INA's senior officers and later became one of the most prominent defendants of the post-war trials.

RED FORT TRIAL · 1945

The trial that made the INA a national political issue

The first and most famous INA trial was the joint court-martial of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon at Delhi's Red Fort. The proceedings ran from November into December 1945.

01

Capture

INA officers were captured after the end of the Burma campaign and taken into British custody.

→
02

Charges

The defendants faced the charge of waging war against the King-Emperor; additional allegations were also brought against individual defendants.

→
03

Public trial

The proceedings were held publicly at the Red Fort, giving the case enormous political visibility.

→
04

National response

Congress organised an INA Defence Committee and public sympathy for the defendants became an important feature of the independence movement.

Outcome

The three were found guilty, but their sentences were remitted and they were released. The trial helped transform the INA defendants into major symbols in the final phase of India's independence struggle.
