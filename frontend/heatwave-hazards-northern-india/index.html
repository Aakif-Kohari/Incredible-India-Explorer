Heatwave Hazards Extreme Summer Temperatures & Climatic Risks Across Northern India

As one of South Asia's most prominent environmental hazards, summer heatwaves across Northern India impact hundreds of millions of people. Driven by dry westerly winds, proximity to the Thar Desert, and the concrete-heavy urban structures of modern metropolitan centers, these extreme heat hazards present significant challenges to public health, agriculture, and water sustainability.

Climatic Definition

Extreme Heatwave Hazards in the Indo-Gangetic Basin

According to the India Meteorological Department (IMD), a heatwave is declared over plains when the maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C, and departs by 4.5°C to 6.4°C above normal. These extreme events occur pre-monsoon, creating dry convective drafts and high-exposure dangers for urban and rural populations alike.

Geographic Vulnerability

Core Exposure Regions

Heatwaves are not uniform; they peak across specific pathways, including the plains of Rajasthan, the concrete basins of Delhi NCR, and the fertile fields of Uttar Pradesh and Punjab.

🏜️ Arid Plains

Rajasthan & Thar Outskirts

The epicentre of dry convective heating, with temperatures regularly crossing 47°C, fueled by dry desert sand absorption.

🏢 Urban Centers

Delhi NCR & Lucknow Basin

Concrete structures block breeze pathways and trap solar heat, creating severe urban heat islands that remain hot overnight.
