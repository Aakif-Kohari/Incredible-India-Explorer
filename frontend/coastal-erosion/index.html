What is Coastal Erosion?

Natural Processes

Coastal erosion is the wearing away of land and removal of beach or sand dune sediments by wave action, tidal currents, wave currents, or drainage. It's a natural process that has shaped coastlines for millions of years.

  • Wave Action: Constant battering by waves breaks down rocks and carries sediment away
  • Tidal Currents: Rising and falling tides create strong currents that transport sediment
  • Longshore Drift: Waves approaching at an angle move sediment along the coast
  • Storm Surges: Extreme weather events cause rapid, severe erosion

Human Acceleration

While erosion is natural, human activities have significantly accelerated the process along India's coastline, leading to loss of land, property, and ecosystems.

  • Coastal Construction: Buildings and infrastructure disrupt natural sediment flow
  • Sand Mining: Extraction of beach sand for construction reduces natural buffers
  • Deforestation: Removal of mangroves and coastal vegetation eliminates natural protection
  • Sea Level Rise: Climate change causes oceans to rise, intensifying erosion

Coastal Management Strategies

🏗️

Hard Engineering

Physical structures to prevent erosion and protect coastlines

  • Seawalls: Concrete barriers that reflect wave energy
  • Groynes: Perpendicular structures that trap sediment
  • Breakwaters: Offshore structures that reduce wave energy
  • Revetments: Sloped structures that absorb wave impact
🌿

Soft Engineering

Working with natural processes to manage coastal erosion

  • Beach Nourishment: Adding sand to eroded beaches
  • Mangrove Restoration: Planting mangroves as natural barriers
  • Dune Restoration: Rebuilding sand dunes with vegetation
  • Managed Retreat: Relocating infrastructure away from vulnerable areas
📋

Policy & Regulation

Legal frameworks and planning for sustainable coastal management

  • CRZ Regulations: Coastal Regulation Zone notifications
  • ICZM: Integrated Coastal Zone Management
  • Setback Lines: No-build zones near the coast
  • Environmental Impact Assessment: Evaluating development impacts
