Sanchi Stupa India's Ancient Buddhist Sanctuary

Commissioned in the 3rd century BCE by Mauryan Emperor Ashoka the Great, the Great Stupa at Sanchi is India's oldest surviving stone structure. Standing atop a serene hill overlooking the Betwa Valley, its four intricately sculpted Toranas chronicle the life, enlightenment, and Jataka tales of Gautama Buddha.

Buddhist Monuments at Sanchi

Ancient Monastic Haven

Buddhist Heritage & Emperor Ashoka

The hill of Sanchi (anciently known as Kakanaya or Chetiyagiri) was chosen by Emperor Ashoka the Great in the 3rd century BCE as a primary monastic sanctuary for the preservation and propagation of the Buddha's Dhamma.

Unlike other sacred Buddhist sites (Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar), Sanchi was never directly visited by Gautama Buddha during his lifetime. Ashoka selected this location because of its tranquil hilltop seclusion situated just 9 km from Vidisha—a bustling trade metropolis and the hometown of his beloved queen, Devi (Mahadevi), who was a devout Buddhist.

Ashoka constructed the core brick stupa over sacred corporeal relics (dhatu) of the Buddha, erected a gleaming monolithic polished sandstone Ashoka Pillar crowned with four lions, and founded a flourishing monastic academy that endured for over 1,300 years.

Location: Raisen District, 46 km NE of Bhopal, MP
Key Dynasties: Maurya, Shunga, Satavahana, Gupta
Primary Stone: Local Grey-Brown Sandstone
Discovery: General Henry Taylor (1818 CE)
The Great Stupa 1: India's iconic hemispherical stone monument flanked by ornate Torana gateways.
Sacred Cosmic Geometry

Architecture of the Great Stupa (Stupa 1)

The Great Stupa is an architectural diagram of Buddhist cosmology, designed to elevate the pilgrim from worldly existence to spiritual transcendence through ritual circumambulation.

01

Anda (The Hemispherical Mound)

The massive hemispherical dome (Anda, literally "egg") symbolizing the cosmic vault of heaven and the womb of universe. Originally a low brick mound built by Ashoka, it was doubled in size and encased in dressed sandstone blocks during the Shunga period (2nd century BCE), reaching 16.5m in height and 36.6m in diameter.

02

Harmika (The Sacred Balcony)

Perched atop the crest of the dome is the square stone railing known as the Harmika. It represents the dwelling place of the gods and fences off the most holy focal point directly above the relic chamber beneath.

03

Chhatra & Yasti (The Three Jewels)

Rising from the center of the Harmika is a central stone mast (Yasti) supporting the Chhatravali—a triple-tiered stone umbrella symbolizing the Triratna (Three Jewels): the Buddha, the Dharma (Teachings), and the Sangha (Monastic Community), as well as royal spiritual sovereignty.

04

Pradakshina Patha & Vedika

Two concentric circular walkways (Pradakshina Patha)—one at ground level and an elevated terrace (Medhi) accessed by a double staircase—surrounded by a heavy timber-mimicking stone balustrade (Vedika) for clockwise meditative circumambulation.

Satavahana Masterpieces

The Four Elaborately Carved Toranas

Added in the 1st century BCE/CE during the Satavahana era, the four ceremonial gateways face the cardinal directions (North, South, East, West). Inscribed records reveal they were crafted by the guild of ivory carvers of Vidisha, explaining their delicate micro-relief precision.

South Gateway

The Oldest Gateway

The earliest of the four Toranas, intimately linked to Emperor Ashoka. The architraves depict the Birth of Buddha, the War over the Relics between eight kings, Ashoka's royal visit to the Ramagrama Stupa, and the celebrated bracket Yakshis.

First Built · Ashokan Themes
North Gateway

The Best Preserved

The most intact of all gateways, crowned by the Wheel of Law (Dharmachakra). It depicts the Vessantara Jataka (the prince who gave away all possessions), the Chaddanta Jataka (the six-tusked elephant), and the Great Miracle of Sravasti.

Best Intact · Jataka Tales
East Gateway

The Great Departure

Famous worldwide for its iconic bracket figure of the Shalabhanjika / Yakshi tree spirit hanging gracefully from a mango bough. The central architrave depicts Prince Siddhartha's Great Departure (Mahabhinishkramana) from Kapilavastu.

Iconic Yakshi Sculpture
West Gateway

The Mahakapi Jataka

Supported by four dwarf atlantes (Ganas / Yakshas). Depicts the Mahakapi Jataka (the Bodhisattva as a monkey king saving his troop across the Ganges), the temptation and assault of Mara, and Buddha preaching to the Shakya clan.

Dwarf Atlas Columns
Visual Vocabulary of Early Buddhism

Aniconic Buddhist Symbolism & Jataka Stories

In early Theravada art at Sanchi, the Buddha was never depicted in human form. Instead, his presence was indicated through sacred aniconic symbols of spiritual liberation.

☸️

Dharmachakra (Wheel of Law)

Represents the Buddha's First Sermon at Deer Park in Sarnath ("Turning the Wheel of Dhamma") and universal cosmic order.

👣

Buddhapada (Sacred Footprints)

Inscribed with auspicious chakras and lotus marks, symbolizing the Buddha's physical passage on Earth and his transcendent spiritual presence.

🌳

Bodhi Tree & Empty Throne

The Ficus religiosa tree and vacant stone seat underneath depict Gautama Buddha's Enlightenment (Bodhi) under the tree at Bodh Gaya.

🔱

Triratna (Three Jewels)

A trident-shaped symbol signifying the core foundation of Buddhism: the Buddha (Enlightened One), Dharma (Law), and Sangha (Community).

🐘

Queen Maya's Dream

A white elephant descending from the heavens to enter the womb of Queen Maya, heralding the conception of the Bodhisattva.

🪷

Lotus & Kalash

The lotus rising unstained through muddy waters signifies spiritual purity, detachment, and supreme enlightenment.

The Wider Monastic Complex

Surrounding Stupas, Temples & Ashoka Pillar

Beyond the Great Stupa, Sanchi is surrounded by over 50 ancient monuments spanning fifteen centuries of architectural evolution.

🪦

Stupa 2 (Shunga Reliquary)

Perched on the western slope, Stupa 2 dates to the late 2nd century BCE. Its balustrades feature the earliest surviving narrative stone reliefs in Indian art, with motifs of elephants, lotus medallions, and Greek-influenced centaurs.

✨

Stupa 3 (Relics of Chief Disciples)

Located near Stupa 1, this stupa enshrined the sacred bone relics of Buddha's foremost disciples, Sariputta and Mahamoggallana, discovered by Alexander Cunningham in 1851 inside inscribed steatite caskets.

🏛️

Temple 17 (Early Gupta Classical Temple)

Dating to the 5th century CE, this jewel of classical Gupta architecture is one of India's earliest structural temples, featuring a flat roof, four carved pillars, and a square sanctum (garbhagriha).

🦁

Ashoka Pillar 25 & Monasteries

The polished Chunar sandstone Ashoka pillar originally bore four back-to-back lions and Ashoka's schism edict. Nearby lie extensive remains of Monasteries 45 & 51 with monk cells and courtyards.

Fifteen Centuries of History

Historical Timeline of Sanchi

From the Mauryan Empire to colonial rediscovery and modern restoration.

c. 250 BCE

Mauryan Era: Ashoka Founds Sanchi

Emperor Ashoka commissions the core brick stupa, pillar, and monastery at Sanchi with his queen Devi of Vidisha.

c. 150 BCE

Shunga Period: Stone Encasement & Expansion

The Great Stupa is doubled in size, encased in sandstone masonry, and enclosed with the heavy stone Vedika railing.

c. 50–1 BCE

Satavahana Era: Erection of Four Toranas

The four elaborately sculpted gateways are carved by Vidisha's ivory artisans and dedicated by Satavahana patrons.

c. 450 CE

Gupta Period: Temple 17 & Buddha Statues

Four seated stone Buddha statues are placed facing the four cardinal Toranas; classical structural temples are constructed.

1818 CE

British Rediscovery by General Taylor

British cavalry officer General Henry Taylor discovers the overgrown, forgotten monuments of Sanchi preserved by dense jungle.

1912–1919 CE

Sir John Marshall's Scientific Restoration

Director General of the ASI Sir John Marshall meticulously clears, documents, and restores the Great Stupa and opens the on-site museum.

1989 CE

Inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Site

Sanchi is designated a UNESCO World Heritage site for its unique testimony to Buddhist art and architecture.

🏛️ UNESCO World Heritage Site · 1989

UNESCO World Heritage Significance

The Buddhist Monuments at Sanchi are universally recognized as the oldest surviving Buddhist sanctuary, containing the finest ensemble of early Indian art and architecture anywhere in the world.

Artistic Masterpiece

The Toranas represent the supreme achievement of early Indian sculpture, bridging ivory miniature carving with monumental architecture.

Direct Ashokan Association

Directly connects to Emperor Ashoka's monumental patronage and the global spread of Buddhist thought across Asia.

Architectural Evolution

Preserves a continuous 1,500-year sequence of Buddhist art, from Mauryan origins to the mature classical Gupta temple form.

Did You Know?

Fascinating Facts About Sanchi

01

Ivory Guild Inscription

An ancient Brahmi inscription on the South Torana records that it was carved by the guild of ivory workers of nearby Vidisha (Vedisakehi damtakarehi).

02

Never Visited by Buddha

Remarkably, despite being India's greatest Buddhist architectural site, Gautama Buddha never set foot in Sanchi during his lifetime.

03

Saved by Dense Jungle

When Buddhism declined in central India after the 12th century, Sanchi was swallowed by dense forests, saving it from medieval destruction until 1818.

04

Featured on Indian Currency

The iconic Great Stupa of Sanchi is honored on the reverse side of the modern ₹200 Indian Rupee currency banknote.
