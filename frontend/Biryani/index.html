Biryani

One dish, many regional identities.

From the layered, saffron-scented biryanis of Hyderabad and Lucknow to Kolkata's potato-and-meat combination and Kerala's Malabar traditions, biryani changes with place, pantry and cooking practice.

The dish

What is biryani?

Biryani is a family of fragrant rice dishes in which rice is cooked with seasoned meat, fish, eggs, vegetables or a combination of ingredients. Regional traditions differ in how the rice is prepared, when the protein is added, how strongly the dish is spiced, and whether the pot is finished by dum, a sealed slow-cooking method.

There is no single “original” regional recipe represented here. The map is designed to show how communities developed recognizable local styles rather than to rank one biryani above another.

🍚

Shared foundations

  • Long-grain or local rice varieties
  • Whole and ground spices
  • Layering, mixing or one-pot cooking
  • Slow cooking in many traditional styles

Regional varieties

Six traditions to explore

01

Hyderabadi

Kachhi and pakki traditions, dum cooking, aromatic spices, meat and basmati rice.

02

Lakhnavi / Awadhi

A refined courtly tradition associated with Lucknow, with stock-cooked rice and gentle aromatics.

03

Kolkata

A distinctive style known for fragrant rice, meat and the familiar presence of potato.

04

Malabar

Kerala's northern coast has its own rice, spice and meat traditions, often using short-grain rice such as jeerakasala.

05

Ambur

A Tamil Nadu tradition associated with Ambur, using seeraga samba rice and a distinctive chilli-forward profile.

06

Mumbai

Mumbai's biryani culture reflects the city's layered food history and the many communities that cook and eat it.

The pantry

Ingredients change the identity

Rice is only the beginning. Regional biryanis can use mutton, chicken, fish, prawns, eggs or vegetables, alongside yogurt, stock, ghee, saffron, rose or kewra water, chillies, mint, coriander and different combinations of whole spices.

Cooking traditions

Different paths to the same pot

Kachhi

Marinated meat is cooked together with rice, as in a traditional Hyderabadi kachhi preparation.

Pakki

Cooked meat and partially cooked rice are layered before the final steaming stage.

Dum

A sealed vessel traps steam and aroma while the dish finishes slowly over controlled heat.

Local rice

Not every regional tradition depends on basmati; southern styles may favor smaller-grained aromatic rice.

Cultural history

A dish shaped by movement

  1. Persianate culinary influenceRice, meat, aromatics and layered cooking traditions developed through long histories of cultural exchange across South and Central Asia.
  2. Royal and urban kitchensCourtly cuisines helped refine techniques and ingredients, while cities and trade routes carried recipes beyond palace kitchens.
  3. Regional adaptationLocal rice, produce, spice preferences and cooking equipment turned shared ideas into distinct regional biryanis.
  4. Living food heritageToday biryani belongs to homes, restaurants, festivals and everyday food culture across many parts of India.
