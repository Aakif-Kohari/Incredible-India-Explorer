🛵 Bajaj Auto: The World's Favourite Indian

Explore the legacy of Bajaj Auto, from the iconic "Hamara Bajaj" scooters that mobilized a nation, to its emergence as a global motorcycle powerhouse.

Origins: Mobilizing India

Founded in 1945 by Jamnalal Bajaj, the company initially imported and sold two- and three-wheelers in India. In 1959, Bajaj Auto secured a license from the Government of India to manufacture two-wheelers and three-wheelers, partnering with Piaggio. This laid the foundation for a two-wheeler revolution in India, making affordable personal transportation a reality for millions of middle-class families.

"Hamara Bajaj" & Brand Identity

In the late 1980s, the "Hamara Bajaj" (Our Bajaj) advertising campaign became a cultural phenomenon, deeply associating the brand with Indian family values, pride, and national identity. Today, the brand has evolved from a traditional scooter manufacturer into a dynamic, performance-oriented motorcycle company with a massive global footprint.

Chronological Two-Wheeler Journey

Scroll through the iconic vehicles that defined generations of riders.

1960s

Bajaj Vespa 150

Manufactured under license from Piaggio, this early scooter was the beginning of India's love affair with the geared scooter, setting the stage for Bajaj's future dominance.

1972

Bajaj Chetak

The iconic Chetak, named after Maharana Pratap's legendary horse, was an affordable, robust scooter. It had a massive waiting period (sometimes up to 10 years!) and became the ultimate symbol of middle-class mobility.

1986

Bajaj M-80

A rugged step-through motorcycle designed for both urban and rural utility. Known for its payload capacity and durability, it became a favorite among delivery agents and farmers.

2001

Bajaj Pulsar

A revolutionary launch that transformed the Indian motorcycle market. The Pulsar introduced accessible performance, muscular styling, and advanced features, shifting the focus from mere fuel efficiency to power and thrill.

2004

Bajaj Discover

Targeting the executive commuter segment, the Discover combined the performance traits of the Pulsar with the fuel efficiency of commuter bikes, becoming a massive volume driver for the company.

2012

Bajaj Dominar 400

Entering the premium touring segment, the Dominar 400 offered a power cruiser experience. It demonstrated Bajaj's capability to build large-capacity, feature-rich motorcycles for long-distance riding.

2020

Chetak EV

The legendary Chetak name was resurrected for Bajaj's first electric scooter. It features a premium all-metal body, classic styling, and modern connected EV technology, marking Bajaj's entry into the green mobility era.

Three-Wheelers & Manufacturing Prowess

Bajaj Auto is not just a two-wheeler giant; it is the world's largest manufacturer of three-wheelers (auto rickshaws). The ubiquitous Bajaj RE (Rear Engine) auto rickshaw forms the backbone of last-mile public transport across India and numerous developing nations. With state-of-the-art manufacturing facilities in Waluj, Chakan, and Pantnagar, Bajaj continues to be a powerhouse of Indian engineering.
