01 · Geography

An old, steep wall along the coast

The Western Ghats run roughly parallel to India's west coast from the Tapi River near the Gujarat–Maharashtra border to Kanyakumari at the southern tip — a nearly continuous escarpment older than the Himalayas.

  • ExtentRuns through Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Kerala — a near-continuous chain hugging the Arabian Sea coastline.
  • ProfileA steep, faulted western (windward) escarpment drops sharply to the coastal plain; the eastern slope descends far more gently onto the Deccan Plateau.
  • ElevationPeaks exceed 2,000 m in the south, topping out at Anamudi (2,695 m) in Kerala — the highest point in India outside the Himalayas.
  • RecognitionInscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012 for outstanding biodiversity and endemism; one of the world's eight "hottest" biodiversity hotspots.
  • HydrologyActs as the source of peninsular India's major west- and east-flowing rivers, and as the barrier that shapes the entire southwest monsoon.
Schematic cross-section — the escarpment is steep and short on the sea-facing side, gentle and long on the plateau side. This asymmetry funnels moist air upward fast, which matters later.
02 · Geological conditions

Hard rock, soft skin

The bedrock is old and strong. The problem sits in the metre or two above it — a weathered, water-loving crust that the tropics have been softening for millions of years.

Topsoil & roots

A thin living skin

Forest and plantation roots bind a shallow organic layer to the slope. It is rarely more than a metre deep — nowhere near enough to anchor what lies beneath it.

Deccan Trap basalt

Layered basalt flows erupted roughly 66 million years ago, fractured by vertical cooling joints that act as ready-made channels for infiltrating rain.

South of Goa

Crystalline gneiss & charnockite

Ancient Precambrian rock across Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, deeply weathered by heat and humidity into thick, unstable regolith.

Everywhere

The laterite–saprolite contact

Millions of years of tropical weathering built a porous, iron-rich laterite crust over a weaker, less permeable layer beneath — the boundary where slopes most often let go.

03 · Monsoon rainfall

One coastline, two climates

The southwest monsoon (June–September) slams moist Arabian Sea air straight into the escarpment. Forced upward, it dumps most of its moisture on the windward face — while the eastern, rain-shadow side barely notices.

Hover a bar for its share of monthly rainfall. Windward stations can exceed 7,000 mm a year; leeward towns on the plateau side may see a fraction of that.

Since around 2018, the character of the monsoon over the Ghats has shifted: instead of steady, spread-out rain, the region increasingly gets short, extremely intense bursts. Slope failure depends less on the monthly total than on how fast water arrives — 50 mm in one hour behaves nothing like 50 mm spread over a day, even though the daily total is identical.

That intensity is what turns an ordinary monsoon into a disaster year:

  • 201836% above-normal seasonal rainfall in Kerala; 4,728 landslides recorded and 48+ deaths in a single monsoon.
  • 2019 – 2021Repeated high-intensity bursts across Kerala push the multi-year death toll from monsoon landslides past 100.
  • 2020Pettimudi, Idukki: a single downpour tears a 1,200 m scar through Shola forest and tea estate housing.
  • 2024Wayanad: roughly 570 mm falls in 48 hours before three landslides strike Mundakkai and Chooralmala within four hours.

05 · Infrastructure risks

What sits in the way

The Ghats are not empty wilderness — highways, railways, dams, and plantation settlements all occupy the same slopes that fail.

Roads & highways

Severed corridors

Hairpin ghat roads and national highways through the range are routinely blocked or washed out, cutting off entire towns for days during peak monsoon.

Rail

The Konkan line

The Konkan Railway threads the western escarpment and requires continual slope-stabilisation work to keep cuttings and embankments from failing onto the tracks.

Dams & reservoirs

Catchments under strain

Hydropower reservoirs such as Idukki sit inside landslide-prone catchments, where debris and sediment inflow threaten storage capacity and spillway operation.

Plantation housing

Built on the slope itself

Tea and cardamom estate labour lines are often sited directly below or on converted steep ground — most of the 2024 Wayanad victims were estate workers asleep in worker housing when the slope failed.

Quarrying

Removing the toe support

Unregulated stone quarrying and slope-cutting for construction remove the material that physically holds a slope's base in place, and alter natural drainage in the process.

River systems

Downstream shock

Debris flows choke rivers below the failure — after Wayanad 2024, the Chaliyar river carried landslide debris and bodies far downstream of the source.

06 · Environmental impacts

A hazard that reshapes the hotspot it happens in

The Western Ghats are one of the world's biodiversity hotspots. Landslides are both a symptom of ecological change here and a cause of further damage.

Cause

Forest-to-plantation conversion

Replacing native shola forest and grassland with tea, coffee, cardamom, and rubber reduces root cohesion and rainfall interception, raising a slope's susceptibility — a factor identified in multiple Kerala landslide studies.

Cause

Forest fragmentation

Breaking continuous forest into patches concentrates runoff along new edges and roads, instead of letting a dense canopy and root mat absorb it gradually.

Effect

Smothered streams

Debris and sediment from a single large slide can bury riparian habitat and choke rivers for kilometres downstream, altering channels long after the rain stops.

Effect

Habitat and endemism loss

Because the range holds unusually high species endemism, even localised slope failures can remove habitat found nowhere else on Earth.

Compounding

A warming atmosphere

A warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, giving monsoon weather systems a larger reservoir to draw on — one reason short, extremely intense rainfall bursts have become more common in recent years.

Compounding

Repeat failures

A slope that has already failed once is often left more unstable than before, raising the odds of a secondary slide in a following monsoon on the same hillside.

07 · Early-warning systems

Watching the slope before it moves

The Geological Survey of India (GSI) has been the country's nodal agency for landslide hazard since 2004. What began as static hazard maps is slowly becoming a real-time forecasting network.

2004

GSI named nodal agency

Government of India designates GSI as the nodal agency for landslide investigation, hazard zonation, and mitigation strategy nationwide.

2016 – 2021

Regional forecasting prototype

GSI and the British Geological Survey co-develop a rainfall-threshold landslide early warning prototype, piloted in the Nilgiris (Western Ghats) and Darjeeling.

2024

National Landslide Forecasting Centre

GSI launches the NLFC in Kolkata along with the "Bhusanket" web portal and "Bhooskhalan" mobile app, issuing experimental daily forecast bulletins to state authorities — operational just weeks before the Wayanad disaster.

2019 – 2025

Kerala's SLIP-K system

A real-time, physics-based slope-stability model combined with empirical rainfall thresholds from automated weather stations, built specifically for Idukki district's chronic instability.

2025

National susceptibility mapping

GSI completes large-scale mapping of roughly 4.3 lakh sq. km across 19 states and union territories, including the Western Ghats, with a database of some 91,000 historical landslides.

2026 onward

Phased national rollout

GSI targets extending the regional early-warning prototype — currently experimental in 21 districts across 8 states — to the rest of the country's landslide-prone terrain in phases.

08 · Risk-reduction measures

What actually lowers the risk

No measure stops the monsoon. The goal is to keep people, roads, and buildings out of the way of what the monsoon will keep doing anyway.

Zn

Susceptibility-led land-use planning

Use GSI's 1:50,000-scale susceptibility zonation to steer building codes and settlement approvals away from high-risk laterite-capped slopes, rather than mapping hazards only after they've already been built on.

Fo

Protect and restore natural cover

Retain shola forest and natural drainage lines rather than converting them to monoculture plantation; root cohesion and canopy interception measurably slow how fast a slope saturates.

Qr

Regulate quarrying and slope-cutting

Restrict quarrying, road-widening cuts, and toe excavation in zones already flagged as highly susceptible, since removing material at a slope's base is one of the most direct ways to trigger failure.

Mn

Densify monitoring networks

Expand rain-gauge, soil-moisture, and slope-movement instrumentation beyond today's pilot districts so GSI's forecasting centre has real-time data across the full length of the Ghats, not just Nilgiris.

Cb

Community-level warning and evacuation

Pair technical forecasts with local alert systems and rehearsed evacuation routes in chronic hotspot villages — the gap between a bulletin and an actual evacuation is where lives are lost.

En

Engineering & bioengineering at chronic sites

Retaining structures, slope drainage, and root-mat stabilisation at the roughly 45 chronic sites GSI has studied in detail since 2019, alongside relocation where a site is judged unsalvageable.
