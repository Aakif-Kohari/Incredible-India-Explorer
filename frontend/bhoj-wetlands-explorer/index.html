History & Raja Bhoj's Legacy

The origins of the Bhoj Wetlands trace back to the visionary engineering of the Paramara dynasty.

The Upper Lake (Bada Talab) was constructed in the 11th century by Raja Bhoj, the legendary king of the Paramara dynasty, to meet the water needs of his capital, Bhojpal. It is one of the oldest artificial lakes in India.

The Lower Lake (Chhota Talab) was created later, in 1794, by Nawab Chhote Khan, an official of the Nawab of Bhopal, by constructing a dam across the natural drainage of the Upper Lake.

Today, these twin lakes form the Bhoj Wetland, a testament to ancient Indian water management and a critical lifeline for the city of Bhopal, providing drinking water to over a million residents.

Raja Bhoj's Vision

"A king should build a lake, for it is the lifeblood of the land." This ancient principle guided the creation of what is now a thriving urban ecosystem.

Global Recognition

Ramsar Site Designation

Recognized in 2002, the Bhoj Wetland is a Wetland of International Importance under the Ramsar Convention.

The Bhoj Wetland was designated as a Ramsar Site in 2002, acknowledging its critical role in supporting biodiversity and providing essential ecosystem services to the urban population of Bhopal.

The site meets Ramsar criteria for supporting vulnerable species and maintaining the ecological character of the region. It serves as a crucial stopover for migratory birds along the Central Asian Flyway.

Protection

Water Conservation

Ongoing efforts to protect and restore the ecological health of the twin lakes.

Conservation efforts include the prevention of sewage inflow, afforestation of the catchment area, and regular water quality monitoring. The Bhopal Municipal Corporation and the Madhya Pradesh State Pollution Control Board work collaboratively to manage the wetland.

Community involvement is encouraged through awareness campaigns and the promotion of sustainable tourism practices around the lake periphery.
