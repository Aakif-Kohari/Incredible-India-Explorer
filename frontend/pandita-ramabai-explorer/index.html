Pandita Ramabai

Pioneering Scholar, Educator & Reformer (1858–1922)

Pandita Ramabai was one of the most important pioneers of women's education, social reform, and intellectual scholarship in 19th-century India. Recognized by the Calcutta Senate as a master Sanskrit scholar, she traveled globally to build networks of support for child-widows. Returning to India, she founded the *Sharada Sadan*, the nation's first secular school for widowed girls, and later built the self-sufficient *Mukti Mission* in Kedgaon to house and educate thousands of famine survivors, leaving an immortal legacy of scholarship and feminist resistance.
