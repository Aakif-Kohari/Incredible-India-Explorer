A shared ritual

Chai is more than a beverage

India's tea culture changes with local ingredients, climate, work, hospitality and household habits. There is no single Indian chai.

01

Everyday tea

Tea is commonly part of daily routines, from a first cup at home to a quick glass at a neighbourhood stall.

02

Hospitality

Offering tea is a familiar way to welcome guests and create time for conversation in homes, offices and shops.

03

Street-side life

Tea stalls can become informal meeting points where workers, travellers, neighbours and friends pause together.

04

Regional identity

Tea-growing landscapes influence the character of local cups, from Darjeeling's fragrance to Assam's robust black tea.

Serving practices

The ritual is part of the taste

01

Glass & kulhad

Tea may be served in glasses, cups or traditional clay kulhads depending on setting and region.

02

Small pours

Street-side tea is often served in compact portions for a quick pause and conversation.

03

With a snack

Biscuits, pakoras, samosas, buns and regional savouries commonly accompany tea.

04

Shared time

Tea creates a reason to sit together: family conversations, office breaks and visits with guests.
