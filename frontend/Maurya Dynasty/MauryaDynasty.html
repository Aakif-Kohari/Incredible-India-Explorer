lohara Dynasty

The Lohara Dynasty ruled Kashmir for more than three centuries, beginning in 1003 CE with Samgrāmarāja. Emerging from the Lohara family of the Pir Panjal region, the dynasty shaped the political and cultural history of medieval Kashmir.

Historical Overview

From Lohara roots to the throne of Kashmir

The Lohara Dynasty emerged in Kashmir at the beginning of the 11th century. The Lohara family was associated with the Pir Panjal region, and Queen Didda, daughter of a Lohara ruler, played a crucial role in connecting Lohara with the Kashmir kingdom. After Didda's death in 1003 CE, her adopted heir Samgrāmarāja became ruler of Kashmir and established the Lohara dynasty there.

Major Rulers

Rulers who shaped medieval Kashmir

Queen Didda

A daughter of the Lohara royal family, Didda became one of Kashmir's most powerful rulers. After the death of her husband Kshemagupta, she ruled as regent and later as sovereign. She selected Samgrāmarāja as her heir, establishing the succession that brought the Lohara dynasty to the Kashmir throne.

Samgrāmarāja

The first ruler of the Lohara dynasty in Kashmir, Samgrāmarāja succeeded Queen Didda in 1003 CE. His reign is remembered for defending Kashmir against invasions, including Mahmud of Ghazni's unsuccessful attempts to enter the valley.

The Mauryas extended their influence from the northwest (Gandhara) to the Deccan plateau in the south, and from the Himalayan frontiers to the fertile plains of Bengal and Bangladesh—the largest empire ever seen in ancient India up to that time.
