Organological Classifications (Ghana & Avanaddha Vadya)

Ancient Indian music treatises (Natya Shastra & Sangita Ratnakara) classify percussion into distinct acoustic categories.

Ghana Vadya (Idiophones)

Solid, non-membrane instruments that produce sound through their own natural acoustic resonance when struck. Includes tuned wooden-bar xylophones (Kashta Tarang), bamboo-slat bars, porcelain water bowls (Jal Tarang), clay pots (Ghatam), and metal jaw harps (Morsing).

Avanaddha Vadya (Membranophones)

Instruments with tightly stretched leather skin membranes over wooden, clay, or metal shells. Includes classical tuned drums like the Mridangam, Tabla, and Pakhawaj, alongside temple drums like the Chenda, Thavil, and Pung.

Xylophones vs. Indian Bar Idiophones

While Western organology uses "xylophone" (Greek for wood-sound) for tuned wooden bar instruments played with mallets, Indian classical tradition names this Kashta Tarang (wooden waves). North-East India also preserves indigenous tuned Naga Bamboo Xylophones.
