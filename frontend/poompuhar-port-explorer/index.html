Poompuhar Ancient Port

Walk through the Chola-era harbour where the Kaveri met the Bay of Bengal, a city remembered by Sangam poets and traced by underwater archaeology.

Begin the explorer

History of the port

A river mouth facing the eastern seas

Poompuhar, also called Kaveripattinam or Puhar, stood near the mouth of the Kaveri in present-day Tamil Nadu. From the early centuries BCE through the early medieval period it served as a major gateway for the Chola country.

Its river landing places, bazaars and quarters for visiting merchants made it a cosmopolitan centre linking inland produce with Bay of Bengal routes.

Port snapshot

  • Region: Mayiladuthurai district, Tamil Nadu
  • Waterways: Kaveri river and Bay of Bengal
  • Known as: Kaveripattinam, Puhar
  • Tradition: Early Chola maritime hub

Maritime networks

Chola trade across the Bay of Bengal

Inland to ocean

Rice, textiles, salt, pearls and crafted goods moved down the Kaveri to ships and coastal vessels.

Overseas connections

Seasonal monsoon sailing connected Tamil merchants with Sri Lanka and ports of Southeast Asia.

People of the harbour

Fisherfolk, artisans, merchants and sailors formed the working communities described in Tamil sources.

Literary significance

Puhar in the voice of Sangam poetry

Pattinappalai portrays a vibrant city of ships, markets, warehouses and diverse communities. The epic Silappatikaram also begins in Puhar, giving the port an enduring place in Tamil literary memory. These works are valuable cultural witnesses, while their poetic descriptions should be read alongside archaeological evidence.

Archaeology

A city partly claimed by the sea

Excavations at Poompuhar and nearby sites have documented brick structures, ring wells, pottery, beads and industrial remains. Marine archaeological surveys off the coast have identified stone features and artefacts that help researchers investigate changing shorelines and submerged activity.

What to look for

  • Early historic pottery and terracotta objects
  • Brick-built habitation and water structures
  • Beads, shell working and metal artefacts
  • Evidence of coastal change and inundation
