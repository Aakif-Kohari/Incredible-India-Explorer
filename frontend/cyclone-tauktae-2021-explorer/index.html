Cyclone Tauktae (2021)

14–19 May 2021 · Peak 3-min Sustained Winds: 185 km/h (115 mph) · Saurashtra Landfall

Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm Tauktae was the strongest tropical cyclone to make landfall in Gujarat's Saurashtra region since the 1998 Gujarat cyclone and one of the most powerful storms ever recorded in the Arabian Sea. Originating near the Lakshadweep archipelago, Tauktae tracked parallel to India's entire western coast—bringing torrential downpours and destructive gales to Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, and Maharashtra—before slamming into Gujarat with ferocious gusts reaching 220 km/h.

Meteorology & Oceanography

Formation, Rapid Intensification & Arabian Sea Context

Tauktae highlighted a marked trend in Arabian Sea meteorology: warming sea surface temperatures fueling rapid cyclonic intensification.

Tropical Cyclogenesis & Rapid Intensification

On 14 May 2021, a low-pressure area organized into Depression ARB 01 over the southeastern Arabian Sea near Lakshadweep. Over exceptionally warm waters (SSTs of 30°C–31°C) and low vertical wind shear, it underwent rapid intensification, upgrading to a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm within 24 hours.

The Coast-Parallel Trajectory

Rather than curving westward toward Oman, Tauktae was steered northward along the eastern periphery of a subtropical ridge. Its massive rainbands swept along the Western Ghats, delivering catastrophic rainfall to coastal states sequentially from south to north.

Key Meteorological Parameters

  • Naming: Proposed by Myanmar, meaning "Gecko" (a vocal lizard in the Burmese language).
  • Category: Category 4 equivalent on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale (SSHWS).
  • Rainfall Records: Mumbai recorded 214 mm in 24 hours, its highest May 24-hour rainfall in history.
  • Storm Surge: 3.0 to 4.5 meters along Saurashtra coast between Diu, Una, and Jafrabad.
Coastal Geography

Sequential Impact Across Five Coastal States

From the southern tip of Kerala to the salt flats of the Rann of Kutch, Tauktae battered over 2,000 km of Indian coastline.

Kerala & Lakshadweep

Heavy storm surges overtopped coastal seawalls in Chellanam and Valiyathura. Flash floods and high sea swells battered the Lakshadweep atolls, snapping island power lines.

Karnataka & Goa

Uprooted over 1,000 ancient trees, severely damaged coastal highway bridges in Karwar and Mangalore, and submerged low-lying tourist strips across North and South Goa.

Maharashtra & Mumbai

Gale-force gusts of 114 km/h tore roofs off buildings, suspended flight operations at Mumbai Airport, shut down the Bandra-Worli Sea Link, and created massive sea swells at Gateway of India.

Landfall Devastation

Landfall Devastation in Saurashtra (Gujarat)

On 17 May at approximately 20:30 IST, the eye of Cyclone Tauktae made landfall between Una and Jafrabad in Gir Somnath and Amreli districts.

Destruction of Power & Communication Grids

Over 70,000 electric poles and 5,000 transformers were flattened in Gir Somnath, Amreli, Bhavnagar, and Junagadh districts, leaving millions in total blackout during the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Severe Agricultural & Orchard Losses

Winds shredded world-famous Kesar mango orchards in Talala Gir and decimated coconut and cash crop plantations, causing long-term economic dislocation for local farmers.

Maritime & Harbor Damage

Hundreds of mechanized fishing trawlers anchored at Veraval, Jafrabad, and Porbandar harbors broke their moorings, capsized, or were smashed against concrete jetties by 4-meter surges.

Impact on Gir Wildlife Sanctuary

Tauktae tracked directly over the Gir forest, the last sanctuary of the Asiatic Lion. Thousands of canopy trees collapsed, requiring rapid forest department rescue patrols.

Search & Rescue

Offshore Oil Field Crisis & Operation Prahast

Cyclone Tauktae caused a grave offshore crisis in the Bombay High oil fields, prompting the Indian Navy's largest search-and-rescue mission in peace time.

The Sinking of Barge P305

Accommodation barge P305, carrying 261 personnel supporting ONGC offshore installations, lost anchors amidst 8-meter waves, drifted into an offshore platform, and sank 35 nautical miles off Mumbai.

Indian Navy & Coast Guard Heroism

Naval destroyers INS Kochi and INS Kolkata, frigates INS Talwar and INS Betwa, along with Sea King helicopters and P-8I maritime aircraft, rescued 186 personnel under zero-visibility cyclonic seas.

Early Warning

IMD Forecasting & Mega-Evacuation Amid COVID-19

Despite the logistical challenge of the second COVID-19 pandemic wave, early alerts prevented catastrophic onshore loss of life.

Precise IMD Trajectory Forecasting

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) utilized Doppler Weather Radars at Kochi, Goa, Mumbai, and Bhuj alongside INSAT-3D satellites to predict the exact Saurashtra landfall window with high fidelity.

NDRF Deployment & Oxygen Grid Safeguards

Over 100 National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams executed the evacuation of over 200,000 people to cyclone shelters, simultaneously securing backup diesel generators for hospitals and oxygen plants.

Policy & Resilience

Disaster Management Lessons from Cyclone Tauktae

Key policy takeaways that transformed India's coastal and offshore resilience strategies:

Offshore Safety Protocol Overhaul

Strict mandatory de-manning protocols for offshore barges and rigs at least 72 hours prior to cyclonic gales exceeding 34 knots in the Arabian Sea basin.

Underground Coastal Power Grids

Accelerating the underground cabling of electrical transmission infrastructure across high-vulnerability Saurashtra and Konkan coastal belts.

Arabian Sea Climate Monitoring

Expanding deep-sea ocean buoy networks to track anomalous thermal heat content driving unprecedented rapid intensification along India's west coast.
