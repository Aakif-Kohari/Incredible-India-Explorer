Rajmachi Fort Trek राजमाची किल्ला — Lonavala / Karjat, Maharashtra

Perched 825 meters high in the Sahyadri mountain range between Lonavala and Karjat, Rajmachi is one of Maharashtra's most celebrated hill fort complexes. Comprising twin hill citadels—Shrivardhan and Manaranjan—rising above Udhewadi plateau village, Rajmachi was expanded by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj in 1657 to guard the historic Borghat trade route. Famous for its roaring monsoon waterfalls, ancient Kondhane Buddhist Caves, and lush green mountain trails.

Shrivardhan & Manaranjan Twin Citadels

The Rajmachi fort complex is distinguished by two twin hill citadels rising above the central Machi plateau village of Udhewadi. Shrivardhan Fort (the taller eastern peak) served as the primary watchtower citadel, while Manaranjan Fort (western peak) housed administrative structures and ramparts overlooking the Konkan trade valley.

Maratha Heritage

Historical Significance of Rajmachi

Originally constructed during the Satavahana era, Rajmachi gained prominence in 1657 CE when Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj fortified the site to safeguard the Borghat trade highway connecting Deccan plateau cities with Konkan sea ports.

Trail Approaches

Two Distinct Starting Routes

Trekkers can access Udhewadi village and Rajmachi forts via two famous starting points: a flat plateau walk from Lonavala or a steep forest climb from Kondhane village (Karjat).

Seasonal Transformation

Sahyadri Monsoon Scenery & Waterfalls

From June to September, heavy monsoon rains transform the Sahyadri mountains into a misty green wonderland with overflowing streams and roaring waterfalls.
