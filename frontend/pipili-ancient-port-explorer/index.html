Historical naming note

The historical source used here spells the trading centre Pipli and places it on the Subernarekha. Modern Pipili in Puri district is a major appliqué craft centre. This explorer keeps both references visible rather than presenting them as the same historical location.

HISTORICAL OVERVIEW

A port story within Odisha's wider maritime network

Odisha Review describes Odisha's long maritime tradition and records a Portuguese trade centre at Pipli in 1514. It says Portuguese commercial activity continued for more than a century before Dutch, French and English merchants entered the trade, and records an English trade centre at Pipli until 1634.

Rather than treating a port as an isolated point, the story is best read as part of a larger Bay of Bengal network in which coastal settlements, river routes, merchants and overseas connections interacted.

“

Odisha has a long and glorious tradition of maritime history because of its close association with the sea.

Odisha Review, April 2013
CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE

Pipili's living craft heritage

Modern Pipili is especially celebrated for chandua, its vibrant appliqué tradition. Government sources connect the craft with Jagannath culture, temple rituals and Rath Yatra.

🧵

Chandua appliqué

Artisans cut coloured cloth into floral, animal, geometric and narrative motifs and stitch them onto a base fabric.

🛕

Jagannath connection

Pipli appliqué is closely associated with the Jagannath Temple and ceremonial textiles used in religious celebrations.

🎨

Living economy

The craft continues to support contemporary products including umbrellas, canopies, wall hangings, bags and home décor.
