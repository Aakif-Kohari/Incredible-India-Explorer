🏹 Prehistoric Archaeological Profile

Mesolithic Sites Across India

Microliths, Settlements & Rock Art of the Middle Stone Age

📍 Sites Across the Indian Subcontinent

The Mesolithic in India spans the period between the end of the Ice Age and the rise of farming communities. Its small, finely worked stone tools, early cemeteries, and vivid rock art give a rare, detailed view of hunter-gatherer life in the subcontinent.

Stone Tools

Mesolithic toolkits in India are dominated by microliths: tiny, precisely worked blades, points, lunates, and trapezes, often only a few centimetres long.

These small tools were commonly hafted in sets onto wood or bone shafts to make composite arrows, spears, and sickles, a marked shift from the large single-piece tools of earlier periods.

Settlement Evidence

Excavations at Mesolithic sites have revealed hearths, occupation floors, and, at several locations, deliberate human burials, some of the earliest funerary evidence known from the subcontinent.

Faunal and floral remains found alongside these settlements suggest small, mobile groups relying on hunting, fishing, and the gathering of wild plant foods.

Major Sites

🎨 Bhimbetka, Madhya Pradesh

A vast complex of rock shelters with one of the world's richest collections of prehistoric rock art, spanning the Mesolithic and later periods.

🏺 Bagor, Rajasthan

A long-occupied riverside site with a detailed microlithic sequence and evidence of animal domestication in its later phases.

⚰️ Sarai Nahar Rai, Uttar Pradesh

Known for its Mesolithic human burials, offering some of the earliest evidence of deliberate interment in the region.

🦴 Langhnaj, Gujarat

A dune site yielding microliths alongside human skeletal remains and animal bones, informing early studies of Mesolithic populations.

🏕️ Adamgarh, Madhya Pradesh

Rock shelters near the Narmada valley with microlithic tools and early evidence associated with animal domestication.

🌾 Tilwara, Rajasthan

A desert-margin site contributing to the understanding of Mesolithic adaptation in arid western India.

Rock Art

🐃 Hunting Scenes

Paintings at Bhimbetka and related shelters depict hunters with bows and spears pursuing deer, boar, and other wild animals.

💃 Group & Dance Scenes

Figures shown in rows or circular arrangements are widely interpreted as depictions of communal dances or gatherings.

🖌️ Pigments Used

Artists used natural mineral pigments, chiefly red ochre and white, applied directly onto rock shelter walls and ceilings.

Chronology

Early Mesolithic
Emergence of Microlithic Industries

Small, standardised microlith production begins to replace the larger tool traditions of the preceding period.

Middle Mesolithic
Expanding Settlement

Hunter-gatherer groups occupy a wide range of environments, from riversides to desert margins and rock shelters.

Late Mesolithic
Transition Toward Farming

At sites like Bagor and Adamgarh, evidence of early animal domestication marks a gradual shift toward Neolithic lifeways.

20th Cent.
Discovery & Excavation

Systematic excavation at Bhimbetka, Bagor, Sarai Nahar Rai, and Langhnaj establishes the framework for Indian Mesolithic studies.
