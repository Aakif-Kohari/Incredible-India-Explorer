Causatthi Ghat

Sanctuary of the Sixty-Four Yoginis & Sacred Feminine Energy of Kashi

Perched along the central riverfront of Varanasi, Causatthi Ghat (Chausatthi Ghat) derives its sacred name from the sixty-four Yoginis—the divine tantric manifestations of cosmic feminine energy (*Shakti*). Famous for its extraordinarily steep stone stairways leading to the cliffside Chausath Yogini Temple, this ghat stands as a vital pilgrimage center for Shakta devotion, Bengali cultural heritage, and spiritual penance.

🕉️ Mythology, History & Shakta Lineage

Legend of the 64 Yoginis

According to the Kashi Khanda of the Skanda Purana, Lord Shiva sent the sixty-four Yoginis to Kashi during the reign of King Divodasa. Upon arriving, the Yoginis were so entranced by the supreme spiritual beauty and sanctity of Varanasi that they decided to establish their permanent residence right here at Causatthi Ghat.

Raja Digpatia & Bengali Patronage (1670)

In 1670 CE, King Digpatia of Bengal rebuilt Causatthi Ghat with sturdy sandstone steps and surrounding residential ashrams. For centuries, it served as a major sanctuary for Bengali pilgrims, scholars, and widows seeking lifelong spiritual refuge in Kashi.

Swami Bhaskarananda Saraswati

The renowned 19th-century saint and scholar Swami Bhaskarananda Saraswati lived, performed intense tapasya, and attained samadhi near Causatthi Ghat. His presence drew kings, scholars, and international seekers to the ghat's peaceful precincts.

📜 Exploring Causatthi's Sacred Traditions

The Sixty-Four Divine Manifestations

In Hindu Tantra and Shaktism, the 64 Yoginis represent the varied energetic aspects of the Supreme Goddess (*Adishakti*).

  • Protective Guardians: Revered as fierce guardian deities who grant spiritual liberation (*Moksha*) and esoteric wisdom (*Siddhi*).
  • Tantric Iconography: Each Yogini embodies specific cosmic powers, weapons, and meditative postures (*mudras*).
  • Feminine Energy Focus: Causatthi Ghat stands as Varanasi's principal sanctuary celebrating divine feminine power.

🌺 Sacred Energy

"Worshipping at Causatthi Ghat is believed to bestow protection from negative energies and awaken dormant spiritual strength."

The Cliffside Chausath Yogini Temple

Perched high above the river embankment, the temple is reached via a steep flight of narrow stone stairs.

  • Presiding Deities: Houses the sanctum of Goddess Kali along with images of the 64 Yoginis surrounding the central shrine.
  • Chausatthi Devi Image: Devotees offer red flowers, vermillion (*sindoor*), and red saris to the deity.
  • Bengali Architectural Accents: Courtyards and sanctum structures reflecting traditional East Indian temple styles.

🛕 Temple Access

The steep climb from the ghat steps into the elevated temple courtyard offers a commanding view over the bend of the Ganges.

The Skanda Purana Legend of King Divodasa

The mythical story of King Divodasa explains why gods and goddesses chose to remain in Varanasi.

  • Shiva's Exile: King Divodasa ruled Kashi on the condition that gods remain in the celestial realms. Shiva was eager to return to his beloved city.
  • The Mission of the Yoginis: Lord Shiva dispatched the 64 Yoginis to create illusions and persuade Divodasa to leave.
  • Eternal Abode: Enchanted by Kashi's holiness, the Yoginis refused to return to Shiva and settled at Causatthi Ghat instead.

📜 Kashi Mahatmya

This legend underlines the fundamental Hindu belief that Kashi is so spiritually alluring that even divine beings cannot bear to leave it.

Navratri Festivals & Pilgrimage Rituals

Causatthi Ghat transforms into a bustling sea of red during spring and autumn Navratri festivals.

  • Chaitra Krishna Ashtami (Badi Gaura): A major festival where women assemble at Causatthi Ghat to pray for marital harmony and family well-being.
  • Chausatthi Yatra: Pilgrims perform ritual baths (*snan*) in the Ganges before climbing to offer prayers at the temple.
  • Kali Puja & Durga Puja: Traditional Tantric recitations, Vedic mantras, and evening lamp offerings along the steps.

🕯️ Festival Atmosphere

During Navratri, the steep steps are illuminated with lamps and decorated with marigold garlands as thousands chant hymns to the Goddess.
