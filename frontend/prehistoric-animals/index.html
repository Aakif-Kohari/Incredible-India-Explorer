🦖 The Prehistoric Animals Catalogue

1. Rajasaurus narmadensis

The Princely Lizard of Narmada

Geological Period:

Late Cretaceous (~70 to 66 Million Years Ago)

Fossil Locations:

Lameta Formation along the Narmada River Valley (Rahioli, Gujarat & Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh)

Size Dimensions:

Stood roughly 2.4 meters tall, stretched 9 meters in length, and weighed around 4 tonnes.

Anatomy & Habitat:

A heavy-bodied, carnivorous theropod (abelisaurid) distinguished by an unusual, low rounded horn crown atop its skull. It was the apex predator of its ecosystem, hunting in warm, marshy riverine corridors and coastal estuaries.

Scientific Significance:

Its discovery proves that the Indian plate maintained a distinctive, isolated biological evolutionary nursery while drifting away from Gondwana.

2. Sivatherium giganteum

The Giant Beast of Shiva

Geological Period:

Pliocene to Late Pleistocene (~5 Million to 100,000 Years Ago)

Fossil Locations:

The Shivalik Hills foothills (Himachal Pradesh and Jammu & Kashmir)

Size Dimensions:

Stood over 2.2 meters tall at the shoulder, with total heights crossing 3 meters, weighing nearly 1.5 tonnes.

Anatomy & Habitat:

The largest relative of modern giraffes ever known to exist. It possessed a heavy, moose-like body profile and featured two massive, branching ossicones (horn-like structures) expanding from its skull. It grazed on mixed shrublands and savannas bordering the early sub-Himalayan plains.

Scientific Significance:

It links ancient African giraffe lines with the migration bridges that connected India to Eurasia.

3. Palaeoloxodon namadicus

The Colossal Narmada Elephant

Geological Period:

Middle to Late Pleistocene (~500,000 to 50,000 Years Ago)

Fossil Locations:

Ancient river gravel banks of the Narmada Valley (Madhya Pradesh)

Size Dimensions:

Shoulder heights crossing 5 to 5.2 meters, with weight boundaries scaling up to 22 tonnes.

Anatomy & Habitat:

Universally recognized as one of the largest land mammals to ever walk the earth—more than triple the mass of a modern African elephant. It featured straight, thick tusks and adapted its massive skeletal frame to navigate the highly productive Pleistocene savannas and riverine forests of central India.

Scientific Significance:

Highlights the hyper-favorable ecological productivity of India’s ancient grassland corridors before the arrival of modern climates.

4. Hyperodapedon

The Triassic Beaked Reptile

Geological Period:

Late Triassic (~230 to 220 Million Years Ago)

Fossil Locations:

Maleri and Tiki Formations (Pranhita-Godavari Basin, Telangana & Madhya Pradesh)

Size Dimensions:

Stood short and stocky, measuring about 1.3 meters in length and weighing around 40 kg.

Anatomy & Habitat:

A specialized, thick-bodied reptile (rhynchosaur) featuring a sharp, downward-curving beak and rows of crushing dental plates. It burrowed and fed on tough seed ferns in vast, low-lying alluvial plains before dinosaurs rose to dominance.

Scientific Significance:

Shows the early diversification of reptiles in the Indian subcontinent prior to the dominance of dinosaurs.

📝 Test Your Understanding (OpenPrep AI Prehistoric India Challenge)

Abelisaurid Distribution

Question: Why is the discovery of the horned predatory dinosaur 'Rajasaurus narmadensis' in Gujarat of immense importance to scientists tracking continental drift?

Correct Answer: Its close anatomical links to abelisaurids in Madagascar and South America prove these landmasses were once fused as part of Gondwana.

Shivalik Megafauna

Question: The giant fossil animal 'Sivatherium giganteum', discovered in the sub-Himalayan Shivalik Hills, represents a massive prehistoric member of which modern mammal family?

Correct Answer: The giraffe family (Giraffidae), despite having short necks and heavy, moose-like horn antlers.
