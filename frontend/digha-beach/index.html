Digha Beach

West Bengal's “Brighton of the East”

Digha is West Bengal's best-loved seaside escape — a wide, flat and remarkably shallow beach on the Bay of Bengal where the tide recedes nearly a mile, making it one of the safest stretches of sand in India for a swim. First charmed by the British in the 1780s and developed into a resort town after independence, Digha today stretches from the quieter Old Digha to the breezy promenade of New Digha, framed the entire way by whispering casuarina groves and famous for watching both sunrise and sunset over the same horizon.

📍 Location & Geographical Profile

Digha lies on the northern edge of the Bay of Bengal in the Contai subdivision of Purba Medinipur district, West Bengal, close to the Odisha border. It is roughly 185 km southwest of Kolkata and is administered by the Digha Sankarpur Development Authority (DSDA). The town is split into Old Digha, known for calmer sunrise views, and New Digha, two km away, with a longer, better-maintained promenade and wider stretch of sand.

🏖️ Beach Overview

  • Ultra-wide & shallow: The sea recedes almost a mile at low tide, giving one of the flattest, safest swimming beaches in the country.
  • Old vs New Digha: Old Digha is quieter and closer to the railway station; New Digha, 2 km away, has a longer concrete promenade and denser resorts.
  • Casuarina groves: The entire coast is lined with whispering casuarina (jhau) plantations instead of the coconut palms typical of other Indian beaches.
  • Sunrise & sunset: Digha's east-facing curve lets visitors watch both the sunrise and sunset reflect off the same stretch of water.
  • Historic roots: Called the “Brighton of the East” in Warren Hastings's 1780 letters; developed as a resort town after independence on the urging of writer John Frank Smith.
  • Access: Well connected by NH-116B and regular buses/trains from Kolkata's Howrah and Sealdah stations; roughly a 4-hour drive.

🌴 Coastal Landscape

  • Low-gradient shore: A near-flat sand profile means the tide line shifts dramatically between high and low tide, exposing a huge tidal flat.
  • Casuarina windbreaks: Dense jhau plantations reduce dune erosion and shelter the promenade from sea winds — a signature Digha silhouette.
  • Coastal erosion: Digha faces active shoreline erosion; sections of the beach have been reinforced with concrete embankments to protect the town.
  • Tidal flats & crabs: The wet sand flats at low tide are alive with ghost crabs and shorebirds, especially near quieter stretches like Udaipur.
  • Estuarine surroundings: Paddy fields, brackish creeks and fishing villages ring the town, feeding into the wider Bay of Bengal delta ecology.
  • Elevation: A low-lying coastal town at roughly 6 m elevation, making it climatically mild but exposed to monsoon storm surges.

🐬 Coastal Environment

  • Bay of Bengal waters: Warm, gently sloped and generally calm outside monsoon, making Digha one of India's safer beaches for wading and swimming.
  • Marine Aquarium & Research Centre: Run by the Zoological Survey of India, it showcases the region's fish, corals and marine biodiversity.
  • Fishing harbour: Mohana and Shankarpur's fishing jetties supply Digha's famous seafood markets and give a glimpse of daily coastal livelihoods.
  • Monsoon caution: June–September brings rough seas, undertows and periodic swimming bans; always heed lifeguard flags.
  • Marine etiquette: Avoid single-use plastics on the sand, respect fishing-boat zones, and keep a respectful distance from nesting shorebirds.

🎭 Cultural Highlights

  • Bengal's weekend getaway: Digha is the quintessential short holiday for Kolkata families — beach walks, shell handicrafts and sunset adda on the sand.
  • Seafood culture: Roadside stalls serve Bengali-style fried pomfret, prawn and crab, alongside classic ilish (hilsa) preparations.
  • Colonial-era heritage: A handful of old bungalows near Old Digha recall its 18th-century discovery and British-era beginnings.
  • Amarabati Park: Also called the Snake Park, it houses reptiles and a mini zoo, a favourite short outing for visiting families.
  • Handicraft markets: Stalls along the promenade sell jewellery and décor crafted from seashells, driftwood and sea-stones.
  • DSDA Civic Infrastructure: A dedicated civic body maintains the promenade, beautification and beach-front year-round.

🚆 How to Reach Digha

Comfortable connectivity by express train, four-lane highway, and state luxury buses.

🚆 By Train

Digha Railway Station (DGHA) is directly connected with Kolkata's Howrah and Santragachi stations via scenic daily express trains.

12857 Tamralipta Exp 18001 Kandari Exp 22897 Digha AC Exp

⏱️ Travel time: ~3 to 3.5 hours from Howrah.

🚗 By Road

Connected via NH-116B. The route passes through Kolaghat, Nandakumar, Contai, and Ramnagar along well-paved 4-lane highways.

Recommended Stop: Kolaghat for roadside hot tea and Bengali snacks.

⏱️ Drive time: ~4 hours (~185 km from Kolkata).

🚌 By Bus

Frequent WBTC, SBSTC and private AC/non-AC luxury buses ply round the clock from Kolkata's Esplanade (Dharmatala), Karunamoyee, and Howrah.

Departs every 15-30 minutes during peak morning and evening hours.

⏱️ Bus journey: ~4.5 to 5 hours.

🎯 Explore Digha Beach Hub

Quick reference guide for activities, nearby gems, and travel tips.

  • Beach walks & horse rides: Stroll the flat sand at low tide or take a pony ride along the New Digha promenade.
  • Swimming & wading: Shallow, gentle waters outside monsoon are ideal for families and first-time swimmers.
  • Water sports: Jet-skiing, banana-boat rides and para-sailing operate seasonally along New Digha.
  • Marine Aquarium visit: Learn about Bay of Bengal marine life at the Zoological Survey of India's research aquarium.
  • Sunrise & sunset watching: Catch both from the same shoreline — early mornings and dusk are the best times on the sand.
  • Seafood trail: Sample fresh-caught prawn, pomfret and crab at the beach-side stalls and Digha's fish market.
  • Shankarpur Beach (~14 km): A quieter twin beach with a working fishing harbour and dense casuarina cover.
  • Udaipur Beach (~3 km from Digha station): A calmer, less-crowded stretch of silvery sand near the Odisha border.
  • Talsari Beach (~8 km, Odisha): Famous for red crabs, palm-fringed dunes and the Subarnarekha river estuary.
  • Amarabati Park (~1 km): Digha's park and mini zoo, a short walk from New Digha with a boating lake and ropeway.
  • Marine Aquarium & Research Centre (~2 km): Zoological Survey of India facility with regional marine species on display.
  • Chandaneswar Temple (~12 km): A popular Shiva temple just across the Odisha border, often combined with a Digha trip.
  • Best time to visit: October to March is pleasant and dry; April–June turns hot and humid; July–September brings monsoon swells.
  • Getting there: Regular trains and WBSTC/private buses run from Kolkata's Howrah, Sealdah and Esplanade (Dharmatala); about 4 hours by road via Kolaghat.
  • Entry: The beach itself is free; nominal charges apply for the aquarium, water sports and pony/horse rides.
  • Safety: Swim only in permitted, lifeguard-marked zones; avoid the water entirely during monsoon red-flag warnings.
  • What to bring: Light cottons, sunscreen, hat, flip-flops for the hot midday sand, and cash for market stalls.
  • Responsible travel: Carry reusable bottles, dispose of waste in bins, and avoid disturbing fishing boats and nets on the shore.
