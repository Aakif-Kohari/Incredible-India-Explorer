Vamana and King Bali — The Three Steps

Bhagavata Purana · Vamana avatara · Kerala’s Onam

A generous asura king offers three paces of land to a dwarf brahmin. The guest grows to the measure of the cosmos. Two steps cover earth and heaven; the third is placed on Bali’s head. Kerala remembers the king’s yearly return as Onam.

Who

Vamana and King Bali

King Bali (Mahabali)

Grandson of Prahlada and king of the asuras. Traditional tellings describe his rule as just and generous: no hunger, no theft, no lying. He had won the three worlds through valour and a great sacrifice, which alarmed the gods. His word, once given, is the hinge of the story.

Vamana

Vishnu’s fifth avatara in the usual list of ten. He appears as a dwarf brahmacarin — small, with staff and umbrella — and asks only for three paces of ground measured by his own feet. When the gift is sealed, he reveals the cosmic form called Trivikrama, “the one of three strides.”

Chronology

The traditional narrative

  1. Bali’s rise: After a victorious campaign, Bali performs a sacrifice (often told as an ashvamedha). The worlds pass under asura rule. Aditi, mother of the gods, prays to Vishnu.
  2. The dwarf guest: Vishnu is born as Vamana and comes to the sacrificial enclosure. Bali, true to the dharma of giving, offers him a boon.
  3. Three paces: Vamana refuses gold and cattle. He asks for land covered by three of his small steps. Guru Shukracharya warns Bali that the guest is Vishnu. Bali will not take back a promise.
  4. Trivikrama: Vamana grows. The first stride covers the earth; the second covers the heavens. No ground remains for the third.
  5. The head: Bali offers his own head. The third step sends him to Sutala. Vishnu is pleased with his devotion and, in many tellings, becomes the guardian at Bali’s door and allows him to visit his people once a year.
Episode

The three-step encounter

First step — the earth

The dwarf becomes immeasurable. One foot spans continents, mountains, and seas. Bali’s earthly kingdom is already gone.

Second step — the heavens

The next stride takes the sky and the realms of the gods. Two steps have measured all that Bali thought he owned.

Third step — Bali’s head

Nothing is left to give except himself. Bali bows. The third step honours the gift and sends him to Sutala, not as a discarded enemy but as a king under Vishnu’s care.

Kerala

Connection with Onam

Onam, Kerala’s harvest festival (Chingam in the Malayalam calendar), is kept as Mahabali’s annual homecoming. Homes lay pookkalam (floral carpets), serve Onam sadya, and hold boat races and dance. The ten-day season treats the old king as a returning guest who must find the land still prosperous and fair.

Thrikkakara Vamanamoorthy Temple near Kochi is closely tied to this telling: Vamana is the deity, and Onam rites there remember both the dwarf avatara and the beloved king. The festival thus holds two moods at once — Vishnu’s three strides, and Kerala’s welcome to Bali.

Context

Regional cultural interpretations

  • Kerala: Mahabali is the just king of a golden age. Onam is his visit, not a festival of defeat. Vamana and Bali are honoured together at Thrikkakara.
  • Vaishnava Puranic reading: The three steps restore the gods’ worlds and test dana (giving). Bali becomes a model devotee; Vishnu guards Sutala.
  • Maharashtra and western India: Bali Pratipada (the day after Diwali in some calendars) remembers Bali’s generosity and the same three-step gift.
  • Vedic echo: The Rigveda already praises Vishnu’s three strides. The Puranas turn that image into the Vamana–Bali story.
Notes

Did you know?

  • Vamana is usually counted as Vishnu’s fifth avatara in the Dashavatara list.
  • Shukracharya is said to have tried to block the water of the gift; Bali still completed the dana.
  • In several tellings Vishnu remains as a doorkeeper in Sutala so that Bali’s realm is as splendid as heaven.
  • Bali is promised Indra’s office in a future age (the Savarni manvantara) in Puranic accounts.
  • Onam at Thrikkakara is among the oldest documented Kerala celebrations linked to this shrine.
