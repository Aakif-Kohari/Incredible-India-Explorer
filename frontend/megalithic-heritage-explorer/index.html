The Megalithic
Heritage of India

Long before empires and inscriptions, communities across the subcontinent raised stone to mark their dead and their ancestors — dolmens, menhirs, and cairn circles that still stand in the Deccan uplands, the Nilgiri slopes, and the hills of Meghalaya, where the tradition is still alive today.

03 — Material Culture

Grave goods & Iron Age technology

Excavations inside burial chambers and cairn circles have uncovered sophisticated metallurgy, craft traditions, and burial offerings that reveal Iron Age life.

🗡️ Metallurgy

Iron Weapons & Tools

Iron spearheads, daggers, arrowheads, axes, and horse bits recovered in large numbers at Mahurjhari and Naikund highlight an armed pastoralist and equestrian society.

🏺 Ceramics

Black-and-Red Ware (BRW)

The diagnostic pottery of Indian megaliths, produced using inverted firing. Bowls and dishes often bear ancient post-firing graffiti symbols and linear geometric motifs.

✨ Ornaments

Etched Carnelian & Gold

White-etched carnelian beads, jasper pendants, copper bangles, and gold diadems (such as at Adichanallur) indicating far-reaching trade routes across Peninsular India.

🐎 Equestrian

Horse Trappings & Sacrifices

Complete equine skeletons buried with bronze face ornaments, curb bits, and copper bells at Vidarbha sites illustrate the exalted cultural status of the horse.
