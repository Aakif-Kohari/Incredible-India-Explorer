April 18, 1930 · Bengal Presidency

The Chittagong Armoury Raid

A daring, meticulously planned revolutionary uprising led by Surya Sen and the Indian Republican Army to paralyze the British administrative and military apparatus in Chittagong.

The Masterda's Vision

The Revolutionary Environment & Objectives

By 1930, the revolutionary environment in Bengal was highly charged. Disillusioned by the slow pace of constitutional reforms, a group of fierce youths formed the Chittagong branch of the Indian Republican Army under the leadership of school teacher Surya Sen, affectionately known as "Masterda".

The objective was audacious: simultaneously attack and capture the two main armouries in Chittagong, destroy the telegraph and telephone office to cut off communications, and assassinate members of the European Club to incapacitate the local British command structure. This would effectively liberate Chittagong and spark a nationwide armed rebellion.

🔗 The Revolutionary Network

Surya Sen built a highly disciplined network including prominent figures like Ganesh Ghosh, Lokenath Bal, Ambika Chakrobarty, Ananta Singh, Kalpana Datta, and Pritilata Waddedar. Their inclusion of women in direct combat roles was a radical departure from traditional resistance norms.

  • Jalalabad Hills: The site of a fierce pitched battle on April 22 between revolutionaries and British troops.

Chronology of the Uprising

18 April 1930 (10:00 PM)

The Simultaneous Strikes

Groups of revolutionaries, dressed in British khaki uniforms, simultaneously capture the Police and Auxiliary armouries. They successfully sever telegraph and railway lines. Surya Sen hoists the National Flag outside the Police Armoury, declaring a Provisional Revolutionary Government.

18 April 1930 (Midnight)

The Ammunition Crisis

A fatal flaw emerges: while they capture massive stockpiles of rifles and machine guns, they cannot locate the matching ammunition. Forced to abandon the heavy weapons, they retreat into the hills.

22 April 1930

Battle of Jalalabad Hills

British forces locate the revolutionaries in the Jalalabad Hills. A brutal gunfight ensues. Over 80 British troops and 12 revolutionaries (including teenagers) are killed. The surviving revolutionaries disperse into small guerrilla units.

1930 – 1933

Underground Resistance

Surya Sen evades capture for three years, continuing guerrilla operations with the help of local villagers. Notably, Pritilata Waddedar leads a successful attack on the Pahartali European Club in 1932 before martyring herself.

16 February 1933

Arrest & Trial

Betrayed by a local, Surya Sen is arrested. Following a brief trial and severe torture, Surya Sen and Tarakeshwar Dastidar are hanged on January 12, 1934.

Impact

Aftermath & Historical Legacy

Though the raid failed to permanently liberate Chittagong, it dealt a shattering psychological blow to the myth of British invincibility. It inspired a massive surge of youth participation in the independence movement across India and forced the colonial administration to heavily militarize Bengal. The Chittagong Armoury Raid remains one of the most meticulously planned and valiant chapters in the history of anti-colonial armed struggle.
