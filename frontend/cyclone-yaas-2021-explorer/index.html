Cyclone Yaas (2021)

23–28 May 2021 · Peak 3-min Sustained Winds: 140 km/h (85 mph) · Landfall at Dhamra Port

Just days after Cyclone Tauktae struck India's western seaboard, Very Severe Cyclonic Storm Yaas formed in the northern Bay of Bengal and slammed into the eastern coastline near Dhamra Port in Odisha's Bhadrak district on 26 May 2021. Coinciding disastrously with a perigean spring tide (full moon), Yaas generated massive 3–4 meter storm surges that breached river embankments across West Bengal and the fragile UNESCO World Heritage Sundarbans delta, impacting over 10 million people.

Meteorological Evolution

Formation, Rapid Intensification & Bay of Bengal Dynamics

The warm waters and shallow bathymetry of the northern Bay of Bengal created ideal conditions for cyclogenesis and storm surge amplification.

Tropical Cyclogenesis in North Bay of Bengal

On 23 May 2021, a low-pressure area organized into Depression BOB 02 over the East-Central Bay of Bengal. Fueled by sea surface temperatures of 31°C–32°C and high tropical cyclone heat potential, the system rapidly intensified into a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm.

North-Northwest Trajectory toward Odisha

Steered by a subtropical ridge situated to its east, Yaas tracked steadily north-northwestward toward the Odisha–West Bengal border, making landfall near Dhamra Port and south of Balasore on the morning of 26 May 2021.

Key Meteorological Parameters

  • Naming: Proposed by Oman; "Yaas" refers to a fragrant, jasmine-like flowering tree in the Persian/Arabic language.
  • Coincidental Astronomical Tide: Struck during the Bhora Kotal (full moon perigean spring tide), which magnified surge heights by up to 2 meters.
  • Extreme Wave Heights: Offshore wave riders recorded maximum wave heights exceeding 7 meters in the northern Bay of Bengal.
  • Rainfall Footprint: Dropped 250–300 mm of torrential rainfall across Balasore, Mayurbhanj, Bhadrak, and East Medinipur.
Ground Zero

Landfall Devastation in Coastal Odisha

Between 09:00 and 13:00 IST on 26 May, the core of Cyclone Yaas crossed the Odisha coast just north of Dhamra and south of Bahanaga.

Bhadrak & Balasore Districts

Winds of 130–140 km/h flattened kutcha houses, ripped tin roofs, snapped high-voltage transmission pylons, and uprooted tens of thousands of trees across coastal highways.

Dhamra & Chandbali Port Terminals

Seawater inundation overwhelmed commercial fishing wharves and salt pans, submerging port approach corridors and flooding low-lying fishing colonies.

Mayurbhanj & Simlipal Forest

As the system tracked inland into Mayurbhanj district, torrential rains triggered flash floods in the Baitarani and Budhabalanga river basins and localized mudslides in Simlipal.

Delta Hazards

West Bengal Coastal Inundation & Sundarbans Crisis

Although the storm center made landfall in Odisha, West Bengal's coastal districts suffered severe tidal devastation.

Digha, Mandarmani & Shankarpur Resorts

Monster storm waves overtopped and demolished the massive concrete sea-dikes of Digha, flooding the tourist boulevard and destroying commercial hotel basements and markets.

Sundarbans UNESCO Mangrove Delta

Over 130 earthen river embankments in the Sundarbans (South and North 24 Parganas) collapsed under high-tide pressure, inundating hundreds of delta islands with hypersaline seawater.

Agrarian Fallout

Agricultural Loss, Saline Inundation & Fishery Ruin

Seawater breach caused severe, long-lasting agro-ecological damage across the Gangetic and Mahanadi delta plains.

Salinization of Paddy Farmland

Over 300,000 hectares of fertile agrarian soil were inundated with saline water, sterilizing paddy fields for upcoming Kharif sowing seasons and forcing extensive soil rehabilitation.

Collapse of Brackish Aquaculture & Freshwater Ponds

Tens of thousands of freshwater village ponds used for drinking water and domestic fish culture were contaminated, leading to acute drinking water shortages and massive shrimp farm losses.

Emergency Mobilization

Record 2.1 Million Citizen Evacuation

Operating under the severe constraints of the nationwide COVID-19 pandemic wave, state authorities executed massive evacuation drives.

Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF) & OSDMA

Odisha successfully relocated over 600,000 vulnerable individuals to reinforced cyclone shelters with specialized COVID-19 isolation corners and rapid antigen testing protocols.

West Bengal Mega-Evacuation

The West Bengal administration and NDRF evacuated over 1.5 million people from low-lying delta islands and coastal embankments, preventing large-scale drowning casualties.

Resilience Strategy

Disaster Management Lessons from Cyclone Yaas

Key institutional takeaways from Yaas that are shaping long-term coastal protection policies:

Mangrove Bio-Shield Expansion

Launch of massive mangrove plantation missions (like the MISHTI initiative) along the Sundarbans and Odisha coasts to replace brittle earthen river dikes with living ecological buffers.

Saline-Resilient Agriculture

Accelerating the cultivation and distribution of traditional indigenous flood- and saline-tolerant paddy varieties (e.g., Nonabokra, Hamilton, and Dudheswar) across delta islands.

Geotextile Reinforced Embankments

Transitioning from earthen mud bunds to engineered geotextile and boulder-pitched sea embankments designed to withstand 5-meter astronomical storm-surge overlaps.
