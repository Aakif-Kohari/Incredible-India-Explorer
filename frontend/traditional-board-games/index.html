Chaturanga

The Ancestor of Chess

Chaturanga

📜 Historical Background

Originating in the Gupta Empire (c. 6th century CE), "Chaturanga" translates to "four divisions" representing infantry, cavalry, elephantry, and chariotry.

⚖️ Original Rules

Played on an uncheckered 8x8 board (Ashtāpada). Pieces moved similarly to modern chess, but the elephant (bishop) and chariot (rook) had different constraints.

🌍 Modern Evolution

Spread to Persia as Shatranj, and later to Europe where it evolved into modern Chess. Also influenced Shogi in Japan and Xiangqi in China.

Pachisi

The National Game of India

Pachisi

📜 Historical Background

Created in ancient India and heavily played during the Mughal period. Emperor Akbar famously played it on a life-sized board at Fatehpur Sikri using human pieces.

⚖️ Original Rules

A cross-and-circle game where players move pieces based on the throw of cowrie shells. The name derives from the Hindi word 'pachis' (twenty-five), the highest score possible.

🌍 Modern Evolution

Brought to England in the late 19th century and patented as Ludo. It also evolved into the American game Parcheesi.

Moksha Patam

The Game of Karma

Moksha Patam

📜 Historical Background

Created by Hindu spiritual teachers in ancient India (often attributed to Sant Dnyaneshwar). It was originally designed to teach children moral lessons about karma and kama (destiny and desire).

⚖️ Original Rules

Ladders represented virtues (generosity, faith) and snakes represented vices (lust, anger). The goal was to reach 'Moksha' (spiritual liberation) at the top of the board.

🌍 Modern Evolution

Stripped of its moral and religious teachings by the Victorians, it was published in the West as Snakes and Ladders (or Chutes and Ladders in the US).

Chaupar

The Royal Strategy Game

Chaupar

📜 Historical Background

Often confused with Pachisi, Chaupar is a more complex and strategic variant. It is famously referenced in the Hindu epic Mahabharata, where a high-stakes game leads to the Kurukshetra war.

⚖️ Original Rules

Played with three long dice (pasa) instead of cowrie shells. It involves complex rules for capturing opponents, forming blocks, and returning pieces to the start.

🌍 Modern Evolution

Though less globally known than Pachisi, Chaupar remains a popular traditional game in rural North India, often played on cloth boards.
