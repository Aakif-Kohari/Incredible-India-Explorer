Cyclone Biparjoy (2023)

6–19 June 2023 · Lifespan: 13 Days 3 Hours (Arabian Sea Record) · Landfall at Jakhau Port

Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm Biparjoy was a meteorological marvel and a landmark in modern disaster management. Traversing the Arabian Sea for an astonishing 13 days and 3 hours—making it the longest-lived cyclone in the Arabian Sea since modern satellite records began—Biparjoy executed a complex recurving trajectory before slamming into Gujarat's arid Kutch coastline near Jakhau Port with sustained winds of 125–140 km/h. Remarkably, precision forecasting and the proactive evacuation of over 100,000 people prevented a single direct storm fatality in Gujarat.

Meteorological Evolution

Formation, Recurving Trajectory & Record Arabian Sea Longevity

Biparjoy's protracted journey across the Arabian Sea provided atmospheric scientists unprecedented insights into steering currents and cyclonic stagnation.

Cyclogenesis & Unprecedented Longevity

Forming on 6 June 2023 from a low-pressure area in the southeast Arabian Sea, Biparjoy maintained cyclonic storm intensity or higher for over 13 consecutive days. Sustained by high ocean heat content (31°C–32°C), it broke the previous Arabian Sea longevity record of Cyclone Kyarr (2019).

Steering Dynamics & Recurvature toward Kutch

Initially steering almost due northward toward the Oman/Pakistan coast, Biparjoy encountered an upper-tropospheric westerly trough and an anticyclonic ridge that halted its northward progression, steering the system northeastward directly toward the Gulf of Kutch.

Key Meteorological Parameters

  • Naming: Proposed by Bangladesh; "Biparjoy" translates to "Disaster" or "Calamity" in the Bengali language.
  • Lowest Central Pressure: 958 hPa at peak Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm stage.
  • Accumulated Cyclone Energy (ACE): Generated the highest ACE index of any single Arabian Sea cyclone on record.
  • Landfall Coordinates: 23.23°N, 68.61°E near Jakhau Port in Abdasa taluka of Kutch on the night of 15 June 2023.
Regional Vulnerability

The Arid Coastline & Mangrove Geography of Kutch

Kutch possesses one of the world's most unique coastal ecologies: low-gradient mudflats, extensive salt pans, and dense Avicennia mangrove swamps.

Low-Lying Tidal Mudflats & Salt Pans

The vast intertidal expanse of the Gulf of Kutch creates exceptional vulnerability to storm surge amplification, where even moderate sea-level rise can submerge coastal villages kilometers inland.

The Strategic Ports Network

Kutch hosts critical national energy gateways including Deendayal Port (Kandla), Mundra Port, and Jakhau fishing harbor, requiring complete suspension of shipping operations.

Ecological Heritage & Pastoral Communities

Home to the Banni grasslands, desert wildlife sanctuaries, and Maldhari pastoralists who live in traditional mud-and-thatch circular huts (Bhungas).

Hazard Profile

Wind Gale Devastation & Torrential Inland Rainfall

Biparjoy crossed the coast between 18:30 and 23:30 IST on 15 June, maintaining destructive gales as it pushed across Gujarat into Rajasthan.

Electrical Grid & Infrastructure Damage

Winds of 140 km/h uprooted over 5,000 electric poles, flattened 800+ telecom towers, and damaged 1,000+ villages' power distribution lines across Kutch, Jamnagar, and Devbhumi Dwarka.

Extreme Flooding in Southwest Rajasthan

After crossing Gujarat, Biparjoy weakened into a deep depression over Rajasthan, dropping over 400 mm of rain in Jalore, Sirohi, and Barmer, causing historic flash floods in the Thar Desert.

Marine Hazards

Storm-Surge Inundation & Mangrove Buffer Protection

Storm surges of 2.0 to 3.5 meters breached coastal embankments across the Gulf of Kutch.

Seawater Intrusion in Salt Farms & Agriculture

Surge waters flooded thousands of hectares of coastal salt farming beds and date palm plantations, elevating soil salinity in Abdasa and Mandvi talukas.

Natural Buffer: Kutch Mangrove Ecosystems

Extensive coastal mangrove forests around Jakhau and the Kori Creek acted as natural shock absorbers, attenuating wave energy and shielding inland settlements from extreme hydraulic erosion.

Evacuation Operations

The Zero-Casualty Mega-Evacuation Operation

Recognized globally as a masterclass in early action, the Gujarat State Administration and NDMA evacuated over 108,000 citizens from 0–10 km coastal belts.

Door-to-Door Vulnerability Mapping

Administration mapped pregnant women, elderly citizens, children, and thatched-hut dwellers across 120 coastal villages in Kutch and Dwarka, transporting them to reinforced cyclone shelters 72 hours prior to landfall.

Multi-Agency Deployment (NDRF, SDRF & Armed Forces)

30 NDRF teams, 15 SDRF battalions, along with Indian Army column reserves and Coast Guard hovercraft units pre-positioned tree-clearing bulldozers, satellite satphones, and mobile water purifiers.

Best Practices

Disaster Management Lessons from Cyclone Biparjoy

Key institutional takeaways from Biparjoy that set the benchmark for global disaster risk reduction:

Early Action & Dynamic Sheltering

Proof that high-precision track forecasts paired with strict 0-10 km coastal exclusion zones can achieve zero direct storm fatalities even in high-intensity Category 3-equivalent landfalls.

Resilience of Bhunga Architecture

Traditional circular Bhunga houses with conical thatch roofs in Kutch once again proved aerodynamic superiority, suffering minimal structural roof loss compared to unreinforced flat-roof additions.

Inland Desert Flooding Protocols

Demonstrated the critical necessity for tracking decaying cyclonic remnants that generate extreme precipitation in normally arid desert river catchments hundreds of kilometers inland.
