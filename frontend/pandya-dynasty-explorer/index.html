Pandya Dynasty

Discover a long-lived Tamil dynasty whose pearl fisheries, literature and temple cities shaped southern India's history.

Historical overview

The Pandyas were based in the far south of the Tamil country, with Madurai as their celebrated political and cultural centre. Their history spans early historic rulers, periods of revival and the powerful later Pandyas of the 13th and 14th centuries.

Pearl trade

Korkai and the Gulf of Mannar were famous for pearl fisheries. Pearls, conch shell and coastal products joined routes to Sri Lanka, the Arabian Sea and Roman markets.

Architectural legacy

Later Pandya patronage contributed to temple expansion, gateway towers, rock-cut shrines and the vibrant sacred landscape around Madurai and Tirunelveli.

Timeline

  1. Early historic period: Pandya power and pearl fisheries appear in Tamil and foreign accounts.
  2. c. 6th–9th centuries: Pandya revival centres on Madurai and supports Tamil Shaiva and Vaishnava institutions.
  3. 13th century: The later Pandyas become a major South Indian power and expand overseas trade.
  4. 14th century: Political fragmentation allows successor states and new regional powers to emerge.

Major rulers

Nedunjeliyan

Early Pandya ruler celebrated in Sangam poems and associated with Madurai.

Maravarman Sundara Pandyan

A later ruler during Pandya expansion and temple patronage.

Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan

A powerful 13th-century king remembered for wealth, conquest and grand endowments.
