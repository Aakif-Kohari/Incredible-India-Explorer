History & visual language

When cloth becomes a story

Kalamkari is a family of Indian textile-painting traditions associated especially with Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The word is commonly linked to kalam, a pen, and kari, work. Artists draw and paint on cloth using a combination of outlining, natural dyes and controlled mordanting processes.

01

Origins & practice

Temple and narrative textile traditions used painted cloth to communicate religious stories, figures and decorative themes.

02

Natural palette

Traditional practice can use plant, mineral and other natural sources for colours, with mordants helping pigments bind to the fabric.

03

Motifs as language

Flora, animals, trees, borders, deities and narrative scenes create a visual vocabulary that can be read across a panel.

04

Artisan knowledge

Preparation, drawing, dyeing, washing and repeated colour application require patience and skilled control of the cloth and pigments.

Regional traditions

One name, several practices

Kalamkari is not a single fixed recipe. Regional and workshop traditions have their own materials, subjects and techniques.

SRISAILAM / TEMPLE TRADITIONS

Narrative cloth

Painted textiles can carry devotional figures, episodes and ornamental borders, connecting visual art with storytelling.

MACHILIPATNAM TRADITION

Block & pen work

The Machilipatnam/Kalamkari tradition is known for textile decoration using hand-drawn and block-printed elements.

ANDHRA PRADESH & TELANGANA

Living craft ecology

Artists and craft communities continue to adapt motifs, products and presentation while retaining knowledge of traditional processes.
