Kutch Embroidery

Explore Gujarat's rich embroidery traditions, regional styles, stitches, motifs, and artisan heritage.

Historical Background

Kutch embroidery is a handicraft and textile signature art tradition of the tribal community of Kutch District in Gujarat, India. This embroidery with its rich designs has made a significant contribution to the Indian embroidery traditions. Tracing its roots back to the 16th and 17th centuries, the art was brought to the region by migrating communities, possibly starting with the Kathis.

Materials & Tools

  • 🧵 Fabric: Traditionally cotton and silk fabrics are used as the base.
  • 🧶 Threads: Vibrant silken and cotton threads in bright colors (red, yellow, green, blue).
  • 🪡 Needles: Various sizes of needles depending on the stitch intricacy.
  • ✨ Mirrors (Abhla): Small mirrors in different shapes used extensively to embellish the fabric.

Artisan Communities

The craft is primarily practiced by women of various communities, including the Rabari, Ahir, Jat, and Mutwa. Each community has its own distinct style and set of motifs that reflect their culture, social status, and daily life. The art is passed down from mother to daughter as a treasured heirloom.
