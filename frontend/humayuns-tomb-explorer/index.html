Humayun's Tomb

Discover the Birth of Mughal Garden-Tomb Architecture

📍 Nizamuddin East, Delhi, India

History & Background

Humayun was the second Mughal emperor, son of Babur. His reign was marked by turbulent periods of exile and eventual restoration of the Mughal Empire. He died tragically in 1556 after falling down the stairs of his library.

The tomb was commissioned in 1569 by his first wife and chief consort, Empress Bega Begum (also known as Hamida Banu Begum), who dedicated her life to its construction.

Tomb Architecture

  • Timeline: Built between 1569 and 1572.
  • Architects: Designed by Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas and his son Sayyid Muhammad.
  • Style: A striking synthesis of Persian and early Indian Mughal influences, characterized by perfect symmetrical design.
  • Structure: It features a massive central double dome, sitting upon a raised platform accessed through towering arched entrances (iwans).

The Charbagh Gardens

The monument is set within a traditional Persian Charbagh (four-part garden) layout. It represents the concept of the Islamic paradise garden on Earth.

Water channels divide the garden into four perfectly symmetrical square sections. Stone pathways intersect to create geometric symmetry that leads the eye directly toward the central mausoleum.

Materials & Design

The primary building material is striking red sandstone, which contrasts beautifully with intricate white and black marble details and inlay work.

The roofline is adorned with traditional chhatris (dome-shaped pavilions) and ornamental arches that soften the monument's massive scale.

Architectural Influence

Humayun's Tomb is a crucial transitional monument in Mughal architecture. It was the first substantial example of the garden-tomb tradition in the Indian subcontinent.

This structural prototype directly influenced the design of many later Mughal monuments, most notably establishing the architectural foundation that eventually culminated in the Taj Mahal.

Interesting Facts & UNESCO Status

The complex is often called the "Dormitory of the Mughals" as it contains the tombs of over 150 members of the Mughal dynasty.

Recognized for its extraordinary cultural and architectural significance, it was officially added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993.
