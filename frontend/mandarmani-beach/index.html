Location

Where it sits

Mandarmani is a seaside village at the northern end of the Bay of Bengal, in the East Medinipur (Purba Medinipur) district of West Bengal — on the Kolkata–Digha coastal route, between the fishing town of Shankarpur and the beach at Tajpur.

By Road

+

The most common route from Kolkata, via the Kolkata–Digha highway to Chaulkhola, then local roads into the village. A paved back road runs behind the beach to the hotels, since private cars are restricted from driving straight onto the sand.

By Rail

+

The nearest railway stations are at Contai and Digha, roughly 30–37 km away. From either, an auto-rickshaw or taxi covers the rest of the way in under an hour.

By Air

+

Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose International Airport in Kolkata is the nearest airport, around 180–191 km away — about a 4 to 4.5 hour drive to the coast.

Beach Characteristics

What the sand is like

Mandarmani's defining trait is underfoot: the sand packs hard enough to drive on, running flat and wide from the dunes to the tide line for the length of the village.

Hard, flat sand

The compact, gently sloping sand is firm enough for vehicles at low tide — the reason Mandarmani is described as India's longest "drive-in" beach.

Whitish to chocolate-brown sand

Visitors describe the shade differently by season and light — from pale, clean sand to a soft, chocolate-brown tone closer to the waterline.

Low, calm tidal waves

The surf here is relatively gentle and low compared with nearby Digha — some compare its flat sea bed to Odisha's Chandipur beach.

A working road, too

Because vehicles can drive on it, the beach doubles as a route between hotels and villages — though sand-driving for thrill rides is discouraged for the shore's sake.

Family-friendly gradient

A fairly flat shore with a gradual entry into the water, generally regarded as an easy, unhurried beach for families with children.

A quieter alternative

Compared with the busier beaches at Digha, Mandarmani keeps a calmer, less crowded atmosphere outside of peak weekends.

🌊 Tidal Cycles & Driveability Guide

Low Tide vs. High Tide

Mandarmani's sand hardens during low tide and submerges at high tide. Plan vehicle movements and walks accordingly.

Low Tide Phase

Hard Beach Walking & Crabs

The sea recedes hundreds of meters, revealing thousands of red ghost crabs and compact driving-grade sand flats.

High Tide Phase

Embankment Waves & No Driving

Water surges up to the resort fences; driving on sand is strictly prohibited for safety and environmental protection.

Best Drive Time

Early Dawn & Dusk

Gentle sea breeze, cooler sand temperatures, and clear views along the 13-km coastline stretch.

Natural Surroundings

Life along the shore

  • Red crab colonies

    Thousands of small red crabs scatter across the wet sand — one of the beach's best-known, most-photographed sights.

  • Small fishing hamlets

    The beach is fringed by modest fishing villages, with boats drawn up along the shore against the backdrop of the sea.

  • A river estuary nearby

    Locals can guide visitors to a nearby estuary, where a river meets the tide — a quieter, scenic detour from the main beach.

  • Open horizon, few structures

    Much of the shoreline stays undeveloped and open, giving long, uninterrupted views up and down the coast.

Local Highlights

What makes it distinct

Fresh, straight-off-the-boat seafood

Beach shacks and small dhabas along the sand serve Bengali-style fish, prawns, crab and pomfret — simple, unfussy meals eaten within earshot of the waves.

Chilli crabFried pomfretPrawnsLuchi torkari

Digha–Mandarmani Festival

Held each January, with Bengali bands and a lively coastal atmosphere drawing visitors from across the region.

Seashell thrift market

Small stalls near the beach sell ornaments and objects crafted from local seashells.

A fishing-hamlet-turned-resort-town

Once a small fishing settlement, Mandarmani has grown into a fast-developing string of beachfront resorts while keeping its village character.
