The Living Threshold Art of India

Rangoli

A sacred geometry drawn at dawn, offered to the cosmos, and let go by dusk.

Long before it was a festival contest, rangoli was a daily prayer in powder: a handful of stone-ground rice flour, a nimble hand, and a doorstep consecrated before the first rays of sunlight. Every region in India speaks this visual language through its own grammar — Kolam, Muggu, Alpana, Mandana, Aripana, Pookalam — welcoming deities, ancestors, and passing creatures into the household.

✨ Impermanence as Devotion 🌿 Eco-Hospitality (Bhuta Yajna) 📐 Sacred Symmetry

01The Philosophy of Impermanence

Rangoli is fundamentally a threshold art (*Dwara Puja*) — drawn at the entrance of a home, inner courtyard, or temple sanctum, predominantly by women before the day's first footsteps disturb the morning dust. Unlike architecture or sculpture, rangoli is created with the deliberate understanding that it will not endure: wind, rain, and wandering feet erase it within hours, and the artist redraws it the next morning.

Beyond aesthetics, using edible rice flour (*Arisi Maavu*) embodies the ancient Vedic duty of *Bhuta Yajna* — daily charity offered to nature. Small birds, ants, and ground insects feed on the threshold drawings, turning the artwork into an act of coexistence that bridges the domestic realm with the wild ecosystem.

02Regional Names & Dialects

As one travels across India, ground drawing transforms its material, geometric rules, and spiritual intent every few hundred kilometres.

03The Sacred Grammar of Symmetry

Beneath the colourful powders lies an ancient mathematical discipline of topology, knot theory, and cosmic order.

Pulli / Chukka (The Dot Matrix)

The universe begins with the unmanifest point (*Bindu*). Dots are plotted in square grids (*Nerkodu*) or interlocking triangles (*Kanni*) to anchor the cosmic matrix.

Sikku / Brahma Mudi (Endless Knots)

Continuous closed loops that wind around dots without touching them, symbolising eternity, protection against negative energies, and the cyclical nature of time.

Mandala (Concentric Radiance)

Multi-fold radial symmetries (4, 8, 12, or 16 petals) reflecting the solar calendar, the lotus of consciousness, and planetary orbits.

04Symbolic Motifs & Natural Pigments

Every element placed on the earth carries symbolic weight and natural resonance.
