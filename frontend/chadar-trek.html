Chadar Trek

Trek profile

Chadar at a glance

The Chadar Trek follows sections of the Zanskar River when winter cold creates a walkable ice sheet. Conditions are variable and the route is controlled by local authorities and operators.

Why it is unique

The frozen-river landscape

During the coldest part of winter, the Zanskar River can form a continuous or semi-continuous sheet of ice known as the “Chadar”. The surface can change from hard blue ice to snow-covered plates, wet sections and open leads.

The trail is enclosed by steep canyon walls. Trekkers may alternate between walking on the ice and using ledges beside the river when the frozen surface is unsafe or impassable.

  • Ice patterns and translucent blue surfaces
  • High canyon walls and narrow gorges
  • Frozen waterfalls and winter formations
  • Remote camps and Zanskari village culture

Important

Chadar is a condition-dependent trek

A route that was safe yesterday can become unsafe after temperature changes, snowfall, moving ice or water exposure. Never treat a published itinerary as a guarantee of current trail conditions.

Route

Major route highlights

Exact camps and walking distances vary with ice conditions. The following sequence describes the commonly associated route rather than a fixed day-by-day promise.

01

Leh acclimatization

Most itineraries begin with time in Leh to adjust to altitude before the river section. Hydration, rest and symptom monitoring are essential.

02

Leh → Chilling

The road journey approaches the Zanskar valley and the usual trekking start area around Chilling. The final start point can shift with road and route conditions.

03

Chilling → river camps

The route descends toward the frozen river and follows its canyon. Camp locations are selected according to ice, water, weather and local guidance.

04

Deep canyon sections

Narrow gorges, frozen cascades and high rock walls create the signature Chadar scenery. The walking line can switch between ice and the riverbank.

05

Nerak area

The Nerak region is a major highlight on classic itineraries, associated with the frozen waterfall and deeper Zanskar canyon landscape.

06

Return to Chilling

The return follows the river corridor, but the surface may look and behave differently. Guides reassess the safest line each day.

Conditions

What the ice can be like

Hard ice

Firm, textured ice can allow efficient walking but may be extremely slippery.

Soft snow

Fresh snow can conceal uneven surfaces and make each step more demanding.

Wet patches

Water can appear through weak points or near river margins. Waterproof footwear matters.

Open water

Never step onto open leads or thin-looking ice. Follow the guide's route around them.

Altitude

Acclimatization matters

Leh sits at high altitude, and the trek corridor is also a high-altitude environment. Arrive early enough to acclimatize according to the operator's itinerary.

Headache, nausea, unusual fatigue, dizziness or breathlessness can indicate altitude illness. Symptoms should be taken seriously rather than “walked off”.

Do not descend alone.Tell your guide immediately if you develop concerning symptoms.

Safety first

Cold, ice and altitude require preparation

The Chadar Trek is not an ordinary winter hike. Use current official guidance, local permissions and a qualified local operator before attempting it.

❄

Extreme cold

Layer clothing, protect extremities and carry reliable insulation. Overnight temperatures can be far below freezing.

🧊

Ice safety

Do not walk ahead of the guide. Ice thickness and stability can vary with temperature, current and river flow.

⛰

Altitude

Acclimatize before trekking and report symptoms early. Follow the expedition's medical and evacuation plan.

🧭

Navigation

Stay with the group. Canyon conditions can make independent route-finding difficult, especially during poor visibility.

🥾

Footwear

Use insulated, waterproof boots with traction appropriate for ice. Carry spare socks and keep feet dry.

📱

Communication

Connectivity can be limited. Confirm the group's emergency communication and evacuation arrangements before departure.

Responsible trekking

Leave the frozen valley as you found it

Pack out waste

Carry all packaging and personal waste back to designated disposal points.

Respect communities

Ask before photographing people, homes or religious sites and follow local customs.

Protect the ice

Use established camps and routes. Avoid damaging frozen formations for photographs.

Use local expertise

Local guides understand river behaviour, route changes and community protocols.
