Yoga and Wellness

The Origins and History of Yoga

Yoga is a profound system of holistic health and spiritual awakening that originated in ancient India thousands of years ago. Its roots can be traced back to the Indus-Sarasvati civilization in Northern India over 5,000 years ago, with the word "yoga" first mentioned in the oldest sacred texts, the Rig Veda.

The practice evolved through the pre-classical period (Upanishads), classical period (defined by Patanjali's Yoga Sutras), and post-classical period, eventually reaching the modern era where it has become a global phenomenon promoting physical and mental well-being.

Patanjali and the Yoga Sutras

Sage Patanjali is often considered the father of classical yoga. He compiled the Yoga Sutras around 200 CE. This foundational text consists of 196 aphorisms outlining the philosophy and practice of yoga. Patanjali defined yoga as "Yogas chitta vritti nirodhah" — the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind.

The Eight Limbs of Yoga (Ashtanga)

Patanjali outlined an eight-fold path designed to provide structural guidelines for living a meaningful and purposeful life.

Major Yoga Traditions

Hatha Yoga

Focus: Physical purification and balance.
The most widely practiced form globally, focusing on Asanas (postures) and Pranayama (breath control).

Raja Yoga

Focus: Mental control and meditation.
The "Royal Path" encompassing the eight limbs, focusing heavily on internal concentration and Samadhi.

Bhakti Yoga

Focus: Devotion and love.
The path of the heart, emphasizing devotion to the Divine through chanting, singing, and prayer.

Karma Yoga

Focus: Selfless action.
The path of service, performing duties without attachment to the outcome, purifying the heart.

Jnana Yoga

Focus: Wisdom and intellect.
The path of self-inquiry and intellectual contemplation to discern the real from the unreal.

Kundalini Yoga

Focus: Spiritual energy.
Aims to awaken dormant energy at the base of the spine through breath, mantras, and dynamic postures.

Ayurveda: The Science of Life

Ayurveda, originating over 3,000 years ago, is India's traditional natural healing system. It emphasizes a preventative and holistic approach to health, striving to balance the body, mind, spirit, and environment.

According to Ayurveda, everything in the universe is composed of five elements: Space (Ether), Air, Fire, Water, and Earth. These elements combine in the human body to form three life forces or energies, known as Doshas.

Dosha Elements Characteristics
Vata Space + Air Creative, energetic, governs movement.
Pitta Fire + Water Focused, intense, governs metabolism and digestion.
Kapha Earth + Water Calm, stable, governs structure and lubrication.
