History

The Diu Fort, also known as 'Praça de Diu' in Portuguese, is a massive fort built by the Portuguese in 1535. The fort was constructed following a defense alliance forged between Bahadur Shah, the Sultan of Gujarat, and the Portuguese, to defend against the Mughal Emperor Humayun.

Once the Mughal threat receded, Bahadur Shah attempted to remove the Portuguese, leading to a conflict in which he was killed. The fort subsequently became a key stronghold for the Portuguese empire in India.

Portuguese Rule

Diu Fort served as one of the most vital strategic nodes of the Portuguese State of India (Estado da Índia). It successfully withstood major sieges by the Ottoman Empire and the Sultanate of Gujarat in 1538 and 1546.

The fort remained under Portuguese control for over four centuries, from its construction in 1535 until 1961, when Diu, along with Goa and Daman, was annexed by the Indian Armed Forces during Operation Vijay.

Coastal Defences

Bordered by the sea on three sides, Diu Fort is a masterpiece of maritime military architecture. Its coastal defenses were designed to repel naval attacks, featuring a double moat (one tidal) that isolated the fort from the mainland.

The seaward walls are incredibly thick and lined with numerous bronze cannons that still point out towards the Arabian Sea, serving as a reminder of the fort's impenetrable naval defense strategy.

Architecture

The fort's layout exemplifies classic European fortification techniques adapted to the Indian coastal environment:

  • Ramparts & Bastions: Huge stone bastions named after saints (e.g., St. George, St. John) dot the perimeter.
  • Chapels: The fort houses several ancient chapels, though most are in ruins today.
  • Cisterns & Granaries: Large underground reservoirs and storerooms were built to withstand long sieges.
  • Intricate Gateways: The main entrance features detailed stonework with Portuguese coats of arms.

Lighthouse

Perched on the highest point of the fort is the Diu Lighthouse. Climbing to the top offers spectacular, uninterrupted views of the Arabian Sea and the entire Diu town. The lighthouse still serves as a navigational aid for ships sailing along the western coast of India.

Interesting Facts

  • Seven Wonders: Diu Fort was selected as one of the Seven Wonders of Portuguese Origin in the World (New Seven Wonders).
  • Panikota Fort: Visible from Diu Fort is the Fortim do Mar (Panikota Fort), a sea fort built on an island in the bay.
  • Cannon Collection: It boasts an impressive collection of broad-gauge cannons, many bearing intricate Portuguese engravings.
