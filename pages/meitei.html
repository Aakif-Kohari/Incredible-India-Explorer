Origins & Presence

The Tibeto-Burman Jewel

Meitei (also officially known as Manipuri) is a Tibeto-Burman language originating from the fertile plains of the Manipur valley in Northeast India.

Functioning as a lingua franca across the highly diverse and polyglot region of Manipur, it connects various ethnic tribes. The language's complex tonal system and distinct agglutinative grammar set it apart firmly from the Indo-Aryan languages of mainland India.

Though written in the Bengali-Assamese script for centuries following religious shifts in the 18th century, a massive modern cultural revival has successfully resurrected its ancient indigenous script: Meitei Mayek.

Polite Foundations

The Honorable Greeting

Meitei culture places a robust emphasis on politeness, respect for elders, and calm temperaments. Understanding how to politely greet someone opens doors across the entire state.

[ Khurumjari ]
With Boundless Respect
(Formal Greeting / Namaste)

Often accompanied by a slight bow or folded hands, Khurumjari is constructed from "Khurumaba" (to bow/worship). It signifies immense respect to the recipient.

Chronicles & Arts

A Documented Empire

Cheitharol Kumbaba

The royal court chronicles of the Kings of Manipur. This incredibly rare historical text meticulously records state events from 33 AD, providing an unbroken linguistic and dynastic record unmatched in the Northeast.

Eighth Schedule (1992)

Added to the Indian Constitution during the 71st Amendment alongside Konkani and Nepali, ensuring Manipuri operates strictly as an official language with full government patronage and Sahitya Akademi resources.

Raas Leela Arts

A significant portion of classical Meitei literature orbits visually around the Manipuri classical dances, mapping precise linguistic poetry to physical storytelling exploring Vaishnavism and indigenous folklore.
