Prabhavati Gupta

Gupta Princess, Vakataka Queen Regent, and Stateswoman

Introduction & Early Life

Prabhavati Gupta (c. 4th–5th century CE) was a powerful royal woman of the classical Gupta Empire. Born as the daughter of the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II and queen Kuberanaga, her life offers exceptional insights into statecraft, dynastic alliances, and female political authority in early historic India.

The Gupta–Vakataka Alliance

To secure his southern flank and counter the Western Kshatrapas, Chandragupta II arranged the strategic marriage of his daughter Prabhavati Gupta to Rudrasena II, the king of the Vakataka dynasty. This alliance united two of the most influential political powers of ancient India, granting the Guptas decisive indirect influence over the Deccan plateau.

Role as Regent

Following the untimely death of her husband Rudrasena II around 390 CE, Prabhavati Gupta assumed full administrative control as regent on behalf of her young sons, Divakarasena and Damodarasena. She governed the Vakataka kingdom effectively for over a decade, issuing royal charters, directing state policies, and maintaining close diplomatic ties with her imperial Gupta family.

Copper-Plate Inscriptions & Political Authority

Her historical legacy is remarkably well-documented through epigraphical records, most notably the Paddaghatta and Rithpur copper-plate inscriptions issued under her authority. These epigraphs are vital to historical research because they explicitly bear her name, her royal titles, and records of land grants, demonstrating that women from royal families could exercise sovereign administrative and executive powers in early India.

Geographical Context: Gupta & Vakataka Territories

The Gupta Empire spanned Northern and Central India, while the Vakataka kingdom occupied the strategic regions of Vidarbha and parts of the Deccan. Prabhavati Gupta's reign bridged these two pivotal geopolitical zones.

Chronological Timeline

  • c. 380 CE: Birth and early upbringing as princess of the Gupta Empire under Chandragupta II.
  • c. 390 CE: Marriage to Vakataka king Rudrasena II, consolidating the Gupta-Vakataka alliance.
  • c. 390–410 CE: Regency period; Prabhavati Gupta rules the Vakataka kingdom as queen regent for her minor sons.
  • Issuance of Charters: Publication of notable copper-plate inscriptions detailing land grants and administrative acts.
  • Later Years: Continued influence during the reigns of her sons, fostering art, religion, and trade.
