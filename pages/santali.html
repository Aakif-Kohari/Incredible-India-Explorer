Roots & Geography

The Austroasiatic Tapestry

Santali is the most widely spoken language of the Munda subfamily of the Austroasiatic languages, predating the influx of Indo-Aryan and Dravidian linguistic groups into the subcontinent.

Spoken predominantly by the Santhal people across Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, and Bihar, it bridges modern India with its ancient tribal bedrock. With over 7.6 million active speakers, it remains a living, evolving testament to indigenous resilience.

Modern Recognition

8th Schedule Integration

92nd Amendment (2003)

Santali officially entered the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, cementing its geopolitical status and ensuring systemic government preservation efforts across its core states.

Pandit Raghunath Murmu

Revered as Guru Gomke (The Great Teacher), Murmu's invention of Ol Chiki unified the language, shifting it from a purely oral tradition utilizing foreign scripts to a proud literate engine.

Sahitya Akademi

Since its inclusion, Santali literature continually wins national literary awards, rapidly expanding printed encyclopedias, poetry collections, and modern novels preserving the tribal ethos.
