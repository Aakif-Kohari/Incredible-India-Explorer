The Divine Foundling

Who Was Andal?

📜

Historical Evidence

What scholars can confirm

Andal (also known as Goda Devi or Kothai) is the only female among the twelve Alvars — Tamil Vaishnavite poet-saints who composed devotional hymns to Vishnu between the 6th and 9th centuries CE. She is traditionally dated to the 8th century CE and associated with the Sri Vaishnava tradition. Her two works — the Tiruppavai (30 verses) and Nachiyar Tirumozhi (143 verses) — are part of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the canonical collection of Alvar hymns.

  • Associated with Srivilliputhur, Tamil Nadu
  • c. 8th century CE (approximate)
  • 173 verses in Naalayira Divya Prabandham
  • Only female Alvar among twelve
🙏

Traditional Accounts

Stories passed through devotion

The hagiographic tradition tells of a Vaishnavite saint named Vishnuchitta (later known as Periyalvar) who found a baby girl beneath a tulsi plant in his garden. He named her Kothai and raised her with intense devotion to Vishnu. From childhood, Kothai was obsessed with marrying Vishnu — she adorned the temple idol with garlands, tried them on herself first to check their beauty, and composed passionate poems expressing her longing for the divine husband.

  • Found as a baby beneath a tulsi plant
  • Raised by Vishnuchitta (Periyalvar) at Srivilliputhur
  • Childhood devotion to Vishnu idol
  • Garland incident led to divine union
🌺

The Garland Incident

Andal's most famous traditional story: she secretly tried on the garlands meant for the Vishnu idol before offering them — a transgression considered deeply impure by temple standards. One night, Vishnu appeared in Periyalvar's dream and demanded: "I will only wear garlands that Andal has worn." This revelation led to Andal being married to Vishnu in a grand ceremony at Srirangam, after which she was never seen again — believed to have merged with the deity.

Chronicle of Devotion

The Life of Andal

From foundling to poet-saint to divine bride — Andal's life was a living poem of devotion that transcended mortal boundaries.

🌱

Discovery at Srivilliputhur

c. 8th century CE

The Vaishnavite poet-saint Vishnuchitta (later Periyalvar) found a baby girl beneath a tulsi (holy basil) plant in his garden at Srivilliputhur. He named her Kothai and raised her as his own. The tulsi connection was considered auspicious — tulsi being sacred to Vishnu. From infancy, Kothai displayed an unusual attachment to Vishnu worship.

Some traditions say she was a celestial nymph (Apsara) sent to earth, while others say she was a gift from Vishnu himself.
🌺

Childhood Devotion

Growing years

Kothai spent her days making garlands for the Vishnu temple. She would secretly try on each garland before offering it — believing that if the garland looked beautiful on her, it would please Vishnu even more. Her father, unaware, continued his devotional work. The temple priests eventually discovered her practice, and Vishnuchitta was horrified — but Vishnu had other plans.

The "garland wearing" (maal polarity) became a central image in Andal's later poetry — she saw herself as the ideal devotee adorned for God.
✍️

Composing the Tiruppavai

During Margazhi month

During the sacred month of Margazhi (December–January), Kothai composed the Tiruppavai — 30 verses describing her dream of gathering young women to perform austerities and worship Vishnu. The poems express her longing for divine marriage and her desire to serve as a bride of God. She adopted the name Andal (meaning "she who rules") during this period.

The Tiruppavai is recited every Margazhi morning in Vaishnavite households across Tamil Nadu — a tradition dating back over 1,000 years.
💍

The Divine Marriage

c. 8th century CE

According to tradition, Vishnu appeared in Periyalvar's dream and declared he would marry Andal. The wedding was celebrated with grand festivities at Srirangam — Andal was dressed as a bride and carried to the temple. When the doors opened, she had vanished — believed to have merged with the Vishnu idol. This event is celebrated annually as the Andal wedding festival.

This narrative parallels the stories of Andal's counterparts — Mirabai's absorption at Dwarka, and the divine marriage traditions of other Bhakti saints.
📖

The Nachiyar Tirumozhi

Composed during lifetime

Andal's second major work, the Nachiyar Tirumozhi (143 verses), is a collection of passionate, intimate poems expressing her longing for Vishnu. Unlike the communal Tiruppavai, these are deeply personal — addressing God as lover, husband, and beloved. The poems borrow imagery from Tamil Sangam literature's love poetry, transforming erotic longing into spiritual devotion.

The Nachiyar Tirumozhi is considered one of the most emotionally intense devotional works in all of Indian literature.
🕉️

Eternal Legacy

8th century CE onwards

Andal was canonised as one of the twelve Alvars and her works became part of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham — the "4,000 Divine Verses" that form the Tamil canon of Vaishnavism. She is worshipped as a goddess in her own right, with the Srivilliputhur temple — her birthplace — being one of the most important Vishnu temples in Tamil Nadu.

Andal is the presiding deity of Srivilliputhur temple, and her image appears alongside Vishnu in many South Indian temples.

The Sacred 30 Verses

The Tiruppavai

Andal's Tiruppavai is a 30-verse hymn composed during the month of Margazhi, describing a dream of gathering maidens to worship Vishnu. It is one of the most recited devotional texts in Tamil Vaishnavism.

The closing verse — Andal asks Vishnu to accept her as his servant and bride. It culminates in the declaration that she belongs to him alone, completing the arc from devotee to divine consort.

Significance of Tiruppavai

The Tiruppavai is recited daily during the month of Margazhi in Vaishnavite households and temples across Tamil Nadu. It established the practice of Margazhi bhajans — pre-dawn devotional singing that continues to this day. The text is also significant for its use of Tamil (Senthamizh) rather than Sanskrit, making devotion accessible to common people. Ramanuja, the great Vaishnavite philosopher, elevated the Tiruppavai to canonical status alongside the Vedas.

Living Traditions

Festivals & Temple Traditions

🌺

Andal Kalyanam

The annual divine wedding festival of Andal and Vishnu, celebrated at Srirangam and Srivilliputhur. The festival recreates the traditional wedding ceremony, with Andal adorned as a bride. It typically falls in the Tamil month of Aadi (July–August) and attracts thousands of devotees.

🎵

Margazhi Bhajans

During the month of Margazhi (December–January), devotees recite the Tiruppavai at pre-dawn hours. Temples across Tamil Nadu conduct Tiruppavai recitals and bhajan sessions. This tradition, dating back over 1,000 years, is one of the oldest continuous devotional practices in Indian culture.

🏛️

Srivilliputhur Temple

The Andal Temple at Srivilliputhur is one of the most important Vishnu temples in Tamil Nadu. It is the birthplace of Andal and Periyalvar, and its 192-foot gopuram (tower) is the official symbol of the Tamil Nadu government. The temple celebrates Andal's wedding annually.

🪷

Vaikunta Ekadashi

The most important Vaishnavite festival, celebrating Vishnu's cosmic gate (Vaikunta). At Srirangam and other Andal-associated temples, special ceremonies honour Andal's devotion. Devotees believe she stands at Vishnu's side eternally, welcoming the blessed into Vaikunta.

🎶

Andal Music Festival

Annual music festivals celebrating Andal's compositions are held across Tamil Nadu. Carnatic musicians perform compositions inspired by her verses, including the famous "Azhai Pozhirukkum" and "Vaadinen Vaadi". These festivals bridge classical music and devotional tradition.

💐

Garland Traditions

In many Vishnu temples, the garland tradition associated with Andal continues — fresh garlands are offered to the deity daily, and at Srivilliputhur, the tulsi garland (symbolising Andal's discovery) is specially prepared and offered to Vishnu.

Enduring Influence

Cultural Legacy & Modern Resonance

🎭

Performing Arts

Andal's life and poetry have inspired Bharatanatyam dance compositions, Carnatic music classics, and Tamizh theater. The famous Bharatanatyam piece "Andal Nrityam" is performed at major dance festivals worldwide. Her story also appears in Tamil cinema and television.

🎨

Visual Art & Iconography

Andal is depicted in Tanjore paintings, temple sculptures, and modern art. Her iconic image — adorned with tulsi garlands, holding a parrot, wearing a red sari — appears in homes and temples across South India. She is often shown alongside Vishnu as his consort.

📚

Literary Influence

Andal influenced poets from Alvars to modern Tamil writers. Her use of Sangam love imagery for devotional purposes created a new literary tradition. Scholars like A.K. Ramanujan and Srividya Natarajan have studied her work extensively.

🌍

Global Recognition

Andal's works are studied in religious studies, women's studies, and Tamil literature courses worldwide. Translations by scholars like Srividya Natarajan and Ramanujan brought her to international audiences. She is celebrated as one of India's earliest female literary voices.

👩

Feminist Icon

Andal is celebrated as one of India's earliest female literary voices. Her assertive devotion — declaring herself the bride of God, composing passionate poetry — challenged patriarchal norms. She proved that a woman's spiritual voice could be as powerful as any male saint's.

🏛️

Government Symbol

The gopuram of Srivilliputhur temple — Andal's birthplace — is the official emblem of the Tamil Nadu government. This makes Andal's legacy literally inscribed on the state's identity — a remarkable testament to her enduring cultural significance.
