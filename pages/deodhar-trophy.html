The Grand Old Man

Prof. D. B. Deodhar

Dinkar Balwant Deodhar (1892–1993) is the revered patriarch of Indian cricket. While he predominantly played first-class cricket from 1911 to 1947, he was vastly influential off the pitch. A professor of Sanskrit, he seamlessly bridged academic discipline with athletic rigor.

Deodhar was vice-president of the BCCI and the undisputed authority on domestic talent. The tournament was initiated in 1973-74 to recognize his unprecedented contribution—a towering tribute to a man who lived to 101 and watched Indian cricket conquer the world.

His wisdom shaped the very foundation of how cricket operates internally within the subcontinent.

List A Structure

The Ultimate Selector's Stage

Serving as the 50-over equivalent to the first-class Duleep Trophy, the Deodhar Trophy pits the elite performers of the Vijay Hazare Trophy against each other in a fierce knockout / round-robin environment.

List A Status

Every run scored and wicket taken here is officially etched into global List A records, making it a critical metric for international white-ball selection.

Vijay Hazare Bridge

Historically, Deodhar Trophy squads are exclusively drafted from the top performers of the concurrent season's Vijay Hazare Trophy circuit.

The Scouting Ground

National selectors use this high-pressure microcosm to evaluate fringe players right before major ODI World Cups and Champions Trophy tournaments.

Constant Reinvention

Format Evolution Timeline

The BCCI has repeatedly overhauled the Deodhar Trophy's structure to optimize competitiveness. Explore how the tournament shifted from a simple Zonal clash to a highly curated talent pool.

1973 - 2015

The Inter-Zonal Era

For over 40 years, the tournament functioned exactly like the Duleep Trophy. Teams were hard-locked into geographic grids: North Zone, South Zone, East Zone, West Zone, and Central Zone.

It was a straight knockout tournament. While it fostered regional rivalry, national selectors felt dominant zones often overshadowed brilliant individual players trapped in weaker zones.

The India A/B Shift (2015-18)

The BCCI completely scrapped the regional system. They introduced a radical 3-team approach to maximize talent density.

The winner of the Vijay Hazare Trophy played as an independent team, while the BCCI selectors hand-picked two all-star squads: India A and India B.

Vijay Hazare Chapmion India A India B

The Trilateral Pinnacle (2018-2023)

Determined to make the tournament the ultimate selection trial, the domestic champion team was removed entirely.

Instead, selectors began drafting three perfectly balanced super-teams: India A, India B, and India C. This created a pure round-robin crucible where the top two advanced to the Grand Final.

India A India B India C

Return to Roots (2023-Present)

Following a pandemic hiatus, the BCCI stunningly reversed course. The Deodhar Trophy reverted back to the inter-zonal format.

However, an additional North East Zone was introduced, reflecting the BCCI's aggressive expansion into new domestic territories, rounding the competition to 6 teams.
