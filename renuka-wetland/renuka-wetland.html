Renuka Wetland

01 · A Himalayan Landmark

History

Renuka Wetland lies about 38 km northeast of Nahan, the headquarters of Sirmaur district, in the lower Sivalik hills of Himachal Pradesh. The lake has endured through natural disasters and tectonic shifts across geological history, fed today by a small stream that gathers from the surrounding lower Himalayan slopes before draining into the Giri River. Its banks are wrapped in lush sub-tropical forest, and the surrounding hills have long drawn pilgrims and travelers along the routes linking the Sirmaur hills to the plains.

The area around the lake was formally protected as the Renuka Wildlife Sanctuary, and in November 2005 the wetland itself was designated a Ramsar Site of international importance — recognition of both its ecological value and its long-standing religious significance to the region.

02 · The Legend of Renuka

Mythological Significance

The lake is named for Renuka, wife of the sage Jamadagni and mother of Parashurama, an avatar of Vishnu revered in Hindu tradition. According to the legend most commonly told here, Jamadagni — angered by a momentary lapse in his wife's devotion — commanded his sons to behead her; only Parashurama obeyed, and it was this same unwavering obedience that later led his father to grant him the boon of restoring her to life. The lake is said to have taken shape where Renuka's story unfolded, and locals point out that its outline resembles a woman lying in repose — the natural landmark understood as her form.

The Renuka Ji temple stands on the lakeshore as the focus of this devotion, and each year on the eve of Prabodhini Ekadashi (typically October–November) the multi-day Renuka Fair draws thousands of pilgrims to commemorate the reunion of Parashurama and his mother, with processions carrying palanquins of local deities around the lake.

03 · How the Lake Stays Full

Lake Ecosystem

Renuka is a natural freshwater lake with a circumference of roughly 3.2 km, making it the largest lake in Himachal Pradesh by that measure — even though the Ramsar-designated wetland area itself is a compact 20 hectares, among the smallest Ramsar sites in India. It's a perennial water body, kept full by around 21 seasonal streams that swell during the monsoon, together with freshwater springs and a subterranean network of channels running through the surrounding limestone and dolomite — a karst formation that keeps water reaching the lake even outside the rains. The lake sits at an elevation of about 672 m and eventually feeds into the Giri River, a tributary of the Yamuna.

04 · Life In and Around the Wetland

Wetland Biodiversity

Despite its modest size, faunal surveys have recorded more than 440 species around Renuka, ranging from protozoa to mammals. Nineteen species of fish live in the lake, including Puntius, Labeo, Rasbora, and Channa. Birdlife is equally rich, with over 100 recorded species — 66 of them resident — including the crimson-breasted barbet, myna, bulbul, pheasants, egrets, herons, mallards, and lapwings.

The surrounding sanctuary supports Sambar deer, barking deer (kakar), Himalayan goral, black bear, langur, rhesus macaque, porcupine, and hare, sheltered by sub-tropical forest that includes sal (Shorea robusta), Terminalia tomentosa, sheesham (Dalbergia sissoo), kachnar, tun, and amaltas — with a natural sal forest standing along the lake's northern edge.

05 · Protecting a Small but Vital Site

Conservation

Renuka Wetland is managed by the Himachal Pradesh Forest Department, with the lake and its catchment protected as part of the Renuka Wildlife Sanctuary. Because the wetland itself covers just 20 hectares within a catchment of about 500 hectares, it's especially exposed to erosion and silt washing down from the surrounding slopes. Conservation efforts have focused on community awareness programs, checking silt influx from eroded hillsides, and roughly 50 hectares of plantation across the catchment to stabilize the surrounding terrain and protect water quality for the fish, birds, and pilgrims who all depend on the lake.
