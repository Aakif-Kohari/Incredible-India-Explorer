# Historical Profile: Bina Das — The Student Revolutionary Who Challenged Colonial Rule

## Hero
- **Name:** Bina Das (later Bina Bhaumik)
- **Life Span:** August 24, 1911 – December 26, 1986
- **Affiliation:** Chhatri Sangha / Jugantar Dal
- **Key Event:** Attempted assassination of Bengal Governor Sir Stanley Jackson at the University of Calcutta Convocation (February 6, 1932)
- **Honours:** Padma Shri (1960, for Social Work); Posthumous Degree conferred by the University of Calcutta (2012)
- **Legacy:** A defining figure of student-led militant anti-colonial resistance and women's revolutionary participation in British India.

## Early Life
Bina Das was born on August 24, 1911, in Krishnanagar, Nadia district, Bengal (now West Bengal) into an intellectually progressive, nationalist Brahmo family.
- **Family Background:** Her father, Beni Madhab Das, was a renowned educator and reformer who taught at Ravenshaw College, Cuttack (where he profoundly influenced a young Subhas Chandra Bose). Her mother, Sarala Devi, was an ardent social reformer who ran Punya Ashram, a women's hostel in Calcutta that quietly served as a haven and storage point for anti-colonial activists.
- **Early Influence:** Bina grew up surrounded by patriotic literature and reformist ideals. Her elder sister, Kalyani Das (Bhattacharjee), was also an activist who played a pivotal role in organizing youth organizations.

## Education & Political Awakening
Bina received her early schooling at St. John’s Diocesan Girls’ Higher Secondary School in Calcutta.
- **Chhatri Sangha:** While pursuing her Bachelor of Arts at Diocesan College and Bethune College, she joined Chhatri Sangha (Women Students' Association), an organization founded by her sister Kalyani and associates.
- **Cadre Training:** Chhatri Sangha went beyond study circles, providing women with training in physical conditioning, martial defense, lathi play, and underground political agitation.
- **Underground Links:** Through Chhatri Sangha, Bina connected with the revolutionary group Jugantar, collaborating with activists like Kamala Dasgupta and Suhasini Ganguly.

## Revolutionary Bengal
The early 1930s were characterized by intense confrontation between the colonial government and armed revolutionary groups in Bengal.
- **Repression & Retaliation:** Following the 1930 Chittagong Armoury Raid and the assassination of colonial officials (such as Inspector General Simpson at Writers' Building), the British government unleashed severe emergency decrees, mass arrests, and custodial abuses across the province.
- **Women on the Frontlines:** Women moved from auxiliary roles to frontline direct action. Figures like Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Datta, Santi Ghose, and Suniti Choudhury demonstrated that female students were prepared to strike directly at the high command of the colonial apparatus.

## The 1932 University of Calcutta Incident
On February 6, 1932, the annual Convocation of the University of Calcutta was convened at the Senate Hall. The Chancellor and Governor of Bengal, Sir Stanley Jackson, was addressing the assembly.

`[Kamala Dasgupta / Jugantar] ──> Procures Revolver ──> [Bina Das (In Convocation Gown)] │ (Fires 5 shots at Dais) ▼ [Disarmed & Arrested]`

### The Attack
- **The Weapon:** Bina procured a five-chambered revolver with the assistance of fellow revolutionary Kamala Dasgupta.
- **The Approach:** Concealing the weapon underneath her academic convocation gown, 21-year-old Bina walked down the aisle toward the dais to receive her degree.
- **The Shots:** At close range, she drew the firearm and discharged five rounds toward Governor Jackson. The governor ducked to evade the bullets, and Vice-Chancellor Lt. Col. Hassan Suhrawardy lunged forward to physically restrain and disarm her.
- **The Capture:** Bina was wrestled to the ground and taken into custody. No fatal injuries were sustained by the governor or attendees.

## Trial & Imprisonment
The British administration initiated a high-profile, expedited trial under emergency tribunal rules.
- **The Famous Statement:** In a five-page written confession submitted to the court, Bina eloquently expressed the political motivations behind her actions, stating: "I can assure all that I have no personal feeling against the governor... but as the Governor of Bengal he represents a system which has kept enslaved 300 million men and women of my country... My object was to die, and if to die, to die nobly fighting against this despotic system of Government."
- **Sentencing:** Bina was convicted of attempted murder and sentenced to nine years of rigorous imprisonment.
- **Withheld Degree:** In an act of institutional retribution, the University of Calcutta canceled her academic credentials and withheld her BA Honours degree.

## Later Political Life & Post-Independence
- **Quit India Movement:** Released early from prison in 1939, Bina joined the Indian National Congress and threw herself into mass mobilization. She was arrested again during the Quit India Movement (1942) and imprisoned until 1945.
- **Parliamentary Career:** In 1946, she was elected to the Bengal Provincial Legislative Assembly, and later served in the West Bengal Legislative Assembly from 1947 to 1951.
- **Personal Life & Ideology:** In 1947, she married fellow revolutionary Jatish Chandra Bhaumik. In her later years, she aligned with socialist principles and focused heavily on social work.
- **Memoirs:** She penned significant autobiographical works in Bengali, including Shrinkhal Jhankar (The Clanking of Chains) and Pitridhan.
- **Final Years:** Following the death of her husband, she relocated to Rishikesh, living a simple and reclusive life after turning down government freedom fighter pensions. She passed away in obscurity in December 1986.

## Women in Bengal's Revolutionary Movement
Bina Das belonged to an extraordinary cohort of women in 1930s Bengal who challenged both colonial authority and conservative social expectations:
- **Armed Vanguard:** Alongside Pritilata Waddedar (Pahartali Club raid), Kalpana Datta (Chittagong Armoury), and Santi Ghose & Suniti Choudhury (Comilla District Magistrate assassination), Bina demonstrated tactical autonomy.
- **Organizational Core:** Networks like Chhatri Sangha and Dipali Sangha (founded by Leela Roy) served as training grounds, creating institutional pathways for female leadership across labor, student, and armed resistance wings.
