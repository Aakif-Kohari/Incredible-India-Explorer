# Historical Profile: Kalpana Datta — The Chittagong Revolutionary Who Joined the Armed Resistance

## Hero
- **Name:** Kalpana Datta (later Kalpana Joshi)
- **Life Span:** July 27, 1913 – February 8, 1995
- **Affiliation:** Indian Republican Army (Chittagong Branch) / Communist Party of India (CPI)
- **Key Role:** Armed revolutionary fighter, explosive specialist, and underground organizer.
- **Legacy:** One of the prominent female combatants of the Indian anti-colonial struggle, bridging armed nationalism and grassroots communist labor movements.

## Early Life
Kalpana Datta was born into a progressive middle-class Bengali Baidya family in Sripur village, located within the Chittagong district of undivided Bengal (now Bangladesh).
- **Family Background:** Her father was Binod Behari Datta, a government employee.
- **Early Ideology:** Growing up in a household that valued academic pursuits, she was exposed early on to patriotic literature and regional biographies of 19th-century freedom fighters.

## Political Awakening
Datta’s path to radical politics solidified during her higher education journeys in Calcutta.
- **The Student Movement:** After matriculating in Chittagong in 1929, she moved to Calcutta to study Chemistry at Bethune College. There, she joined the Chhatri Sangha (Women Students’ Association).
- **Radicalization:** Through the association, she met prominent activists like Pritilata Waddedar and Bina Das. Her study of chemistry soon turned practical, as she became fascinated with laboratory techniques that could be applied to processing explosives for the anti-colonial underground.

## Chittagong Revolutionary Movement
By 1930, Chittagong had become an active hotbed for militant nationalism under the visionary leadership of Surya Sen (Masterda).
### The Strategic Landscape
- **The 1930 Armouries Raid:** In April 1930, Surya Sen's group executed a daring raid on the local British armouries, cut off telegraph lines, and declared a provisional revolutionary government.
- **The Crackdown:** The British response was swift and brutal, forcing the top revolutionary leadership into the surrounding deep forests and villages of Chittagong.

## Revolutionary Activities
Kalpana returned to Chittagong to embed herself entirely within Surya Sen's underground network.

### Combat and Logistics Roll
- **The Bomb Maker:** Utilizing her scientific background, Kalpana actively manufactured and transported gun-cotton and liquid explosives to the guerrilla cells hidden across the district.
- **The Disguise Operations:** She frequently traveled disguised as a male peasant or laborer to study British military movements, map security protocols, and deliver funds.
- **The Pahartali Plot:** She was originally chosen to attack the Pahartali European Club alongside Pritilata Waddedar in September 1932. However, she was arrested micro-moments before the strike while scouting the area in male clothing, serving a brief detention.

## Arrest, Trial, and Imprisonment
Following her release from initial detention, she went completely underground to rejoin Surya Sen in evasion.
- **The Gairala Raid:** In February 1933, British military forces tracked down their safehouse in Gairala village. While Surya Sen was captured during the fierce shootout, Kalpana managed to escape through the cordon with fellow fighter Tarakeswar Dastidar.
- **The Capture:** Three months later, in May 1933, British forces cornered her unit. Following a brief exchange of fire, Kalpana was arrested.
- **The Sentence:** In the second supplementary trial of the Chittagong Armoury Raid Case (1933), Surya Sen and Tarakeswar Dastidar were sentenced to death by hanging. Kalpana Datta was sentenced to transportation for life (life imprisonment) at the Second Lahore Conspiracy Trial framework.

## Later Life & Social Work
She spent nearly six years in solitary confinement before being released in 1939 due to persistent public campaigns led by Mahatma Gandhi and Rabindranath Tagore.
- **Ideological Shift:** While in jail, she read extensive Marxist literature. Upon release, she joined the Communist Party of India (CPI).
- **The 1943 Famine Relief:** She married Puran Chand Joshi, the General Secretary of the CPI, in 1943. During the devastating Bengal Famine of 1943, she threw herself into relief operations, organizing medical camps and grain distribution networks.
- **Preserving History:** She transitioned into a writer, penning Chittagong Armoury Raiders: Reminiscences, which serves as a vital primary source document for 20th-century Indian historiography.

## Fact vs. Commemorative Narrative
To maintain strict historical objectivity, researchers separate primary records from later retellings:
- **Documented Fact:** The official court transcripts of the Chittagong Supplementary Trial, her prison intake logs, and her direct literary memoirs provide completely verified accounts of her activities.
- **Commemorative Narratives:** Modern cinematic portrayals and fictionalized biographies frequently amplify her interpersonal dynamics with Surya Sen or present her as a contemporary military commander. Historians note that her real strength lay in her logistical brilliance, explosive assembly, and long-term political resilience rather than romanticized individual duel victories.

## Timeline
- **July 27, 1913 ──>** Born in Sripur village, Chittagong, Bengal
- **1930 CE ──>** Joins Chhatri Sangha while studying Chemistry in Calcutta
- **May 19, 1933 ──>** Captured by British forces after the Gairala armed clash
- **1939 CE ──>** Released from prison; transitions to Marxist grassroots politics
- **1943 CE ──>** Marries P.C. Joshi and leads Bengal Famine relief operations
- **February 8, 1995 ──>** Passes away in New Delhi, leaving an extensive archival record

## Legacy
- **The Written Word:** Her autobiographical accounts remain the definitive primary records used by international scholars studying women's participation in Indian urban guerrilla movements.
- **Cinematic Homage:** Her character was brought to life by actress Deepika Padukone in the film Khelein Hum Jee Jaan Sey (2010) and by Vega Tamotia in the critically acclaimed film Chittagong (2012).
