# Historical Profile: Rani Velu Nachiyar — The Queen Who Resisted British Expansion

## Hero
- **Name:** Rani Velu Nachiyar (Veeramangai / Brave Woman)
- **Life Span:** January 3, 1730 – December 25, 1796
- **Reign / Realm:** Queen Regnant of the Sivaganga Kingdom (1780–1790 CE)
- **Royal Lineage:** Princess of Ramanathapuram (Sethupathi Dynasty); Queen Consort & Ruler of Sivaganga
- **Key Operations:** Anti-colonial military resistance against British East India Company (EIC) & Nawab of Arcot; recapture of Sivaganga (1780)
- **Legacy:** The first Indian queen to wage armed warfare against British colonial forces and successfully reclaim her annexed kingdom; pioneer of early anti-colonial resistance in southern India.

## Sivaganga Kingdom
The Kingdom of Sivaganga (established c. 1730 CE) was a prominent principality in southern Tamil Nadu, carved out from the Greater Ramnad (Ramanathapuram) Sethupathi realm.
- **Geopolitical Significance:** Situated between Madurai, Thanjavur, and Ramanathapuram, Sivaganga controlled vital agricultural belts, pilgrim routes to Rameswaram, and strategic forested fortresses such as Kalaiyarkovil and Sivaganga Palace.
- **Feudal & Military Structure:** The kingdom was defended by martial palayams and led by royal commanders, notably the Marudhu Pandiyar brothers (Periya Marudhu and Chinna Marudhu), who managed regional military logistics, cavalry regiments, and revenue administration.

## Early Life
Velu Nachiyar was born on January 3, 1730, in Ramanathapuram as the only child of King Chellamuthu Vijayaragunatha Sethupathi and Queen Sakandhimuthal of the Ramnad Kingdom.
- **Martial & Scholarly Upbringing:** Raised as a royal heir without gender restrictions, she was formally trained in traditional martial arts, including *Silambam* (quarterstaff combat), *Valari* (boomerang throwing), archery, swordsmanship, and equestrian warfare.
- **Multilingual Diplomacy:** She achieved scholarly fluency in multiple languages—Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Sanskrit, French, and Urdu—a linguistic mastery that later proved decisive in negotiating anti-colonial military pacts.
- **Royal Marriage:** In 1746, at the age of sixteen, she married King Muthu Vaduganatha Periya Udaiyathevar of Sivaganga, becoming the Queen Consort of Sivaganga and bearing a daughter, Princess Vellachi Nachiyar.

## Conflict & Exile
During the 1760s and 1770s, Muhammad Ali Khan Wallajah (the Nawab of Arcot) entered into financial and military alliances with the British East India Company to subjugate the autonomous southern palayams and extract hefty tributes.
- **The Kalaiyarkovil Ambush (1772):** In June 1772, combined forces of the British East India Company under Colonel Joseph Smith and Abraham Collier, operating alongside the Nawab's troops, launched an unprovoked surprise assault on the sacred fort-complex of Kalaiyarkovil.
- **Martyrdom of the King:** King Muthu Vaduganatha was killed in the fierce battle of Kalaiyarkovil, and the British-Arcot alliance annexed the Sivaganga kingdom, looting the palace and imposing punitive administrative controls.
- **The Flight into Exile:** Escaping through dense jungle corridors with her young daughter Vellachi and loyal commanders, Velu Nachiyar took refuge in Virupakshi near Dindigul under the protection of Palayakarar Gopala Nayaker.

## Building Resistance
During her eight-year exile at Virupakshi (1772–1780), Velu Nachiyar transformed a displaced royal court into a sophisticated military high command.
- **Strategic Alliance with Hyder Ali:** Utilizing her fluency in Urdu, Velu Nachiyar met and corresponded directly with Sultan Hyder Ali of Mysore at Dindigul. Impressed by her strategic clarity and fortitude, Hyder Ali provided 5,000 infantry, 5,000 cavalry troops, and advanced artillery armaments stationed under his Dindigul commander, Syed Karki.
- **The Southern Coalition:** She formed a coordinated anti-British league comprising Gopala Nayaker of Virupakshi, the Marudhu brothers, and regional chieftains from Tirunelveli and Ramanathapuram.
- **Udaiyaal & Kuyili Battalions:** She raised and commanded dedicated military regiments, including *Udaiyaal Padai*—an elite all-women combat wing named in honor of Udaiyaal, a local pastoral woman who was executed by British forces for refusing to betray the Queen's hideout. The wing was commanded by her close general, Kuyili.

## Return to Sivaganga & Anti-Company Campaign
In 1780, with full logistical and military preparations complete, Velu Nachiyar launched an offensive to liberate the kingdom from British and Arcot garrisons.
- **Cavalry Advances:** Her allied forces marched across Madurai and Dindigul, securing outer outposts and severing British supply routes between Madras and Tiruchirappalli.
- **The Assault on Sivaganga Fort:** On the auspicious occasion of Vijayadashami in October 1780, local women gained access to the inner temple of Rajarajeswari within the fort complex for ceremonial worship.
- **Neutralization of the British Arsenal:** In popular historical tradition, Commander Kuyili and her elite combatants infiltrated the ammunition storage depot inside the fort, igniting the gunpowder reserves in a coordinated suicide raid that destroyed the Company's military advantage.
- **Liberation & Coronation:** Following the destruction of the arsenal, the Sivaganga cavalry and infantry stormed the fortifications, defeating British forces under Captain Maclean and the Nawab's garrison. Rani Velu Nachiyar entered Sivaganga in triumph and was crowned Queen Regnant in 1780.

## Governance, Succession & Later Life
- **Administrative Stabilization:** After reclaiming the throne, Rani Velu Nachiyar appointed Chinna Marudhu as her Chief Minister (Diwan) and Periya Marudhu as Commander-in-Chief (*Senapati*), stabilizing the agrarian economy, rebuilding temples, and maintaining defensive vigilance against colonial counter-offensives.
- **Treaty of Alliance & Succession:** In 1789–1790, she formally transferred administrative and sovereign powers to her daughter Princess Vellachi Nachiyar and the Marudhu brothers.
- **Demise:** Having secured the independence of her kingdom for over a decade, Rani Velu Nachiyar passed away peacefully on December 25, 1796, in Sivaganga.

## Historical Evidence vs. Later Narratives
To preserve historiographical integrity, modern researchers distinguish primary colonial/dynastic records from later folklore:
- **Documented Archival Fact:** British East India Company military dispatches (Madras Military Consultations 1772–1781), correspondence between the Nawab of Arcot and Governor of Fort St. George, and Mysore administrative archives definitively verify Velu Nachiyar's exile, her diplomatic negotiations in Urdu with Hyder Ali, the military deployment from Dindigul, the recapture of Sivaganga in 1780, and her recognized status as ruler.
- **Oral Tradition & Commemorative Narratives:** Details regarding specific clandestine temple disguises and the dramatic individual self-immolation of Kuyili at the ammunition depot derive primarily from regional oral ballads (*Ammanai* folk poetry), temple records, and 19th-century regional Tamil histories. While treated by historians as culturally vital collective memories, they illustrate the deep grassroots participation of women in early anti-colonial resistance.

## Legacy & National Memory
- **First Woman Anti-Colonial Monarch:** Revered in Tamil history as *Veeramangai* (Brave Woman), she predates Rani Chennamma of Kittur (1824) and Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi (1857) by decades as an armed female ruler challenging colonial expansion.
- **Postal & State Honors:** On December 31, 2008, the Government of India issued a commemorative postage stamp honoring her courage. The Government of Tamil Nadu erected a grand memorial and statue in Sivaganga.
- **Cultural Iconography:** Her life is commemorated in classical dance-dramas, university curricula, and military history studies as an exemplar of multi-faith alliances, linguistic diplomacy, and female military leadership.
