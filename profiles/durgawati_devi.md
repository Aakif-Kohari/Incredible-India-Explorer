# Historical Profile: Durgawati Devi — Durga Bhabhi and the Revolutionary Underground

## Hero
- **Name:** Durgawati Devi (popularly revered as Durga Bhabhi)
- **Life Span:** October 7, 1907 – October 15, 1999
- **Affiliation:** Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) / Naujawan Bharat Sabha
- **Key Operations:** Orchestrated Bhagat Singh’s escape from Lahore (1928); led an armed retaliatory attack on British official Sergeant Taylor in Bombay (1930)
- **Legacy:** The operational backbone, courier coordinator, and arms custodian of the northern Indian revolutionary underground.

## Early Life
Durgawati Devi was born on October 7, 1907, into a traditional Gujarati Brahmin family settled in Shahjahanpur, Uttar Pradesh.
- **Family Hardships:** She lost her mother at a very early age and was raised primarily by her father, who was an official in the collectorate.
- **Early Marriage:** In accordance with prevailing social customs, she was married at the age of eleven to Bhagwati Charan Vohra, a brilliant youth from an affluent family in Lahore. This union dramatically redirected the course of her life toward the anti-colonial struggle.

## Revolutionary Network & Bhagwati Charan Vohra
Lahore in the 1920s was the intellectual and political crucible of radical youth politics in northern India.
- **The Dynamic Duo:** Bhagwati Charan Vohra emerged as a premier ideologue, organizer, and propaganda strategist for the underground movement. Alongside Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev Thapar, he co-founded the Naujawan Bharat Sabha in 1926.
- **The Title of Respect:** Durgawati actively worked alongside her husband, managing safe houses and maintaining hidden weapon caches. Because she was the wife of their senior comrade, iconic revolutionaries like Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Sukhdev affectionately addressed her as Durga Bhabhi (Sister-in-law). The moniker eventually became her legendary code name within British intelligence logs.

## The Escape of Bhagat Singh (December 1928)
Following the assassination of British police official John Saunders in Lahore on December 17, 1928 (in retaliation for the death of Lala Lajpat Rai), the British administration placed Lahore under absolute military lockdown.

`[Lahore Shootout] ──> Absolute Cordon ──> [Bhagat Singh (Disguised as British Officer)] │ (Durga Bhabhi as Wife) ▼ [First-Class Train to Calcutta]`

### The Infiltration Strategy
- **The Disguise:** Bhagat Singh shaved his beard and cut his hair to pass as a high-ranking, Westernized gentleman.
- **The Cover Identity:** Durgawati risked immediate arrest and execution by executing a brilliant psychological ruse. She posed as his elite, aristocratic wife, traveling with her infant son, Shachi.
- **Slipping the Cordon:** Escorted by fellow revolutionary Rajguru (who posed as their family domestic servant), they walked past hundreds of colonial policemen stationed at the Lahore Railway Station.
- **The Departure:** They boarded a first-class carriage on a train bound for Calcutta. This legendary escape successfully broke the British dragnet and allowed the HSRA high command to regroup.

## Revolutionary Activities & Bomb-Making
Durgawati's role was highly tactical, evolving from disguise operations to direct logistical and armed combat assignments.
- **The Bomb Factory:** She funded and managed the clandestine ammunition and chemical labs set up by the HSRA in Lahore and Saharanpur.
- **The Ultimate Sacrifice:** In May 1930, her husband Bhagwati Charan Vohra died tragically on the banks of the Ravi River while testing a highly advanced, newly fabricated picric acid bomb intended to rescue Bhagat Singh from prison.
- **The Bombay Retaliation:** Despite being shattered by her husband’s sudden death, Durgawati remained active. On October 8, 1930, she led an armed cell in Bombay, opening fire on a car carrying British official Sergeant Taylor and his wife in broad daylight. She was arrested shortly after and served multiple terms of rigorous imprisonment.

## Fact vs. Popular Retelling
To maintain standard historical objectivity, researchers separate primary records from folklore variations:
- **Documented Fact:** British intelligence files, the official Lahore Conspiracy Case trial testimonies, and the memoirs of fellow HSRA members (such as Yashpal and Shiv Verma) fully verify her role in the train escape, the funding of weapon nodes, and her subsequent arrests.
- **Popular Retellings:** Later cinematic pieces and folklore tales frequently depict her as a combat instructor who routinely participated in gunfights alongside Chandrashekhar Azad. Historians emphasize that her primary value lay in her sophisticated logistical network, intelligence handling, and psychological boldness, rather than continuous front-line firefights.

## Later Life & Educational Work
Following her release from prison and the ultimate dissolution of the HSRA network, Durgawati transitioned into grassroots social work.
- **The Montessori Pioneer:** In 1940, she shifted her focus to public development and children's welfare. She established Lucknow's first Montessori school, the Lucknow Montessori Inter College.
- **Rejection of Privilege:** Post-independence, she intentionally avoided political power structures and turned down prominent government posts, choosing instead to live quietly as an educator. She passed away at the age of 92 on October 15, 1999, in Ghaziabad.
