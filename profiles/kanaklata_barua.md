# Historical Profile: Kanaklata Barua — The Young Freedom Fighter of Assam

## Hero
- **Name:** Kanaklata Barua (also known as Birbala)
- **Life Span:** December 22, 1924 – September 20, 1942
- **Age at Martyrdom:** 17 years old
- **Movement:** Quit India Movement (1942)
- **Legacy:** One of India’s youngest female martyrs; a premier icon of sacrifice and youth mobilization in Northeast India's freedom struggle.

## Early Life
Kanaklata Barua was born in the remote village of Borangabari in the undivided Darrang district (now Biswanath district) of Assam.
- **Ancestry:** She belonged to an impoverished, traditional Assamese family of Chutia lineage. Her parents were Krishna Kanta Barua and Karneshwari Barua.
- **Early Hardships:** She lost her mother at the age of five and her father when she was thirteen. She dropped out of school after class three to care for her younger siblings, which fostered early resilience and maturity.

## Assam in 1942
By August 1942, Mahatma Gandhi's "Do or Die" call for the Quit India Movement had swept across the Brahmaputra Valley, triggering intense political mobilization.
- **Colonial Repression:** The British administration imposed brutal martial-style restrictions under the Defence of India Rules, arresting senior leadership across Assam.
- **Mass Awakening:** In response to the leadership vacuum, local communities formed decentralized revolutionary committees. Women and students became the vanguard of the resistance, defying bans on public assemblies.

## Mrityu Bahini
To execute direct, non-violent political actions, Assamese revolutionary leader Jyoti Prasad Agarwala organized two parallel wings of volunteers: the Shanti Bahini (Peace Squad) and the Mrityu Bahini (Death Squad).
- **The Oath:** Members of the Mrityu Bahini took a solemn oath to lay down their lives for the cause of complete independence (Purna Swaraj).
- **Inclusion of Youth:** Kanaklata eagerly sought to join the Mrityu Bahini. Despite initial hesitation from coordinators due to her age, her fierce determination secured her a spot in the local cadre alongside another youth, Mukunda Kakati.

## Gohpur Procession
The regional revolutionary command selected September 20, 1942, as a day of direct action to hoist the Indian National Flag atop British-controlled local police stations—symbols of colonial subjugation.

`[Borangabari Volunteers] ──> Barefoot March ──> [Gohpur Police Station] │ (Rebuke to Police) ▼ [British Gunfire]`

### The Fateful Stand
- **The March:** Kanaklata led a procession of unarmed, barefoot volunteers from Borangabari toward the Gohpur Police Station, holding the tricolour aloft.
- **The Confrontation:** Upon reaching the police station, they were blocked by British-led police forces armed with rifles under the command of Officer-in-Charge Rebati Mahan Das.
- **The Final Defiance:** Warned that they would be shot if they advanced, Kanaklata refused to step back. Historical accounts record her declaring that while her body could be shot, the spirit of the flag and freedom could not be suppressed.
- **The Firing:** As she stepped forward, the police opened fire at point-blank range. A bullet struck her chest, killing her instantly. As she fell, she held the tricolour upright until fellow volunteer Mukunda Kakati took it from her. Kakati was also shot dead moments later.

## Fact vs. Commemorative Narrative
To preserve objective historiography, researchers distinguish between documented accounts and later cultural expansions:
- **Documented Fact:** The archival police reports, local judicial inquiries, and testimonies of contemporary eyewitnesses (such as fellow marcher Pushpalata Das) explicitly confirm the date, the location, the police firing, and her immediate death while leading the march.
- **Commemorative Narratives:** Over the decades, popular folk songs, regional plays, and visual media have romanticized her specific dialogues with the police officer. While culturally vital, these exact exchanges are treated by modern historians as symbolic representations of her unyielding defiance rather than exact transcripts.

## Timeline
- **December 22, 1924 ──>** Born in Borangabari village, Assam
- **Aug–Sep 1942 ──>** Enrolls in the revolutionary 'Mrityu Bahini' (Death Squad)
- **September 20, 1942 ──>** Shot dead by British police during the Gohpur march (Age 17)
- **1997 CE ──>** Indian Navy commissions the Fast Patrol Vessel ICGS Kanaklata Barua
- **2020 CE ──>** Indian Coast Guard commissions a new, advanced patrol vessel of the same name

## Memorials & Legacy
- **Defense Honors:** The Indian Coast Guard commissioned a Fast Patrol Vessel named ICGS Kanaklata Barua in 1997, and a replacement advanced vessel of the same name was commissioned in 2020.
- **Public Monuments:** Life-sized bronze statues depicting her holding the national flag stand prominently in front of the historic Gohpur Police Station and inside the Kanaklata Barua Rock Garden in Tezpur.
- **Institutional Names:** The Government of Assam established the Kanaklata Barua State University to honor her contribution to public empowerment through education.
