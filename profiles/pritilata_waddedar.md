# Historical Profile: Pritilata Waddedar — The Revolutionary of Chittagong

## Hero
- **Name:** Pritilata Waddedar
- **Life Span:** May 5, 1911 – September 24, 1932
- **Age at Martyrdom:** 21 years old
- **Affiliation:** Jugantar / Indian Republican Army (Chittagong Branch)
- **Legacy:** The first woman to lead an armed anti-colonial operation against British rule; a foundational icon of revolutionary courage across India and Bangladesh.

## Early Life
Pritilata Waddedar was born in a middle-class Vaidya family in Dhalghat village, located within the Chittagong district of undivided Bengal (now Bangladesh).
- **Family Roots:** Her parents were Jagabandhu Waddedar, a municipal clerk, and Pratibhamayi Waddedar. The family originally held the surname Dasgupta.
- **Early Exposure:** Growing up in a politically charged Bengal, she was deeply inspired by regional accounts of resistance against the 1905 Partition of Bengal.

## Education
Pritilata was a brilliant student whose intellectual background laid the foundation for her radical political consciousness.
- **Schooling:** She studied at Dr. Khastagir Government Girls' School in Chittagong, graduating with a first-class metric record in 1928.
- **Higher Studies:** She pursued intermediate arts at Eden College in Dhaka, topping the Dhaka Board examinations. She later earned a degree in Philosophy from Bethune College in Calcutta.
- **Leadership Roles:** Returning to Chittagong with her degree, she took a position as the headmistress of Nandankanan Aparnacharan Girls' School, using her role to quietly promote self-defense and nationalist literature among young girls.

## Chittagong Revolutionary Movement
The early 1930s marked the peak of armed revolutionary nationalism in Bengal, moving away from slow constitutional agitations toward localized guerrilla warfare.
- **The Armed Network:** This underground movement sought to disrupt colonial infrastructure by targeting communications, armouries, and transport lines.
- **The Political Sandbox:** The regional network operated as a highly disciplined paramilitary structure, requiring absolute secrecy, rigorous physical training, and ideological commitment to complete independence from the British Empire.

## Association With Surya Sen
Pritilata was introduced to the legendary revolutionary leader Surya Sen (affectionately known as Masterda) through her university networks.
- **Breaking Gender Barriers:** Traditionally, women in underground networks were restricted to providing shelter, moving funds, or delivering messages. Surya Sen recognized Pritilata's resolve and accepted her as a full combatant in the Indian Republican Army's Chittagong branch.
- **Guerrilla Integration:** Alongside fellow revolutionary Kalpana Datta, Pritilata underwent intense military drilling, firearms training, and tactical planning. She survived multiple close encounters with British forces, including the armed confrontation during the Dhalghat leasehouse raid in June 1932.

## Pahartali Operation
The target selected for direct tactical action was the Pahartali European Club, an exclusive social hub for colonial officials notorious for its discriminatory signboards reading, "Whites Only" or "Dogs and Indians not allowed."

`[Guerrilla Strike Team] ──> Punjabi Men's Disguise ──> [Pahartali European Club] │ (Explosives & Firefight) ▼ [Cyanide Self-Sacrifice]`

### The Fateful Stand
- **The Leadership Assignment:** Surya Sen designated Pritilata to lead an elite 10-member guerrilla unit tasked with striking the club on September 24, 1932.
- **The Disguise:** To bypass the heavy outer military cordons around Pahartali, Pritilata disguised herself as a Punjabi male worker, while her teammates wore traditional dhotis and lungis.
- **The Assault:** At approximately 10:45 PM, Pritilata’s team surrounded the building, smashing windows and detonating crude gunpowder charges before opening fire on the inner gathering. The surprise attack caused mass panic among the British officials inside.
- **The Counter-Fire:** As the unit began its organized retreat, colonial sentries recovered and returned heavy fire. A stray bullet hit Pritilata's calf, severely wounding her and slowing her escape.
- **The Final Choice:** Trapped and determined to prevent the British military from torturing her for information regarding Surya Sen's hideouts, she swallowed a lethal dose of potassium cyanide. Her body was discovered by police the following morning.

## Historical Evidence vs. Commemorative Narrative
To preserve historiographical accuracy, researchers maintain a clear line between primary evidence and oral tradition:
- **Documented Fact:** The post-mortem toxicology reports, official British police logs, and the physical suicide note discovered in her pocket—stating that women could fight for freedom just as fiercely as men—are fully verified archival records.
- **Commemorative Expansion:** Later dramatic plays, novels, and films have frequently added romanticized inner monologues or heavily fictionalized her final dialogue with her squad. Historians view these additions as cultural tools that capture her revolutionary impact rather than literal, verbatim history.

- **September 24, 1932 ──>** Leads the assault on Pahartali European Club and consumes cyanide (Age 21)
- **2012 CE ──>** A bronze bust of Pritilata is unveiled in front of the historic club site
- **2023 CE ──>** Government of Bangladesh backs the production of 'Bhalobasha Pritilata' biopic

## Legacy
- **In Bangladesh:** Pritilata is celebrated across Bangladesh as a premier national heroine. A prominent student residence hall at Jahangirnagar University is named Pritilata Hall, and her bronze bust stands permanently outside the historic club building in Chittagong.
- **In India:** Her life is integrated directly into school textbooks across West Bengal and India, immortalizing her statement on gender equality in revolutionary struggles.
- **In Cinema:** Her heroic stand has been depicted in major historical films, including Khelein Hum Jee Jaan Sey (2010) and Chittagong (2012).
